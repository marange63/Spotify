Cautious Optimism Briefings

Capital Markets Cross-Asset Radar

Transcript · August 31, 2026

Good morning, it's Monday, August 31st, and this is Capital Markets Radar.

Backdrop first, briefly, because we led on it Saturday and it is scenery today rather than news. Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole speech on Friday the 28th pushed September hike odds from roughly 35 percent to somewhere near 60. That range moves with every CME snapshot, so hold it loosely. The ten-year yield went to about 4.72 percent on secondary aggregation. Payrolls land September 4th and the FOMC decides September 16th, and neither has happened. So treat the repricing as the setup, not the outcome. What matters this morning is what did, and didn't, move in response to it.

Start with private credit, because it is the cleanest test, and because this program set the test on the record. On Saturday we said that if bond volatility woke up, the widening would reach high yield first and private credit's funding benchmarks and business development company cost of capital second, not first. Three days on, the honest answer is that it has not reached them at all. It got easier.

Blue Owl Technology Finance Corp priced 400 million dollars of bonds, double the 200 million it originally sought. Blackstone's BCRED raised 750 million against a roughly 500 million dollar target. And a separate Blue Owl Capital note sale drew as much as 3.3 billion dollars of investor orders. Those were the first real test of BDC debt appetite since the quarter began, and they cleared at multiples of target right as rate expectations were repricing higher. The pricing dates were reported only as "last week," not pinned to a calendar day, so don't over-fit the timing.

Read that narrowly. Two issuers from two sponsors into a technically starved calendar is a thin sample, and it may be nothing more than a catch-up trade after a summer issuance lull. What it tells you is that sponsors can still fund their own balance sheets cheaply. What it tells you nothing about is the marks on the assets those balance sheets hold. Oversubscription prices the liability side of a levered vehicle. That is all it prices.

So turn to the asset side. The spread advantage direct lenders once earned over syndicated leveraged loans has collapsed, from more than 300 basis points on 2017 and 2018 vintage deals to under 100 basis points by the first quarter of this year. That is PIMCO's vintage comparison rather than a live spread print, so treat it as a trend measurement, not this morning's quote. The implication is blunt either way. New capital deployed into direct lending today gives up quarterly liquidity and mark-to-market pricing for barely more yield than a tradable loan pays. The illiquidity premium has been competed away, and the money keeps arriving anyway.

Underneath that, payment-in-kind usage across BDC loan books has climbed to nearly 10 percent of loans, up from about 6 percent since early 2022. Borrowers are increasingly paying their lenders in more debt rather than in cash. That compounds effective leverage quietly, inside reported net asset value, before it ever shows up as a missed payment. And it does exactly that in the state of the world the market started pricing on Friday, which is floating base rates held higher for longer.

Here is the update, and it is the piece that was missing. That deterioration is no longer only a leading indicator. The median non-accrual ratio across the twenty largest BDCs climbed to 2.8 percent in the second quarter, up from 2.0 percent at the end of the first. Direct-lending data compiled by Houlihan Lokey put the same ratio near 1.3 to 1.4 percent at the end of 2023, a level treated as a healthy baseline for the asset class. So realized non-accruals are now running roughly double that baseline, and they made most of that move in a single quarter.

Two caveats on that figure, because it is load-bearing. It is one aggregator's tally across twenty issuers, not cross-checked against a second tracker. And the underlying filings vary meaningfully depending on whether they mark at fair value or at amortized cost, so read it as a sector direction rather than a number comparable across every name.

The so-what is negative operating leverage across the whole structure. Funding costs are falling toward the risk-free rate at the exact moment the income quality of the assets those liabilities fund is measurably worse. The bond investors oversubscribing these deals are pricing the sponsor. They are not pricing the loan book. Watch whether BDC bond books keep clearing this easily once the non-accrual trend has another quarter to run.

Now from capital that didn't flinch to capital that did. On Saturday we said the price move in bitcoin was not the tell, and that the tell was whether the spot ETF creation streak broke, because those flows are the most reversible capital in the trade. It broke. US spot bitcoin ETFs shed 201.9 million dollars in net outflows on Friday, ending a nine-day run and trimming cumulative net inflows to about 55.1 billion. Bitcoin itself pulled back from a three-month high near 81,455 dollars toward the high 70,000s.

Score that test carefully, though, because it resolves smaller and stranger than the setup implied. Two hundred million against a 55 billion dollar cumulative base is two-tenths of one percent. That is a single session of redemptions, not an exodus. And the flow data does not itself attribute the outflow to the speech. That link is our inference. A Friday after a nine-day run, with profit-taking off a three-month high, explains it about as well.

The stranger part is the other leg. Spot Ethereum ETFs kept taking in fresh cash the same day. That is rotation inside crypto, not a flight from it, and it undercuts the blanket rates-proxy call this program has been carrying since Tuesday. Allocators appear to be pricing bitcoin's macro-hedge narrative and Ethereum's settlement-and-collateral narrative as two separate trades rather than as one asset class.

Part of what underwrites that separation is structural rather than sentimental. Tokenized US Treasury products just crossed a record 16.2 billion dollars in aggregate market cap, up 77 percent year to date, on single-tracker data. That is a rounding error against the bill market, and the level is not the point. The growth rate and the function are. It is a collateral base that lets Ethereum-based strategies post margin, borrow, and earn yield on-chain, largely independent of what the dollar or real yields are doing on the day. That gives Ethereum flows a collateral-driven bid bitcoin does not have, which is a cleaner explanation for the divergence than sentiment.

Last stop, commodities, where the same repricing produced another split. Crude fell for the week, with markets reading new Iran sanctions as economic pressure rather than as a threat to physical supply. We are not going to hand you a weekly settlement price, because the level in today's research does not reconcile with the sub-87 dollar Brent this program cited on Friday. Direction is what stands.

The number that does stand is the one this beat asked for on Thursday, when we argued the crude selloff was being priced on positioning rather than on physics. Goldman Sachs estimates Persian Gulf oil exports have recovered to around 15 to 16 million barrels a day, against a pre-conflict run rate of 22 to 24 million. So the geopolitical premium is coming out of crude while roughly a third of pre-conflict export capacity is still missing. The market is pricing a supply recovery it has only partly received, and the reversal risk from a single transit disruption isn't being paid for. The fair alternative is that those missing barrels were never going to clear anyway, because demand-side caution is doing real work here too.

Copper and uranium sit on a different clock entirely, and shouldn't be forced into the same explanation. Copper touched a fresh record near 6.77 dollars a pound on Comex in recent August trading before easing back, and uranium has firmed through the month on tightening long-term utility contracting. That is an electrification and grid-buildout bid, structurally separate from crude's geopolitical unwind.

Put it together. Three days after the market repriced the Fed hawkish, the rate path is not the binding constraint on much of anything yet. Private credit is funding itself more cheaply than before, crypto flows rotated rather than fled, and the oil premium deflated on barrels that have only partly come back. What is actually moving is the quality of what is being financed at spreads that haven't budged. Into the September 4th payrolls print, watch three things: whether BDC issuance keeps clearing at these multiples of target, whether Ethereum keeps outpacing bitcoin on flows, and whether crude snaps back on any single credible transit disruption.

That's Capital Markets Radar for August 31st. Trade safe, and we'll see you next time.
