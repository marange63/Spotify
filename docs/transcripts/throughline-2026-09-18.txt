Good morning, this is The Throughline for Friday, September 18th, 2026. Today's headline: an unconfirmed pipeline-repair leak did more to reprice US equities Thursday than the Federal Reserve's own hawkish hike did, and the dollar's move never reversed at all.

Start with the macro backdrop, and with Capital Markets Cross-Asset Radar, because everything else on today's tape traces back to it. The Fed's quarter-point hike Wednesday took the target range to three and three-quarters to four percent, and Brent broke above a hundred dollars on a shut Strait of Hormuz and a struck Saudi pipeline. By Thursday's close the S&P had round-tripped the entire hike-day loss, on an anonymous Bloomberg report of a repair timeline that Aramco itself has not confirmed. The dollar, though, did not give its move back, which points the funding stress at emerging markets and levered books rather than at the headline index.

Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle found its growth in the one place none of that stress data reaches. KKR disclosed that it has structured or syndicated more than eighty billion dollars of private investment-grade financing so far in 2026, already double everything it originated in all of 2025, for blue-chip borrowers like Samsung Electronics and Enbridge. Nobody has disclosed what spread that paper pays against the public bond market, even as non-accrual balances at the largest business development companies keep climbing at the bottom of the stack.

Private Equity and the Deal Economy has the same missing disclosure, one layer further down the capital stack. Bloomberg reports Blackstone is in conversations to arrange a secondary sale for investors in an eleven-billion-dollar real estate fund of its own. Normally that trade happens quietly between two limited partners; here the manager is running it. No price, and no discount to net asset value, has been disclosed. That number is what decides whether this is genuine liquidity or a quiet markdown — and by PitchBook's read of fifteen large evergreen funds, retail holders earned roughly half what global public equities returned last year, buying in at exactly the manager-reported marks professional allocators are now routing around.

Now into the AI and compute stack, and Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure, where that same scarcity logic shows up as a price tag. Trade press puts Samsung's thirty-two-gigabyte DDR5 module at two hundred thirty-nine dollars, up from a hundred forty-nine since September — a sixty percent move in a commodity part inside a matter of weeks. Micron is reported to be roughly doubling its high-bandwidth memory output by the end of 2026, which is the first dated supply response to a shortage the market is still pricing as permanent. The bidding behind that squeeze is Broadcom, now roughly eighty percent AI silicon by semiconductor revenue, which makes it upstream cause rather than a separate story.

That pattern of undocumented claims outrunning verified ones runs straight through Frontier AI Lab Competition. Grok 4.7 is past its own target date with no model card, no API identifier and no price, while Tesla quietly shipped Grok's voice assistant into car cabins on a dated, versioned software release — distribution substituting for documentation. Meanwhile OpenAI, Google and Anthropic have converged on one structure, a cheap public model plus a separately gated, vetted-access variant, with Anthropic cutting cached-input token pricing seventy-five percent on the commodity tier.

AI Products and Applications shows the same gap between launch and measurement. Anthropic merged its Cowork workspace back into ordinary Claude chat and launched Claude Docs and Slides in beta, and OpenAI put a free ChatGPT add-in inside Microsoft Word the very next day. Add GitHub's Project HydraFusion, which picks the model for the developer rather than letting the developer pick, and across all of it not one vendor published a price, a customer count or a measured quality figure.

Enterprise Technology produced the hardest dollar figure the sector has managed in weeks. Oracle's own SEC filing supports a two-point-eight-billion-dollar restructuring bill set against twenty-eight-and-a-half billion dollars of capital spending in a single quarter, funding AI infrastructure partly by cutting legacy headcount. Salesforce answered with thirty thousand live Agentforce customers and no seat count or revenue attached. The week's only real price signal came from ServiceNow paying a reported nine figures, never disclosed, for the agent-governance startup Sweep. So the sector's biggest verified number this quarter is money leaving, not coming in.

Out at the applied frontier, Robotics and Physical AI finally produced a controlled number instead of a demo. Figure's Helix 2.5 hit fifty-six percent task success in thirty previously unseen homes, against nine percent for an otherwise identical policy trained from scratch — a genuine ablation, with any run needing a human safety intervention scored as an outright failure. Figure still discloses no trial count behind that percentage and no fielded reliability number, a gap that briefing has now flagged eight sessions running.

Space and the Orbital Economy moved this week's decisive action from launch pads to balance sheets. Rocket Lab fully funded the cash portion of its roughly eight-billion-dollar Iridium acquisition with a one-point-nine-four-billion-dollar equity offering, which on that briefing's own arithmetic prices the deal near seventeen times run-rate EBITDA for a business whose EBITDA actually shrank. Planet Labs, by contrast, posted its first adjusted-profitable quarter on fifty-eight percent revenue growth, while Starship's Flight 14 slipped again — the sixth Space briefing in a row to carry a Starship date that then moved.

One thread is worth naming, because it runs through all nine briefings today rather than two or three. In frontier AI, in semiconductors, in AI products, in enterprise tech, in robotics, in space, and in both credit shows, the number that would actually settle the argument — a price, a spread, a trial count, a discount to net asset value — is the one number nobody has published. The market spent Thursday reacting to an anonymous leak instead of a confirmed fact, and today that is not the exception. It is the rule everywhere else too.
