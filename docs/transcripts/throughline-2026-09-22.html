Good morning, this is The Throughline for Tuesday, September 22nd, 2026. Today's headline: a major public pension turned down a private equity fund purely because it carried too much AI exposure, in the same week Oracle sold twenty billion dollars of its own stock to fund a buildout it had told investors customers were paying for.

Start with the macro backdrop. Strategic Power has the long-term uranium price at a nominal record, ninety-six dollars a pound, and the world's largest producer is refusing to chase it. Kazatomprom has cut its planned 2026 capacity rather than expand into the price. That is a supply signal rather than a demand one, and it argues the price holds.

Digital Money picks up the same gap between a headline and its mechanism. Bitcoin printed an eight-month high on Monday, but roughly seven of every eight dollars of liquidations came from shorts, so forced covering did the work rather than fresh demand. Spot ETF flows were essentially flat in the window before the move, and the stablecoin base underneath all of it has not grown in three months, which makes this the most fragile price of the cycle rather than a confirmation of one.

That fragility under a headline number is what Private Equity and the Deal Economy found on the allocator side. Now into the AI and compute stack, where the question all day is what has been built versus what has been announced. Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure finally has a number for the packaging bottleneck, because supply-chain checks put TSMC's advanced-packaging capacity roughly doubling by the end of 2028, well before Monday's groundbreaking in Kaohsiung opens a site that does not run until late 2029. That puts this cycle's margin with the memory makers. The accelerator vendors are the ones paying for it.
