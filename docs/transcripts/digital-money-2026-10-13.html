Digital Money, Crypto, and Market Structure

Transcript · October 13, 2026

Good morning, it's Tuesday, October 13th, and this is your digital money briefing.

Here's the thesis up front. Bitcoin just took its second long squeeze in three days, and it fell last week while gold rose. On that evidence, the market is pricing bitcoin as levered risk beta, not as digital gold.

Start with Monday. Bitcoin fell two point eight percent over twenty-four hours to eighty-one thousand three hundred eighty-two dollars, as of nine-twenty p.m. Eastern on October 12th, according to The Block. CoinGlass counted seven hundred seventy-one million dollars in liquidations over that window. Six hundred eighty-five point six million of it was long positions getting flushed out. That's one aggregator's count, not an audited market-wide total, and incomplete Binance reporting likely means it understates the real figure. It also lands on top of last week's six hundred eighty-one point one million dollar spot bitcoin ETF outflow, the worst week since late June.

Here's the part that matters more than the number. On Saturday, we looked at the one point zero nine billion dollar liquidation flush in the twenty-four hours to October 9th. It was roughly ninety-six percent longs. We said a flush like that clears out the forced sellers. And we said the next drawdown would be supply-driven, not liquidation-driven. Monday proved that call premature. Another six hundred eighty-five million in long liquidations, three days later, means the leveraged book was deeper than that first flush revealed.

There are two ways to read that, and the evidence in hand can't settle which. Reading one: leverage rebuilt fast, within roughly three days. Traders bought the dip with borrowed money almost immediately. Reading two: Friday's flush simply never reached positions with lower liquidation prices. Monday then hit the next tier down in the same book. Rebuilt leverage implies reflexive, fast-money dip-buying, and it would mean the next flush can come just as quickly. An unreached tier implies the original book was always bigger than it looked. That would mean Saturday's read was wrong about size, not about mechanism. We don't have a verified open-interest or funding reading covering October 9th through 12th, so we can't choose between them. The honest lesson is simpler: one flush does not tell you the size of the book.

So what pushed bitcoin down in the first place? The obvious suspect is rates, and last week finally gives us a cross-asset test, not just a theory. Last week, the ten-year Treasury yield climbed to a multi-decade high. That framing comes from a Babypips market analysis, a secondary source, and we don't have the yield level from a primary print. Over that same week, gold rose about one point three percent. Bitcoin fell about four point five percent. That's a genuine divergence from the digital gold pitch that has underpinned much of this year's institutional case.

This is the first direct cross-asset evidence for a rates trigger. But it's one week from one analysis, not a measured correlation regime. The counter-reading deserves equal weight: positioning alone explains the move. Leverage was already crowded going into the week, and Monday's flush was long-heavy regardless of what rates did. Coincidence in timing is not causation.

The strongest bullish rebuttal follows from that. If Monday was leverage clearing rather than allocators exiting, then bitcoin's risk beta last week tells you who was holding it, not what it is. In other words, it was levered traders, not the asset itself, that produced the correlation. Still, on last week's evidence, bitcoin traded like a risk asset into a rate shock, not like a hedge against one.

That has a concrete consequence for allocators. If bitcoin keeps moving with risk assets during a rates shock, the hedge-based case for sizing an ETF allocation weakens. Bitcoin then competes for the risk budget, not the hedge budget, and that's a much more crowded fight. September CPI and the late-October Fed decision haven't happened yet. So treat this as positioning ahead of those catalysts, not a verdict.

If the price action says risk asset, the institutional story has to live somewhere else right now. It lives in the plumbing, and that's where Coinbase comes in. Coinbase's institutional head, Liz Martin, told Bloomberg the firm's prime brokerage business is, in her words, "probably one of the fastest growing areas of our business." She also argued there's no tension in serving banks as infrastructure while competing with them. Both, she said, are "fueled by the same thing, which is institutional adoption of crypto and a desire to put more financial services on chain." That's management's framing, with no revenue figure attached to test it.

Three concrete pieces sit behind that framing. The first is Samsung Wallet. In Coinbase's own words, when a Samsung Wallet user tops up a stablecoin balance, "USDC will appear automatically as the default dollar stablecoin." Coinbase Prime provides the subcustody, and the balances sit in Coinbase Prime Vault. Keep two limits in mind. This is announced, not launched. And a default setting is not usage; there's no volume figure yet. If it does scale, it pushes USDC further toward payments, while Tether's USDT keeps its hold on trading and collateral. That's a split by use case, not a consolidation around one issuer.

The second piece is leverage. Coinbase's new spot-leverage product, per CryptoSlate's account of the October 7th announcement, sets maximum leverage at ten times on selected major assets and five times on others. It excludes "US customers who do not qualify as Eligible Contract Participants." Regulated US perimeters tend to get built around the lowest-risk business first. Ten-times spot leverage, offered everywhere except to US retail, shows where the high-leverage business goes instead. And it ties back to the lead. Part of Coinbase's institutional growth story is a leverage business, the same kind that just got flushed twice.

The third piece is older, from late September. On September 28th, Coinbase said it had selected Citi's Virtual Account Wallet. In the companies' words, it gives Coinbase's payments customers "bank-account-like functionality to accept, hold, and pay funds." Separately, Citi will let its institutional clients accept stablecoin payments at checkout. Citi settles those funds "as the bank of record." Both pieces launch first in the United States.

So the plumbing is real: bank-grade custody, a stablecoin default inside a mass-market wallet, and a major bank settling stablecoin payments. But there's a strategic tension inside it. Being infrastructure to banks that are also building rival rails can blunt the competitive threat. It can just as easily commoditize Coinbase's own edge. That's an implication, not a measured outcome.

And none of it is showing up in Coinbase's stock. Bloomberg notes Coinbase shares have declined more than fifty percent over the past year. Adoption is being built, but it isn't yet being paid for in the equity. The bull case is that infrastructure revenue is lumpy and doesn't show until it scales, so this may simply be too early to judge. But the gap between the adoption narrative and the share price is exactly what a skeptical listener should sit with.

Pull the three threads together. The leverage in bitcoin's book was deeper than one flush revealed, and we don't yet know if it rebuilt or was hiding in a lower tier. Last week's macro test points to bitcoin trading as risk beta rather than a hedge, though it's a single week of evidence. And the institutional plumbing Coinbase is building is real and expanding, but its own equity isn't capturing it yet.

Watch four things from here. First, whether open interest and funding rebuild now that Monday's flush has passed. Second, whether ETF flows turn genuinely positive rather than just pausing. Third, September's CPI print on Wednesday, which hasn't been released yet, and the late-October Fed decision. Fourth, Coinbase's third-quarter report, due around the end of October, with the exact date still unconfirmed between sources.

That's digital money for October 13th. Stay cautious, stay optimistic, and I'll talk to you tomorrow.
