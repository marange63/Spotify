Good morning. It's Friday, August 28th, and this is your digital money briefing.

Start with the headline the industry wants you to hear, stated plainly and without irony. US spot Bitcoin ETFs have now posted a ninth straight day of inflows, adding two hundred forty-two million dollars on the twenty-seventh. Ether ETFs matched them, nine days running, adding two hundred thirty-five million in the same session. August inflows for the Bitcoin funds now sit above three billion dollars. That is already the strongest month of 2026, roughly double what April managed. It is the strongest run of the year, and it is real. Now decompose it, because every growth headline in digital money this week decomposes into something weaker than the headline.

Begin with the difference between a flow and a stock. Net assets closed just above ninety-nine billion dollars, up from about seventy-seven billion in mid-August. A twenty-two billion dollar jump in three weeks reads like fresh conviction. Most of it isn't. The reporting on those same figures is explicit that most of the gain came from price rather than from fresh money arriving. Bitcoin ran from under seventy thousand dollars in mid-August to near eighty thousand this week, marking up units that were already outstanding. So subscriptions and appreciation are being added together and reported as adoption. The three billion of actual creations is the number that measures demand. The twenty-two billion is mostly Bitcoin's price doing arithmetic on shares that already existed.

Then look inside the streak itself. The daily ticket is shrinking even as the streak lengthens. The peak was six hundred and six million dollars on August 20th. By midweek it was down to two hundred and thirty-two million. So the metric everyone quotes, streak length, is the one still improving. The metric that actually measures conviction, dollars per day, is decaying. That is the signature of a momentum trade running out of fuel, not of allocators arriving in size. And remember what started this move. August's rally opened with the biggest wave of short liquidations in records going back to 2021. That is a forced-buying event, not an allocation decision.

Now the concentration question this show has been chasing all week, because today it settles. On Wednesday we retired our own thesis that BlackRock's IBIT was taking a steadily escalating share of flows, after that share fell to sixty-two percent. Yesterday we reported a single session in which IBIT took roughly ninety percent of everything that came in. Those two readings look irreconcilable, and the multi-day view tells you why. Across an eight-day window, IBIT accounts for about seventy-two percent of the two point eight billion dollars that arrived. That seventy-two is our arithmetic on the aggregator's fund-level numbers, not a share the source states outright, so take the direction rather than the decimal. The day-to-day swings were variance. The dominance underneath them is constant.

The stock-level version is starker than the flow version. IBIT has pulled in sixty-three point one billion dollars of net inflows since launch. Category-wide cumulative net inflows across every US spot Bitcoin ETF stood at fifty-four point four billion as of Wednesday's print. One fund has taken in more than the entire category combined. That is only arithmetically possible because Grayscale's GBTC has been redeeming steadily since it converted, and its cumulative outflows drag the category total below what its single biggest winner has hauled in alone. Two caveats travel with that comparison. Both are third-party aggregator numbers, not issuer disclosures. And they come from two different articles in the same data family, with only the IBIT figure explicitly labelled since-launch. So treat it as a strong indication rather than a reconciled audit. The direction is not in doubt. When you hear "institutional adoption across the ETF complex," the honest version is narrower: one fund is carrying the category, and one legacy fund is the offsetting drag.

That concentration has a second-order consequence worth banking now. If the marginal ETF dollar is momentum rather than allocation, the same channel reverses on a price pullback. And because one wrapper dominates the flow, a single fund's redemption day will get reported as an institutional reversal of the whole complex. The reporting infrastructure will overstate the turn in exactly the way it has been overstating the accumulation.

So if ETF creations are the demand side of this story, who is on the other side supplying coins? One answer is the company that spent five years as the loudest buyer. Strategy, formerly MicroStrategy, is still the largest corporate Bitcoin treasury. In August it raised three point two eight billion dollars selling equity. It spent none of that on Bitcoin. Instead it built its dollar reserves to six point six nine billion. And since its last purchase, back on June 22nd, it has sold six thousand nine hundred and sixteen Bitcoin. The most recently disclosed tranche was one thousand six hundred and ninety coins for a hundred and eight million dollars, at an average price of sixty-four thousand two hundred and sixty-two. Every dollar of those proceeds went into buying back its own STRC preferred stock.

Size that against the flows we just walked through. At that disclosed average price, the cumulative selling is roughly four hundred and forty million dollars of supply. That is our arithmetic, not a disclosed total. Call it the same order of magnitude as two days of the ETF creations everyone is celebrating. Netting the two changes what institutional accumulation means this month.

Be precise about what this is and isn't. It is not a forced margin call. It is liability management: raising equity into dollars, and selling coins, to defend a preferred security trading below par. That is a rational choice under stress, not a collapse. Two limits on the evidence, too. These figures come from secondary reporting of the disclosures rather than from the filing text. And the record here runs through the August 9th sale. Reports of small resumed purchases after that date have not been reconciled, so I am not going to tell you Strategy is still selling this week.

The narrower honest claim is this. The marginal corporate buyer that the 2025 bull case leaned on has stopped buying. And the same vehicle now manufactures supply precisely when its own capital structure comes under pressure. That is a problem for every levered treasury company trading at a premium to the coins it holds, because the premium was always a bet on permanent accumulation with no natural seller. There is a natural seller. It is the largest holder.

The same pattern, growth on top with weaker economics underneath, shows up one layer over in tokenized dollars. Coinbase's average USDC balance hit an all-time high of twenty billion dollars last quarter. That is more than thirty percent of all USDC in circulation sitting inside a single exchange, and it produced two hundred and ninety-two million dollars of stablecoin revenue for Coinbase in the quarter. That is real footprint. It is also a circularity worth naming, because a large share of what gets counted as stablecoin demand is one exchange's own product balances rather than diversified end-user adoption.

Now the issuer's side of that same float. Circle, which issues USDC, reported its second quarter on August 5th. That is three-week-old evidence, used here to explain a live tension rather than offered as news. The stock erased an early seven percent rally and closed down nearly three percent, on concerns about weaker USDC activity and shrinking margins. And the sell side cannot agree on what the print means. Morgan Stanley has Circle at underweight, with a price target of thirty-eight dollars, cut from a hundred and six. TD Cowen initiated coverage at buy, with an eighty-two dollar target. One note on evidence quality: weaker reserve income is the analysts' characterization, not a disclosed line item I can check it against.

A thirty-eight to eighty-two spread on one company is about as clean a statement as you will get that nobody knows what a stablecoin issuer's float income is worth. The reason is structural. Reserve income is a rate-cycle rent. As rates ease, as bank issuers arrive under the GENIUS Act licensing regime, and as distribution partners demand a bigger share of the yield, that rent gets competed away. The likely answer is issuers monetizing through network and partnership fees instead of float. That is a lower-multiple, more competitive business than the one Circle was priced as at listing. Float growth and issuer profitability have decoupled, and the supply chart cannot tell you which way that resolves.

So: three growth headlines this week, and three compositions that argue the other way. An ETF streak lengthening while its daily size shrinks and its dollars funnel through one wrapper. The largest corporate holder converting equity issuance into dollars instead of coins. And a stablecoin issuer scaling its float while the income on that float compresses hard enough to split Wall Street two ways on a single print. Nothing on the policy calendar resolves this soon, since the Senate's first procedural vote on the CLARITY Act is not until September 15th. So watch the market instead. Two things would falsify the skeptical read: the inflow streak surviving its first real down session, and Strategy resuming net purchases. Until one of those happens, treat the headline numbers as composition, not conviction.

That's the digital money briefing for August 28th. Have a good one.
