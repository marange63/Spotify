Good morning, this is The Throughline for Thursday, October 22nd, 2026.

Today's headline: Amazon put its name on new nuclear generation, twelve small modular reactors in Indiana with X-energy and AEP, but it's a development target, not a construction permit.

Nine briefings make this edition. We'll start with the macro backdrop, move into the AI and compute stack, and close on the applied frontier.

Strategic Power opens with that Amazon deal. The plan is twelve Xe-100 reactors at a retired Indiana coal plant, roughly 960 megawatts. It's the first project in the desk's recent run that targets new generation, rather than a subscription to power that already exists. But the announcement carries no price and no in-service date. And against a 268-gigawatt power gap projected by TrendForce, Amazon's entire five-gigawatt program covers only about two percent. The same briefing tracks the negotiated sale of Lukoil's foreign assets, initially valued at about 22 billion dollars. Carlyle has walked away, and the Kremlin says the license for a sale "could be extended without difficulty." In both stories, the test is whether an announcement becomes a binding commitment.

From power to money. Digital Money has the bitcoin ETF bid broadening. On Wednesday, inflows came through BlackRock's IBIT, Fidelity's FBTC, and even Grayscale's GBTC. That breaks the week's pattern of one fund carrying the tape. What the desk can't yet say is whether the buyer is an allocator or a basis trade. The corporate bid is shrinking the other way. Strategy bought just 267 bitcoin last week, funded almost entirely by selling its own common stock. That's under a tenth of IBIT's Wednesday inflow alone. And the OCC finalized its stablecoin reserve and redemption rule, but left exchange-paid yield to future enforcement.

Private Credit finds the squeeze reaching the top of the quality ladder. Blackstone's flagship BDC, the name many allocators treat as the benchmark, is guiding toward a lower dividend even though its credit looks sound. That makes it a rate-and-spread story, not a default story. Further down, FS KKR's income now exactly matches its payout, per a 24/7 Wall St. survey, leaving zero cushion. The Fed's first measurement of private credit in its Financial Accounts shows who absorbs this. The funds are financed mostly by equity, about 1.1 trillion dollars, roughly three times their drawn bank loans. So losses land first on pension funds, insurers, and wealth-channel investors, not on banks.

Private Equity has the biggest entry yet in the show's take-private tally, and the first one two mega-sponsors visibly fought over. EQT agreed to take Qiagen private for about 10.8 billion dollars, after a contest with KKR lifted the price above EQT's reported binding offer. It's signed, not closed, and it's money going in. Not one dollar goes back to a private equity limited partner. Meanwhile the exit side keeps stalling. CVC is exploring a take-private of the recently listed Verisure, a day after AirTrunk's listing reportedly slipped to 2027. The so-what: deployment is competitive and paying up, while distribution still runs mostly through engineered channels like continuation vehicles and NAV loans.

That's the macro backdrop. Now the AI and compute stack, where the question is who allocates a scarce resource. Semiconductors and Compute leads with a Reuters report that ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent have ordered more than 3 million of Nvidia's export-compliant RTX PRO 5500 cards. That's about 35 billion dollars at list. But Reuters also says Nvidia can make no more than 3 million of these cards globally in 2027. So the order book looks inflated by shortage over-ordering. Washington sets the menu, and Nvidia decides the portions. The same briefing flags a single-source SemiAnalysis report that Nvidia shut some neoclouds out of a conference after they added AMD or Google chips.

AI Products and Applications finds the controls lagging the products. OpenAI's frontier models are now in private preview inside Google Cloud, billed against existing Google commitments. That's a real procurement unlock, but Google lists zero data retention as "coming soon." Anthropic, which makes the model behind this show, opened Claude Duet, an always-on personal agent built around an approval gate before it acts. It's a waitlisted preview, and the gate is Anthropic's own description, untested from outside.

Enterprise Technology splits AI backlog by who can fund it. SAP and IBM are building AI-linked backlog their own cash flow can carry. Oracle is not. Its free cash flow was negative 5.4 billion dollars last quarter, against a 664-billion-dollar order book. A TD Cowen analyst now suggests Nvidia may be helping Oracle secure financing, which neither company has confirmed. Anthropic, meanwhile, is reportedly in talks on a second compute deal with the neocloud Nscale, worth roughly 45 billion dollars. Nscale says it hasn't yet secured binding financing for the contracts it already has.

Now the applied frontier, where capacity still runs ahead of proof. Robotics and Physical AI has Tesla describing an Optimus line designed for 20,000 units a week. But reaching that rate likely takes at least a year, and paying customers aren't expected until around the end of next year. Amazon, by contrast, has passed one million warehouse robots, overwhelmingly task-specific machines working today.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with Isomorphic Labs. It's reportedly raising at a 40-to-50-billion-dollar valuation, while declining to say whether it will still meet its year-end pledge to put a drug into humans. The contrast is Anthropic's toxin-binder campaign, again from the company behind this show's model. It published its failures alongside a hit rate of roughly 8 percent. The bottleneck it exposed is physical synthesis and assay capacity, not design.

Here's the thread worth carrying out. On Tuesday, this show named the gap between the headline and the binding document. Today that gap runs through nearly every desk: a reactor target without a permit, a Lukoil sale without a named buyer, data retention marked "coming soon," and an Nscale deal without financing. Watch which of those becomes paper first.

That's The Throughline. Have a good Thursday.
