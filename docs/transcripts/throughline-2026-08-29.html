Good morning, this is The Throughline for Saturday, August 29th, 2026. Today's headline: Kevin Warsh gave his first Jackson Hole address as Fed chair, never named the September meeting, and the market still moved September hike odds from about thirty-five percent to about sixty. One speech moved gold, bitcoin and the discount rate under the whole AI capex trade.

Start with the macro backdrop. Strategic Power tracks three chokepoints on three different clocks. A second tanker was hit in the Strait of Hormuz this week, and crude fell anyway, to about eighty-two dollars. But the Oman-brokered navigation corridor behind that calm is proposed, not signed, and nobody has established it is why oil fell. China's rare-earth controls are still formally suspended, but enforcement has tightened through customs prosecutions. The NdPr magnet benchmark rose twenty-one percent in a month, on a July 1st print, so read it as physical scarcity rather than today's tape. The third chokepoint is domestic. PJM's own market monitor now attributes nine percent of wholesale power prices to data-center load, and says that share will keep growing.

From chokepoints to the rate path, because Capital Markets Radar has the connective tissue for everything after it. That repricing happened in a single session, on CME FedWatch, with no CPI, no payrolls and no FOMC decision in between. Gold sold off about three percent, and bitcoin fell 3.2 percent alongside it. The Radar had expected gold to hold up better than a pure rates asset. It did not, and the Radar marks that call wrong. What did not move is the more interesting part. High-yield spreads sat near 281 basis points going into the speech, the richest decile in that index's history. That is a pre-speech print with no post-speech read behind it. So the market's most expensive credit is priced against a Fed that was still easing.

That repricing lands straight on Digital Money, which spent the day pulling apart the most bullish claim on its beat. Grayscale published research putting bitcoin's ninety-day correlation with gold above fifty percent, up from near zero this year. Read straight, that is bitcoin graduating into a debasement hedge. But the window producing that shift is the same window that held a record short-liquidation cascade, and Grayscale is an ETF issuer describing its own product. Then Warsh spoke, and bitcoin traded down with gold rather than as a hedge against it.

Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle has the day's cleanest decoupling. Institutional limited partners committed a hundred and nineteen billion dollars to private credit funds in the second quarter, the fastest quarter on record. Fitch's trailing default rate hit a record six percent over that same stretch. And the retail wrapper holding much the same paper is queuing to redeem. Same asset class, opposite direction of flow. The binding risk has moved from recognition to vintage. It is no longer whether bad loans get marked down. It is where a record raise gets deployed once the best paper is gone.

Now the AI and compute stack, where a lot of that capital is heading. Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has Nvidia's fiscal-2028 revenue growth outlook of roughly seventy percent, against about forty-four percent penciled in by analysts. How management framed it matters more than the number. They called it supply-constrained, not demand-constrained. That moves the swing factor off hyperscaler budgets and onto memory. In the same report, Nvidia's supply commitments rose to two hundred seventy-nine billion dollars, from a hundred nineteen billion the quarter before, primarily for memory. Nvidia never linked those two numbers, so treat the pairing as timing rather than proof, but a supply-constrained guide and a memory-heavy commitment landing in one report is the tell.

Frontier AI Lab Competition has the closest thing to evidence this stack produced all week. Anthropic opened a research preview that lets Claude agents drive laboratory hardware. At the quantum-hardware firm QuEra, a laser-recovery routine went from fifty-eight percent reliable to ninety-nine point three percent across seven hundred blind trials. That is a claim with a denominator, and with failures counted inside it. But it is still self-reported, published by Anthropic, on hardware Anthropic's own team integrated. Set it beside Alibaba's three billion Qwen downloads, a total with no usage behind it, and the difference is the trial count, not the auditor.

AI Products and Applications has three companies shipping agents inside a two-day window, disagreeing on method and converging on the same evasion. Anthropic took Claude in Chrome to general availability and published prompt-injection rates rather than task-completion rates. OpenAI shipped the opposite architecture inside ChatGPT's browser, letting sites hand agents structured actions, then switched it off for its own enterprise workspaces. Perplexity's new on-device agent scores about seventy-three percent even after escalating hard cases to the cloud, so more than a quarter of tasks still fail. Here it is in plain words. None of the three publishes a clean end-to-end success rate on tasks the vendor did not choose, which is the one number a buyer needs.

Enterprise Technology found the same trap inside earnings. SentinelOne beat on revenue, then fell eight percent on an eleven-percent cut to per-share guidance. But the same release raised both revenue and operating income. The likely culprit is a bigger share count, not a margin break, though that explanation comes from secondary coverage. Salesforce ran the distortion in reverse. Two dollars and fifty-three cents of its five dollars ninety in non-GAAP earnings per share came from an unrealized markup on its Anthropic stake. Two prints, opposite directions, and both times the market traded the per-share line as if it described software.

The same schedule-versus-contract gap shows up one layer out, in space. Space and the Orbital Economy has engineering dates slipping while contractual dates hold. Blue Origin's first Blue Moon lander is now no earlier than the first quarter of 2027, Vast's Haven-1 station has moved into the same quarter, and Rocket Lab's Neutron may follow. Three unrelated companies converging on one quarter points at shared test and qualification capacity, not three separate stumbles. Meanwhile Amazon blew past an FCC licence deadline for sixteen hundred satellites, with under four hundred deployed. And the Space Development Agency wrote 1.75 billion dollars of Golden Dome tracking awards into a programme that may lose funding in October, so the money is committed before the programme's own funding is.

One thread is worth naming, and it has now run three days. This show keeps flagging figures published by the party they flatter, with no outside check attached. Yesterday the only real checks were compelled ones, an OSHA filing and a randomized trial. Today's best number is still self-published. Anthropic's trial count has a denominator and counted failures underneath it, which makes it a better class of claim, not an independent one. SentinelOne's guidance table, Salesforce's equity mark and Alibaba's download total all still carry the original defect.
