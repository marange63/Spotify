Good morning, this is The Throughline for Wednesday, August 12th, 2026. Today's headline: the July core CPI print is due the same morning the White House moved a second time to remove Fed Governor Lisa Cook, so the question of whether the Fed can hold its line is about to get tested from the data side and the political side at once.

Start with the macro backdrop. Strategic Power has both halves of that collision. The July CPI report lands at 8:30 Eastern this morning and is the scheduled test of whether inflation is cooling or reaccelerating, while a new White House letter gives Cook until August 26th to respond to a fraud allegation the Supreme Court's own footnote invited the administration to re-test. Layer on a milestone-gated rare-earths deal and an August 22nd deadline on Lukoil sanctions, and the pattern is the same one: announced power that hasn't yet become delivered power.

Capital Markets Radar frames that same CPI print from the trading desk's side, as the tiebreaker for the FOMC's three-way split, with a hot core reading set to hand the hawkish dissenters real ammunition and a soft one letting the doves close the gap. It hasn't printed yet. Meanwhile gold hit $4,406 an ounce, copper set a fresh record on the same Hormuz disruption that's kept oil elevated, and high-yield spreads still sit near their richest decile ever, pricing almost none of it. So the market that's supposed to arbitrate this fight isn't showing up yet.

Digital Money shows what that rate uncertainty does one layer down. Circle's reserve income and Coinbase's transaction margins are both compressing as short rates soften, and a near-billion-dollar Bitcoin ETF inflow streak reversed this week without ever moving the price, the clearest evidence yet that ETF flows aren't setting Bitcoin's price the way the adoption story assumes.

Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle has the loss-given-default question still unresolved: First Brands' bankruptcy plan models an 8.4-cent recovery, unconfirmed, with a Chapter 7 tail still live, while blue-chip BDC books like Ares Capital keep creeping worse even as the sector headline claims stabilization. Private Equity and the Deal Economy closes the macro tour with a record quarter at the very top of the industry — KKR, Blackstone, Carlyle and Apollo all posted record fee income — but none of them will disclose the one number, realized carry by exit channel, that would prove it's genuine liquidity rather than capital moving through secondaries the same managers control.

Now the AI and compute stack, where today's real news is who's financing it. Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has Samsung's HBM4 yield jumping to 80%, the first credible challenge to SK hynix's scarcity pricing, compounded by a stalled SK hynix wage dispute at home. Frontier AI Lab Competition has the bigger structural move: Nvidia organized a coalition of Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to source over $500 billion in third-party capital for AI infrastructure, and Anthropic answered with its own Theseus joint venture, leasing dedicated capacity from Macquarie and Singapore's GIC rather than owning it outright. AI Products and Applications shows the layer above that: Anthropic now embeds machine-readable watermarks across Claude's outputs under EU rules, and it opened MCP up to standard enterprise identity systems, both unglamorous moves that decide whether agents clear a bank's or hospital's procurement review. Enterprise Technology confirms the gap that leaves open: Atlassian's agent connector hit a million monthly users with zero disclosed revenue attached, and IBM cited Gartner data that 84% of finance leaders can't even measure AI's return, so adoption keeps outrunning proof it pays.

Turning to the applied frontier. Robotics and Physical AI has Symbotic posting a real GAAP profit, the clearest disclosed earnings proof point in physical AI this week, but the stock fell anyway on management's own admission that its Walmart rollout won't reach a fleet order until early 2028 at the earliest. Space and the Orbital Economy has SpaceX's first public earnings report showing AI and cloud-compute revenue, not Starlink, as the biggest driver of its growth, even as the company posted a $541 million quarterly loss and Amazon's Kuiper constellation missed its FCC half-constellation deadline, landing barely a fifth of the way there. AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with the same operations-versus-outcomes split running through medicine: Novo Nordisk committed its infrastructure to AWS's agentic AI tools this week, while Recursion's revenue nearly halved, the clearest sign yet that capital is following auditable data and documentation work well ahead of any proof AI raises the odds a molecule survives the clinic.

One thread worth naming before we close. This is the third straight day this show has tracked the AI race turning into a financing race — Monday it was PJM's power price and TSMC's sold-out packaging, Tuesday it was capital and regulators outrunning proof across robots and rockets. Today that constraint got a name and a term sheet: Nvidia's $500 billion capital coalition, Anthropic's Theseus lease, and SpaceX's own pivot to AI-compute revenue are the same story showing up in three unrelated briefings, and the risk underneath all three is the same — a lot of money is now treating financed compute as a proven asset class before anyone has tested what it's worth when a model generation goes obsolete.
