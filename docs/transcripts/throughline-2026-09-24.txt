Good morning, this is The Throughline for Thursday, September 24th, 2026. Today's headline: the ten-year Treasury yield jumped above 5.1 percent, its highest since 2007, and stocks, Bitcoin, gold and silver all sold off with it.

Start with the macro backdrop. Strategic Power has Texas Governor Greg Abbott widening his data-center freeze from the grid queue to the permitting desk, telling state regulators to withhold new air and water permits until ERCOT audits its queue. The open question is whether that freeze reaches behind-the-meter gas plants. If it does, hyperscalers lose their self-supply workaround as well as the grid.

Capital Markets Cross-Asset Radar traces the yield move to the Fed. Governor Michael Barr backed an October hike, and a manufacturing PMI more than three points above consensus gave the hawks cover. The piece to sit with is a five-year auction that tailed on weak foreign and institutional demand, because if that repeats at the longer auctions, higher yields stop being self-limiting.

Crypto took the same shock. Digital Money has spot Bitcoin ETFs extending their inflow streak to four sessions and 2.3 billion dollars, then Bitcoin getting rejected near 87 thousand on the day the yield spiked, with about 230 million dollars of longs liquidated in a single hour. The desk calls that coincident timing, not proven causation. And with perpetual funding running near double the ten-year yield, some of that ETF flow may be carry rather than conviction, which makes Friday's quarterly expiry the next test.

Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle is about plumbing that now runs both ways. Blackstone is developing a hybrid CLO that blends market-priced syndicated loans with model-priced direct loans, and the briefing found no Moody's, S&P or Fitch framework for a vehicle whose mix can shift after closing. In the other direction, JPMorgan reportedly asked private lenders whether they would approve card applicants the bank itself rejects, though JPMorgan says it has no plans for such a program.

Private Equity and the Deal Economy shows leverage standing in for exits. Apollo is in talks to nearly double its loan against SoftBank's Vision Fund 2 portfolio, to as much as nine billion dollars, timed to SoftBank's OpenAI commitment and letting it borrow rather than sell illiquid stakes. With 2022-vintage funds returning a median of just nine cents on the dollar, borrowing against marks is becoming the industry's substitute for distributions.

Now to the AI and compute stack, starting with the input everyone is paying more for. Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure weighs SK hynix's claim that memory demand could outstrip supply beyond 2030, backed by a broker estimate of supplier inventory below ten days. But hyperscalers have been pulling DRAM forward into their own stockpiles, so thin supplier inventory may partly be stock that migrated to the buyer. The shortage is real today; "structural" is still a claim, on material the briefing flags as ten days to a month old.

One layer up, model prices are falling fast. Frontier AI Lab Competition opens with a correction: OpenAI didn't wait for DevDay. It cut GPT-6 Sol and Luna prices by half or more on September 22nd, the same day Anthropic's Opus 5.5 shipped, and called the cut permanent. Meanwhile Artificial Analysis puts Opus 5.5 five points ahead of GPT-6 Astra on its intelligence index, so Anthropic owns the leaderboard and OpenAI owns the cheaper sticker.

AI Products and Applications finds the catch in the half-price headline. On cached tokens, which Anthropic says dominate agentic coding costs, a secondary pricing table lists Sol and Opus 5.5 at the same twenty cents per million. So the number buyers actually need, cost per completed task, still hasn't been published by either lab.

Enterprise Technology follows the fight into distribution. Anthropic has added Accenture and Deloitte to its Claude Marketplace, where customers can spend part of a committed Anthropic contract on partner software, putting the model vendor upstream of the application sale. Meanwhile a vendor-sponsored survey finds only 31 percent of organizations can immediately stop a malfunctioning agent with an automated kill switch, so distribution is outrunning governance.

Turning to the applied frontier, where the constraint is physical. Robotics and Physical AI puts a price on the magnet and actuator chokepoint. An OpenMind estimate has a humanoid built without Chinese suppliers at roughly 131 thousand dollars, nearly triple the 46 thousand through standard chains. And reports that Tesla has certified three Chinese component makers for Optimus, which Tesla hasn't confirmed, suggest the leading Western program is scaling through that dependency rather than around it.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with the inputs of drug discovery being repriced. Recursion's filing shows it cut its data-licensing bill with Tempus AI by at least a third over three years, in exchange for a longer term and giving up its right to walk away. And AbbVie's deal with Iambic extends a run of large-pharma AI deals with no disclosed cash at signing, as big pharma pays for AI-native design in milestones and royalties rather than upfront dollars.

One change is worth marking against yesterday. On Wednesday, this show described risk assets pricing peace while rates priced persistence, with the ten-year near 4.96 percent. One session later, rates won, and every risk asset on the board sold off with them.
