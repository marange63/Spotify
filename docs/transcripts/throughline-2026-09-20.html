Good morning, this is The Throughline for Sunday, September 20th, 2026. Today's headline came out of a securities filing. Nscale, an AI data center builder now going public, has 103.4 billion dollars of signed backlog. Only 2.6 billion of it is actually running today. Measured under securities liability, the AI buildout is still mostly a promise.

Start with the macro backdrop, and with Strategic Power. The president asked Ukraine to stop striking Russian diesel refineries after US diesel passed six dollars a gallon, and no formal deal stood behind the request. The larger point is that America's buffers are drawing down together, with the strategic oil reserve at its lowest level since 1982 and the Congressional Budget Office saying spent interceptor stocks could take five years to rebuild. Washington is negotiating with less cushion than its posture implies.

Capital Markets Radar stays with the week's rate shock and asks where it has actually been tested. The answer is nowhere that hurts. High-yield spreads tightened six basis points across the Fed's hike, and a record 9.6 trillion dollars of US options expired Friday, which may simply have pinned equity volatility. So the calm is real but unproven, and the dated tests are Monday's spread print and payrolls on October 2nd.

Digital Money finds thin demand under the Bitcoin rally. US spot ETFs took in 433 million dollars on Friday, yet the whole week netted about six million. Glassnode counts roughly a million coins bought between eighty-three and eighty-six thousand dollars, and Bitcoin's high on Friday was just under eighty-two thousand. Those owners are nearly back to even, which is when supply usually comes out, and six million dollars a week will not absorb it.

Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle finds the tail much worse than the headline. Blackstone marks the weakest five percent of its private debt book at 63 cents, far below the mark on the book as a whole. Meanwhile Oracle-linked data center loans sit unsold below par, while a Meta-leased deal was wildly oversubscribed. The pricing variable is tenant credit, not the building.

Private Equity and the Deal Economy revises its own argument in the other direction. Bain counts about 33,000 unsold portfolio companies on a seven-year holding clock, and yet Carlyle and KKR are both returning real cash, so the honest word is dispersion, not freeze. The open question is who prices a continuation vehicle when the sponsor sits on both sides, and ILPA's draft guidance is the first institutional attempt to answer it.

Now the AI and compute stack, where announcement and delivery diverge as a physical limit. Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure argues that announced capability is outrunning buildable capability. Huawei's new flagship pod holds 4,096 chips, against the 15,488-chip system its own release described a year ago, under a different product name, so treat the gap as directional. And JPMorgan came away from an ASML meeting thinking that by 2028 the limit is not orders. It is how fast ASML can assemble the machines. Throughput, not capital, is the binding constraint.

Frontier AI Lab Competition has two threads from the same filing cabinet. A class action now names Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and Musk's lab, the company behind Grok, arguing that their public agreement to slow down was itself an antitrust violation. The complaint is unadjudicated and the damages theory is thin, so weigh the signal, not the merits. The second thread is the Nscale prospectus, which shows Anthropic contracted for payments of up to 44.6 billion dollars. Spending, not speech, is the falsifier for pacing.

AI Products and Applications takes that cost question to the invoice. Microsoft will make usage-based billing the default for new Copilot Business licenses in the partner channel from November 2nd. Every number vendors published this week was a price or a limit, not a result. The meter is being installed before anyone has shown that the agents pay for themselves.

Enterprise Technology reads that same Nscale filing more carefully. The contracted total spans a multi-year term, so it is not an annual figure, and the company lost about a billion dollars in the first half standing the capacity up. Then Oracle said the vast majority of its new backlog came with prepayment or customer-supplied hardware. Customers are financing the buildout, which moves the risk rather than removing it.

Last, the applied frontier, where capital keeps running ahead of proof. Robotics and Physical AI finally has an outside number, and one research house counts about 19,100 humanoid shipments in the first half, up 272 percent, almost entirely from Chinese vendors. Against that, 1X says it will ship 50,000 units in 2027, more than twice what the whole industry managed in six months. Step away from humanoids, and the only physical-AI buyer with a named program and a budget line is the Air Force, which wants at least 500 uncrewed collaborative combat aircraft by 2032.

Space and the Orbital Economy says a counterspace argument became a purchase order. The Defense Innovation Unit and Space Systems Command picked Northrop Grumman and True Anomaly to prototype geosynchronous inspection satellites, with no dollar value published. Everywhere else, capital is spending around the launch bottleneck, and Eutelsat is putting about a billion euros into replenishing OneWeb rather than wait for cheaper lift.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with a new grader. The FDA accepted Takeda's zasocitinib application under Priority Review, with an action date in the first quarter of 2027, after two Phase 3 trials each cleared roughly 70 percent skin clearance. But Takeda does not call the molecule AI-designed, and neither does this briefing. An approval will certify a compound and credit no method.

One change from yesterday is worth naming. On Saturday this show said nearly every new number came from the party being graded, with Nvidia's margin as the exception. Today's exception is a prospectus, a number written under securities liability, and it says the buildout is still mostly promise.
