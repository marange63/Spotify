Good morning, this is The Throughline for Monday, November 9th, 2026.

Today's headline: Nvidia's board is investigating Jensen Huang's ties to a woman U.S. intelligence reportedly suspects of passing information to Beijing, and crypto, the only market open all weekend, priced the shock first.

Nine briefings shipped today. We'll start with the macro backdrop, move through the AI and compute stack, and close on the applied frontier.

Strategic Power has TD Cowen cutting its post-election odds on the bipartisan permitting-reform bill from about 40 percent to about 25, per Reuters. That makes FERC's docket on load interconnection the live federal lever on data-center power, with grid-operator responses due November 16th. Beijing's levers need no paperwork: Taiwan's National Security Council counts more than a hundred PLA Navy and coast guard vessels across the region, with no drill announced. An AEI and ISW assessment reads that as an exercise simulating Taiwan's isolation, though it's analysis, not confirmed fact.

From the strait to the weekend tape. Digital Money has Bitcoin falling below seventy-four thousand dollars late Sunday, its lowest since August, as more than a billion dollars of leveraged positions were liquidated, with Ether's share nearly double Bitcoin's. The most telling weekend price was Nvidia's own, as the tokenized NVDAX share ended about three and a half percent below Friday's close, and this morning's Nasdaq open tests whether that was real price discovery or a thin-market artifact. With spot Bitcoin ETF flows now negative for the year, the desk reads leverage, not institutional demand, as setting the marginal price, and Bitcoin as front-running AI-equity risk rather than diversifying it.

That's the macro backdrop. Now the AI and compute stack, where this week's binding constraints are permission and capital, not the chips themselves.

Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure carries the Nvidia story, stressing that the intelligence concern is reported, not a confirmed finding, and that the board says it has no reason to believe secrets were compromised. The new piece is that three House committee chairmen had already written to Nvidia on November 4th, days before the board's probe, asking Huang to brief Congress. The desk's read is that this hits Nvidia's China channel and its standing in Washington, not its architecture or operations, just as Beijing drafts mirror-image controls on exporting chip-design know-how.

A standing disclosure for the next two desks: Anthropic makes the model behind this show. Frontier AI Lab Competition finds this week's binding constraint was the cheque, not the chip, with OpenAI closing eleven billion dollars, roughly a third of its reported original target. Anthropic's amended IPO filing, per CNBC's read, shows Anthropic owing 69.8 billion dollars of commitments within a year, which the desk puts at over four times its first-half revenue, a base it calls stale. And Moonshot's open-weight Kimi K3.1 Pro, which a widely shared post says serves on a single eight-GPU server, puts a ceiling on the pricing power those raises are meant to underwrite.

AI Products and Applications has each platform absorbing the step beside it: Cursor's new Build Live Apps hands back a running, deployed app rather than code, gated by pre-publish checks Cursor has barely described. Anthropic's Marketplace lets committed Claude spend buy other vendors' agents, and GitHub Copilot retires two Claude models and a GPT model on November 16th. So in each case, the layer underneath becomes a swappable part, replaced on the platform's schedule, not the user's.

Enterprise Technology has Bloomberg reporting that Microsoft is winding down a rented-GPU arrangement with Oracle, which unnamed sources tie to an older Nvidia chip generation. The desk reads that as flipping Oracle's risk from funding its build to whether its customers stay committed to using it. While Microsoft hands rented capacity back, Salesforce is locking customers into a flat fifty-dollar monthly seat for Agentforce Coworker, a price that buys the customer predictability and hands the vendor the inference bill.

That's the compute stack. Now the applied frontier, where the pace is set by who grants permission and by what has actually flown.

Robotics and Physical AI has the Financial Times reporting that Chinese regulators withheld clearance for SoftBank's purchase of ABB's robotics unit over concerns about robot data leaving the country to train AI models. That's a press-sourced rationale for a deal that is paused, not dead, but it moves Beijing's lever from ownership to the training data itself. Capital is chasing the same control logic into sensors, as Shield AI's new 28.8 billion dollar valuation, more than double March's, came alongside its purchase of radar maker Echodyne.

Space and the Orbital Economy has SpaceX's announcement, relayed by a company-watcher on X, planning Starship Flight 15 this Friday as the first to catch both stages. Blue Origin is the opposite case, with a single-sourced Dave Limp quote about flying this year contradicting his own memo targeting January 2027, so the desk says nobody should model a New Glenn date until the company publishes one. Varda's seventh capsule reentry, with three missions run at once, is the week's cleanest example of hardware already flying in a fleet rhythm, though Varda disclosed no revenue.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with Recursion shopping two of its own drug programs to partners rather than funding them, turning a platform pharma buys options on into a seller of options. Yesterday this show said the desk had withdrawn its description of Insilico's AI-discovered drug rentosertib as being in Phase 3, because its research couldn't confirm it. That withdrawal was wrong: Insilico's own release says it has dosed the first patient in a placebo-controlled Phase 3 trial of rentosertib, a status resting on the company's word, not a registry check. With a year-long treatment period, the desk sees the proof clock on AI-designed molecules running in years, not quarters.

One date ran through three desks today. Strategic Power, Semiconductors, and Robotics each flagged tomorrow, when China's pause on its expanded rare-earth export controls is due to lapse, and as of the latest reporting Beijing has published no formal extension.
