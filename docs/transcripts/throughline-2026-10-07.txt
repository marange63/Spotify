Today's headline: DeepSeek is finalizing a funding round of at least eighty billion yuan, roughly twelve billion dollars, and pairing it with a plan to run at least one hundred sixty thousand Huawei chips in a new Inner Mongolia data center.

Twelve briefings shipped today. We'll start with the macro backdrop, move into the AI and compute stack, then finish with the applied frontier.

Strategic Power says Google's deal with Constellation Energy is the first hyperscaler nuclear agreement to pass the desk's additivity test, with 890 genuinely new megawatts for the PJM grid. But the first uprate is only expected by 2028, and a separate 2,700-megawatt contract alongside it looks like existing output. Meanwhile, Texas is auditing a 474-gigawatt connection queue, more than five times record peak demand, and ERCOT says that review won't wrap until December.

Capital Markets Radar explains how the ten-year Treasury touched its highest level since 2002 while stocks kept setting records. A hot ISM services price index pushed the yield above 5.3 percent intraday, but breakevens held flat, so the desk reads a policy-path shock, not an inflation shock. Equities bought further out in time instead, into Marvell's end-of-decade guidance and multi-decade power contracts, so their sensitivity to that ignored rate keeps rising.

Digital Money has bitcoin pinned near 86,300 dollars, where long-term holders and spot ETF buyers roughly break even, so a daily close above 86,000 is the test. Underneath, the stablecoin float has gone flat while on-chain transfer volume fell about 23 percent. And the rulebook itself is now in court, after community banks sued the Comptroller over crypto trust charters.

Private Credit leads with a Semafor report, on unnamed sources, that Fed examiners have been inside JPMorgan, Wells Fargo, Barclays and Morgan Stanley since spring. The review was reportedly prompted in part by JPMorgan's own markdowns on software loans threatened by AI. The stress itself is sorting by sector, concentrated in Blue Owl's tech-focused fund rather than spreading across the complex.

Now the AI and compute stack, where the scarce layers are setting the price.

Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure reports Samsung asking more than triple its current price for next-generation HBM4, though the like-for-like increase is nearer 2.2 times, and no customer has accepted it. TrendForce projects advanced packaging prices up 10 to 15 percent next year, faster than leading-edge wafers. Custom silicon doesn't escape that squeeze, since Marvell's own compute-die ramp lands in fiscal 2029, after 2027 input pricing is being set.

Frontier AI Lab Competition carries the headline in full, with Tencent and CATL reportedly the biggest backers. The desk's read is that if the round closes, DeepSeek is no longer short of cash, but a plan to run Huawei chips is not chips in racks. The pressure lands on mid-tier pricing, where OpenAI and Google both sit at two dollars in and ten out per million tokens.

AI Products and Applications leads with Anthropic's Claude Code Mods, and since Anthropic makes the model behind this show, weigh that accordingly. Mods can rewrite prompts and approve or block tool calls, they're on by default, and they run unsandboxed with the user's own permissions. On the consumer side, free Gemini users reportedly lose Flash and Pro on October 9th, the latest case of launch terms shrinking after launch.

Enterprise Technology finds SAP making a similar control bet, repositioning its Joule assistant as a work layer across SAP and non-SAP systems. Beyond SAP's own 110,000 employees, there's no customer count and no pricing. One layer down, integrators like Accenture and SAIC are buying security and identity software outright, a bet on turning services bookings into owned, recurring revenue.

Now the applied frontier, where the question shifts from who owns the layer to who does the work underneath it.

Robotics and Physical AI reports that California's SB 1246, now signed into law, makes the human behind a robotaxi a regulated line item, requiring U.S.-based remote drivers and on-site incident technicians by July 2028. A new humanoid startup, Minerva, sells that same human as the product, raising about 10 million dollars to let specialists do hazardous work by teleoperation rather than claiming autonomy at all.

Space and the Orbital Economy says Google's Tensor Processing Units reached orbit aboard the Project Suncatcher test satellite, the first physical step toward orbital AI compute. But one qualification satellite, with no published performance data, sits against SpaceX's pending filing for up to a million compute satellites. Golden Dome has the opposite problem, with contractor awards stacking up while its funding depends on a reconciliation package Congress hasn't delivered.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with pharma renting its AI on terms built to be cut. AbbVie's new Iambic deal discloses no amounts, and where terms are public, as in Insilico's alliance with SK Biopharmaceuticals, near-term payments are under one percent of a headline above 2.5 billion dollars. On the build side, Genentech just lost the global head of its AI drug-discovery effort.

So here's the thread. DeepSeek's Huawei plan, Samsung's HBM4 ask, Google's orbital chips and SAP's Joule are all announcements running ahead of delivered capacity, and each briefing names the test that will settle it.
