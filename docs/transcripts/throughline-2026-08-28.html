Good morning, this is The Throughline for Friday, August 28th, 2026. Today's headline: a hyperscaler reportedly walked away from more than a gigawatt of next-generation AI compute in the middle of a capacity crunch, and a frontier lab signed it up, a commitment now doing heavy lifting in its IPO pitch. That is the sharpest test yet of the claim that AI demand is broadening beyond a few hyperscalers, and it came from the buyer's side, not a seller's press release.

Start with the macro backdrop. Strategic Power has the Pentagon signing roughly two billion dollars of critical-minerals agreements in a single week, across battery anodes, scandium, rare-earth-free magnets and bauxite. Watch the verbs: these are mostly conditional loan commitments, not disbursed dollars, copying the MP Materials template that shifts price and demand risk onto the government's balance sheet. That template now carries an unresolved conflict-of-interest question, after reporting tied the lawyer who structured it to the Deputy Defense Secretary personally.

Capital Markets Radar has the print that undercuts this week's easier narrative. July core PCE ran at 3.3 percent, just above the FactSet poll, and headline missed at 3.7. The ten-year yield has risen two sessions running into Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole speech as Fed chair. Nvidia's 108 billion dollar guide, up another 12 percent sequentially, accelerates the capex cycle into a rate path that just got harder to argue down. The Radar's read is that the collision needs reconciling rather than reporting side by side. What makes it tolerable is Brent, down more than seven percent on the week on a reported and unconfirmed Iran-Oman arrangement over Hormuz. So the disinflation capping the long end is hostage to diplomacy rather than to barrels.

Digital Money has bitcoin ETFs on a ninth straight day of inflows. The composition underneath is deteriorating. The daily ticket has shrunk from 606 million dollars to 232 million even as the streak lengthens. And on aggregator data, BlackRock's IBIT alone has taken in more than the entire category's cumulative net inflows, with Grayscale's redemptions dragging the total down. Strategy, the largest corporate holder, has sold nearly 7,000 coins since June to defend its own preferred stock rather than buying more.

Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle has the week's sharpest liquidity tell. A tender for five non-traded BDCs at a 26 percent discount to net asset value drew under five million dollars of orders. Holders chose a rationed redemption queue over a certain loss, which is a statement about the gate rather than a vote of confidence in the marks.

Private Equity and the Deal Economy closes the macro leg with the same governance split. Single-asset continuation vehicles, the ones sponsors pick and time, price at or above par. Multi-asset vehicles, the closest analog to an allocator selling a whole book, price below par 71 percent of the time. Control of the sale, more than asset quality, is setting the price.

Now the AI and compute stack, where today's headline gets its receipts. Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has Nvidia confirming that Vera Rubin began production shipments this month. That resolves, on Nvidia's own record, the gap the Semiconductors beat has tracked against OpenAI's still-engineering-sample Jalapeno chip. The cost side is the offset: HBM3e contract pricing is up roughly twenty percent quarter over quarter, which Nvidia can absorb from a 75-point gross margin and challengers without contracted memory cannot.

Frontier AI Lab Competition has the headline itself. Anthropic has reportedly signed roughly 45 billion dollars of compute from Nscale, on West Virginia capacity Microsoft walked away from this summer. None of it is confirmed by a filing or a press release. And the IPO case behind it leans on revenue figures nobody, Anthropic included, has confirmed since April, with three materially different numbers circulating for one company in one week.

AI Products and Applications has the enterprise version of the same repositioning. Salesforce's Claudeforce puts Salesforce inside Claude rather than Claude inside Salesforce. That concedes the interface and bets the data and permissions layer was the real moat, though it is still a pilot you cannot buy on a single invoice.

Enterprise Technology ties the loop. Salesforce booked a 2.6 billion dollar mark-to-market gain on its Anthropic stake, in the same stretch that fear of Anthropic eating per-seat software revenue was knocking down MongoDB, Okta and peers. ServiceNow and CrowdStrike are pre-empting that fear themselves, selling contract value and consumption envelopes instead of seats.

Turning to the applied frontier, where two genuine outside checks finally arrived. Robotics and Physical AI has OSHA filings showing more than two dozen injuries to Waymo and Zoox safety drivers from hard-braking events. That is the first mandatory, non-self-reported data that beat has had, and it sits against Figure AI's entirely self-counted Index metrics: all inputs, no outcome.

Space and the Orbital Economy has SpaceX now trading as a public stock, repricing Starship's orbital-deployment attempt every day. That flight is set no earlier than September 15th. Meanwhile AST, Amazon's Leo constellation and China's SpaceSail all sit capital-adequate and launch-constrained.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with the first positive, randomized, prospective result the Biopharma beat has found. Merck and Moderna's individualized melanoma vaccine met its endpoints, though no hazard ratio was released, so the effect size is still unknown. And what it validates is computational neoantigen ranking, not an AI-designed molecule, even as the market repriced Tempus AI, a genomics company that was not in the trial, on a linkage the trial sponsors never mentioned.

One thread worth naming. For most of this week this show has flagged self-published claims with no outside check attached. Today two checks arrived, and both were compelled rather than chosen: OSHA's injury filings, and a randomized Phase 3 readout. Neither party volunteered what it disclosed. That is the difference between evidence and marketing, and today it showed up only where regulators or trial design leave no choice.
