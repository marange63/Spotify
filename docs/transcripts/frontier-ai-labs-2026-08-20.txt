Good morning. It's Thursday, August 20th, and this is your frontier AI labs briefing.

The model leaderboard has been essentially frozen for two weeks now. What moved this week is the scoreboard underneath it: revenue. And it just flipped. Anthropic's annualized revenue run rate surpassed sixty-five billion dollars at the end of July, according to Bloomberg and TechCrunch reporting. OpenAI's run rate is set to top forty billion, per Bloomberg and Semafor. Anthropic is now the larger of the two labs by this measure, and it got there fast.

Trace the climb. Anthropic ended 2025 around nine billion in annualized run rate. By May it was forty-seven billion. By the end of July, sixty-five billion. That's roughly a sevenfold jump in seven months, and Anthropic's own investors expect the pace to hold, projecting the company finishes 2026 somewhere between one hundred and one hundred twenty billion. Meanwhile OpenAI, with the much larger consumer base through ChatGPT, is growing too, but from a lower base and at a slower multiple.

So what happened to the old story, that OpenAI's distribution advantage guarantees it the revenue lead? It's not holding up. Anthropic's mix skews toward enterprise and API customers, and that appears to monetize more efficiently per user than OpenAI's mostly consumer subscriber base. Revenue, not benchmark scores, is now the clearest head-to-head measure between these two companies, and on that measure Anthropic is pulling away.

There's a subtlety in how that forty-billion figure was reported, too. Semafor's own framing around it isn't triumphant. It notes that even as OpenAI's revenue climbs, rivals are fast gaining ground, with customers increasingly turning to Chinese firms like DeepSeek and Moonshot, and with SpaceX and Meta muscling into what it calls the AI elite ranks. So the forty-billion number is a growth story with a crowding-in story attached. Anthropic is the sharpest threat to OpenAI on revenue, but it is not the only one.

That gap is now colliding with the IPO calendar, and this is where the story gets sharper. Anthropic confirmed directly, in its own announcement, that it has confidentially submitted a draft Form S-1 to the SEC for a proposed public offering. Anthropic's own language is careful, though. The company says the number of shares and the price haven't been set, and the offering depends on market conditions and other factors. No date. The Financial Times, as reported by TechCrunch, has floated Anthropic going public as soon as this fall, seeking a valuation of two trillion dollars or more, which would be the largest market debut on record. Treat that fall timing as reported, not company-confirmed.

It's worth sitting with that gap for a second. Anthropic's own filing says nothing about a fall listing, yet the fall timing and the two-trillion figure are out in the market via the FT. The company is letting reported leaks set expectations while its own words stay deliberately non-committal. Read the timeline as the market's, not Anthropic's.

OpenAI, by contrast, looks to be sliding the other direction. Reporting says Sam Altman is holding firm on a valuation target of up to one trillion dollars and told advisers that reducing it was, quote, a non-starter. CFO Sarah Friar has reportedly told associates the company is now aiming for a 2027 listing, not this year. That's a real reversal from where this story stood not long ago, when OpenAI was seen as the one racing toward a near-term, trillion-dollar debut. Corroborating that OpenAI isn't in a hurry to reprice itself: the company completed a seven billion dollar employee tender offer earlier this month at eight hundred fifty-two billion dollars, flat versus its March fundraising round. Insiders aren't marking it up.

And Altman's trillion-dollar floor is not a free stance. Refusing to list below a trillion is effectively a bet that OpenAI's revenue keeps growing at triple-digit rates all the way through 2027. That's a long runway to hold. And every quarter of it is a quarter in which Anthropic's own trajectory can compound further ahead.

So if this holds, Anthropic could reach the public markets first, and whichever lab lists first effectively sets the valuation benchmark the other gets measured against. That's the real stake in the timing gap, not just bragging rights.

Now, a caveat that matters as much as the headline. Run rate is not audited revenue. It's an annualized projection built off a short recent window, and both the sixty-five billion and forty billion figures are vendor-reported, not independently audited numbers. Fast-growing, back-loaded contracts can flatter a run rate more than they would a trailing twelve-month GAAP figure. So hold this lead as directional — Anthropic is clearly ahead and compounding faster — rather than settled arithmetic.

The revenue Anthropic just posted has to be served by hardware, and that's exactly what it locked in next. Anthropic announced an expanded partnership with Google and Broadcom for what the company describes as multiple gigawatts of next-generation TPU capacity, coming online starting in 2027. In its own release, Anthropic frames this as scaling infrastructure to match customer growth. The same release also confirms run-rate revenue past thirty billion earlier this summer, and more than a thousand business customers each spending over a million dollars a year, a customer count that doubled in under two months.

This deepens an odd strategic knot. Google is simultaneously Anthropic's chip supplier for this TPU buildout and a direct competitor to Anthropic's Claude through its own Gemini models. That line between competitor and infrastructure vendor is blurring, not resolving, and it's worth watching whether Google's own model ambitions benefit indirectly from Anthropic's scale.

Now step back to what the financing model means. Layered under this deal, and already reported in recent days, is roughly seventy-one billion dollars in off-balance-sheet chip-lease debt that Anthropic's financing vehicles have stacked up. Think of it as an AI lab funding hyperscaler-scale compute the way a telecom once funded fiber: through special-purpose vehicles and private credit, kept off its own balance sheet. That keeps the depreciation hit off Anthropic's books and lets it scale without diluting equity ahead of an IPO. But it also parks the credit risk with lenders and lease counterparties. If utilization softens, or if that run rate turns out to be more marketed than durable, the loss shows up somewhere in the private-credit system rather than on Anthropic's income statement. That's the kind of hidden leverage worth tracking as these labs walk toward public markets.

Put it together, and the picture is this. Anthropic is winning the revenue scoreboard, may reach the public markets first at a record valuation, and is financing the compute to keep that growth curve intact through a deepening tie to Google and Broadcom. OpenAI, meanwhile, has the larger consumer footprint but a slower revenue climb, a stalled private valuation, and a leadership stance that's pushed its own listing out to 2027. None of this is finished business. Anthropic hasn't named a date, and OpenAI's 2027 target is sourced to reporting, not a filing.

And underneath all of it sits the question this whole race turns on: whether frontier AI ends up a winner-take-most market or a commoditized infrastructure layer. Two labs racing to public markets at record valuations is a bet on the former. The vendor-reported, unaudited nature of the numbers driving those valuations is the reason to keep that bet hedged. Watch for an actual S-1 to go effective, and watch whether OpenAI's delay holds once Anthropic's numbers are public.
