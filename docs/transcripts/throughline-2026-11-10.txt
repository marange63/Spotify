Good morning, this is The Throughline for Tuesday, November 10th, 2026.

Today's headline: Anthropic cut list prices by a fifth and gated its most dual-use model, a week after its own IPO filing warned it may soon hit a self-imposed safety trigger.

Twelve briefings shipped today, running from the macro backdrop through the AI and compute stack to the applied frontier.

Strategic Power has Washington and Beijing both putting their leverage on paper, and both instruments are narrower than they look. The Senate voted 73 to 25 to begin debate on a bill making data centers pay their share of the grid, but the House hasn't acted on this text. Beijing extended its rare-earth suspension by just two months, so it now expires on January 10th, the same day as Washington's own export-rule pause.

From grid leverage to market leverage, Capital Markets Radar reads Monday's best S&P session since April as a relief rally the AI complex didn't lead, running on falling oil and positioning. The deeper signal is breadth, with Goldman's ex-AI index now level with the S&P for the year. And hyperscaler credit spreads are about 50 basis points wider this year, as Meta turns to equity that its chip suppliers may buy.

Digital Money has the test this desk has watched since last Thursday resolving to the bear side. Bitcoin posted its first weekly close below its 50-week average since September, if only marginally. But the treasury-company selling headline mostly doesn't hold up, since most of it happened weeks earlier or not at all, and Strategy, the largest corporate holder, kept buying at breakeven.

Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle has a bank, not a fund, about to put a real price on AI debt. ING is auctioning twelve data-center loans worth about 500 million dollars, with bids due November 19th, as Reuters reports some banks hesitating on new AI deals. Blue Owl's listed BDC priced new loans 40 basis points wider, so the margin is repricing while the back book waits for its first price.

Private Equity has KKR answering Saturday's question, whether Carlyle's guided pullback was one firm's wobble or the sector's direction. KKR guided more than 40 billion dollars of client distributions this year, roughly double its recent run rate, so Carlyle now looks like the outlier. But that figure includes credit and real assets, it's guidance rather than cash, and Apollo calls the retail channel soft, so the thaw is concentrated at the top.

That's the macro backdrop. Now the AI and compute stack, starting with its anchor stock.

Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has the Nvidia board's probe of Jensen Huang getting its first market price, a 4.27 percent drop Monday on a day AMD rose 13 percent and Broadcom nearly 9. So the discount belongs to Nvidia, not the sector, and lands on leadership and Washington standing, not the product. AMD has the best share-shift receipts, with data-center revenue up 97 percent to a record, though that line blends server CPUs with GPUs.

A standing disclosure for the next two desks: Anthropic makes the model behind this show.

Frontier AI Lab Competition carries the headline, as Anthropic cut list prices 20 percent with Claude Fable 5.2 and limited Claude Mythos 5.2 to vetted cyber and life-sciences organizations. That came a week after its amended IPO filing tied a quarterly loss to premium-priced compute, and warned it "may imminently meet" its own threshold for AI-accelerated progress. The desk reads those as two stories for two audiences, as the reported IPO range slides toward one to one-and-a-half trillion dollars.

AI Products and Applications covers the same launch from the builder's side, where access, speed and token efficiency are now separate dials. Fable 5.2's fast mode charges double for up to 2.5 times the speed, and Mythos's eligibility criteria are unpublished. With OpenAI's cyber model gated the same way last week, the desk's read is that for this tier, access policy is the product spec.

Enterprise Technology has AI spend starting to route through cloud commitments rather than fresh vendor contracts. Per a single trade report, ChatGPT Enterprise is now on AWS Marketplace, where eligible spend can draw down customers' existing Amazon commitments. The desk's inference is that hyperscalers, not labs, may become the gatekeepers of what enterprises buy.

That's the compute stack. Now the applied frontier, where field reliability counts for more than model rank.

Robotics and Physical AI has Nvidia launching GR00T N2, a robot foundation model whose partners run from ABB to Figure, though a partner isn't an adopter. The sharper evidence came from AgiBot, whose six humanoids ran a Shanghai convenience store for 24 hours, with only four passing AgiBot's own performance assessment. Those robots ran AgiBot's own model stack, not GR00T N2, and a leaderboard measures the brain while a store measures the fleet.

Space and the Orbital Economy has Rocket Lab booking over 515 million dollars of Neutron contracts at a higher price, for a rocket still dated only "no earlier than 2027." Buyers are paying for scarcity on paper, while China's state-linked programs keep turning launch cadence into deployed satellites.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with argenx buying Unnatural Products outright for up to 1.15 billion dollars, just over half of it contingent. That's a drugmaker owning an AI-assisted discovery engine rather than renting one, though argenx was already a minority holder and two-year partner, and has named no programs. The same day, Pierre Fabre took the renting path, signing its second AI design partnership of the year, with XtalPi.

One thread ran through three desks today: markets putting first prices on AI risks they had only modeled. Nvidia's governance risk got its mark on Monday. And around November 19th, ING's loan bids and Anthropic's reportedly slated IPO could set the first public prices on AI data-center debt and a frontier lab.

That's The Throughline. Talk to you tomorrow.
