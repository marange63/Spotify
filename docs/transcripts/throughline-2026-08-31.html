Good morning, this is The Throughline for Monday, August 31st, 2026. Today's headline: across the AI stack, the fight has stopped being about who has the best model, and become a fight over who owns the surface the customer actually touches. Three separate beats caught that happening today, in a supply cutoff, a default setting inside Slack, and a giveaway license out of China.

Start with the macro backdrop. Strategic Power has the reversal of the year on gas. The United States now has twice as much gas-fired capacity in development as China, and the slice tied directly to data centers nearly doubled in six months, to 189 gigawatts. But only about nine percent of that data-center pipeline has actually moved into construction. Turbine lead times now run three years or more, so the binding constraint is a factory floor, not a permit. On the compute side, Nvidia's licensed H200 sales into China came in under one percent of data-center revenue. That is the first hard number behind the argument that Beijing, not Washington, decides how much actually clears. It is still an inference, not testimony. No Chinese official has confirmed a motive, and unattractive tariff economics would fit the same number.

Capital Markets Radar picked up Friday's hawkish Warsh repricing and found it hasn't bitten yet. Private credit is funding itself more cheaply than before Jackson Hole, with Blue Owl and Blackstone vehicles both clearing at multiples of target, even as median non-accruals across the twenty largest BDCs hit 2.8 percent, roughly double the 2023 baseline. Funding costs are falling while the assets underneath them quietly deteriorate. Spot bitcoin ETFs did break a nine-day inflow streak, but 202 million dollars of outflows against a 55 billion dollar cumulative base is two-tenths of one percent, and Ethereum ETFs kept taking in cash the same day. That is rotation inside crypto, not a flight from it.

Digital Money took the other side of that same rate story. Circle's second-quarter release shows reserve income up five percent while average USDC in circulation grew twenty-five percent, which is the decoupling this show had been arguing without proof. But that compression is backward-looking, from cuts already delivered, and a genuinely hawkish Fed reverses it. The structural move is regulatory: the OCC says 23 of the 40 new bank charter applications it has received carry a digital-asset business plan, an eightfold jump, all chasing the same float income Circle just showed is rate-dependent.

Private Equity has the one live deal where nobody controls the price yet. Silver Lake's talks to take Workday private, market-implied in the mid-forty-billions rather than priced in any term sheet, are still unconfirmed two weeks on. But Workday's board just authorized a four-billion-dollar buyback against only 3.4 billion of liquid assets, so it has to be funded out of cash flow over a year or more. Workday hasn't characterized it, and the mechanism is what matters: a target leaning on its own cash flow to hold up the reference price a sponsor would have to beat. Underneath that, EQT's pending purchase of the secondaries firm Coller Capital is a check that only amortizes if the industry's exit logjam lasts years.

That question of who sets the price carries straight into the AI and compute stack. Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has Samsung closing the HBM4 yield gap on SK hynix and guiding HBM4 revenue to more than triple sequentially this quarter. But analysts, not company guidance, now project HBM contract prices up more than fifty percent in 2027, with UBS nearer seventy-nine percent. So a share shift inside the memory duopoly is not buying anyone relief on price, and AMD's memory-heavy MI455X is negotiating from behind on the one input that increasingly sets system cost.

Frontier AI Lab Competition has the cleanest version of today's headline. OpenAI is terminating model supply to Cursor on November 12th, after SpaceX's acquisition of its maker triggered a change-of-control clause. But Cursor's own figure puts OpenAI models at about five percent of its traffic, which reads as the limit of OpenAI's leverage rather than a demonstration of it. Anthropic made the mirror-image bet the same week, striking Claudeforce with Salesforce to become the default reasoning model across Agentforce, Slack and the CRM stack, with no dollar figure attached to it yet.

AI Products and Applications has the discount version of that same fight. Z.ai's GLM-5.3-Flash shipped under a plain, unmodified MIT license at roughly a fortieth of OpenAI's output-token rate. The license is the part that cannot expire; the hosted price is a vendor's promise like any other, and the performance claims are still unreplicated by anyone outside the company.

Enterprise Technology confirms the Salesforce side of it directly. Claude is now the default model inside Slack, not an option you switch on. Salesforce's own leadership framed being bypassed as something that increases Salesforce's value, which is what a vendor says once it has lost the argument over where the interface lives. Nobody yet knows what a seat is worth when the seat stops opening the product.

Into the applied frontier, Robotics and Physical AI found the same commoditization argument at the model layer for machines. Skild AI says its S1 model learns a new task from a single human video, and that one video is worth roughly 380 teleoperated demonstrations. That would undercut the teleoperation-data moat Figure AI is spending over a billion dollars to build. But it rests on one blog post and an internal evaluation against Skild's own unnamed baseline.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes with the one dataset that has aged well. Insilico Medicine posted a real profit, earned on licensing margins rather than on any approved drug, and its AI-designed lung-fibrosis candidate has entered a China-only Phase III on the back of a peer-reviewed Phase 2a signal. But across 117 tracked AI-enabled assets, Phase II success still sits near forty percent, statistically indistinguishable from the industry baseline. AI has bought down the cheap failures, not the expensive ones.

One thread ties the day together, and it is an escalation of the inference this show drew yesterday: that once capability stops commanding the price, the contest moves to distribution. Today it stopped being an inference. OpenAI enforced a distribution cutoff. Anthropic bought a default slot inside Salesforce and Slack. Z.ai gave the weights away to make distribution the only thing left to compete on. And a robotics upstart claimed the same prize before anyone could check its math. The pattern is showing up in signed terms now, not in earnings-call language.
