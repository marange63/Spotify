Good morning. It's Wednesday, October 28th, and this is your frontier AI labs briefing.

One disclosure up front. Anthropic makes the model behind this show, and today's lead is Anthropic's own compute financing.

Here's the thesis. In the space of one week, both leading standalone labs moved part of their compute off a Nvidia-only path. Anthropic did it through a specific, financed deal. OpenAI did it through a reported, unconfirmed one. Yesterday this desk said compute, not distribution, is where the leverage sits. That still holds, but it needs an update. Yesterday's compute counterparty was SpaceX, which owns capacity. Today's counterparties are Broadcom and Google, which design the silicon. So the leverage is going to whoever can design the chip and get it financed, not just whoever owns the racks.

First, a quick bridge from Anthropic's IPO filing, since most of its numbers already ran here this week. Two figures are new. Anthropic's operating loss for the first half was 693 million dollars. The filing's headline net loss for the same half is far larger, at 80.7 billion dollars. But most of that gap is a non-cash accounting charge, from remeasuring convertible notes and other financing instruments, not cash burn. So the operating line is the better read on the business. The second new figure is concentration. For the nine months through September, no single customer accounted for more than 10 percent of revenue. On Sunday, working from the leaked draft, we said two customers contributed about a fifth of first-half revenue. Those reads aren't in conflict. Two customers each just under 10 percent can still add up to roughly a fifth. So what matters here isn't the growth story, which is familiar. It's that this is the business Wall Street just agreed to lend against.

Because on the same day Anthropic filed that S-1, October 26th, Broadcom arranged a 63 billion dollar package to finance chips for Anthropic. Bloomberg named the four firms alongside it: Bank of America, Blackstone, Citigroup, and Morgan Stanley. Follow-up reporting fills in the structure. A newly formed special-purpose vehicle buys Broadcom's AI accelerators and networking equipment. Per that reporting, the vehicle then leases the hardware to Anthropic. The banks have underwritten the senior secured tranche. Its size depends on the source. Bloomberg puts it at 42 billion dollars, while Reuters-sourced coverage citing two people familiar with the matter says about 43.5 billion. The companies haven't disclosed the breakdown. Beneath that, Blackstone provides the committed portion of the second-lien financing. First deliveries are expected to begin in 2027.

The part still exposed to market pricing is the bond leg. Banks plan to sell as much as about 21 billion dollars of high-grade bonds, as soon as early next year. That's up to half of the senior lending, refinanced in the first quarter of 2027. The bonds will probably mature in around five years. And the final size and timing, per the reporting, will depend on market conditions. Nothing has been priced.

Don't read this as 63 billion dollars of new demand. It's a financing wrapper, and the real question is who holds the risk. Banks underwriting the senior debt tells you about their appetite at closing. It doesn't tell you whether the bond market takes the paper later. CoreWeave's bonds trading near 15 percent, as our Throughline noted Monday, show what it looks like when that market balks. Then there's Broadcom's own role. Coverage of the deal describes Broadcom providing residual value support, a backstop for lenders against the hardware losing value. The precise terms of that backstop aren't in the reporting we can quote. But even on the confirmed facts, the chip vendor is arranging the financing for its own customer's purchase. That's vendor financing. It's the pattern this week's coverage flagged in Nvidia prepaying its suppliers and voicing support for CoreWeave. Now it has reached a frontier lab.

That leaves two honest readings for Anthropic. The bullish one is a real second compute path outside Nvidia, financed at Wall Street scale. The bearish one is a fixed lease obligation stacked on the large noncancelable compute book the leaked draft showed. Which reading wins depends on lease terms that still aren't public. Even the accounting is undisclosed. No Anthropic or Broadcom statement says whether this sits on the balance sheet as a lease obligation or off it, inside the vehicle. And a five-year bond against chips that start shipping in 2027 puts one question at the center: what that hardware is worth when the paper comes due.

Step back, and this is a new rung on a ladder this desk has tracked all week. It started with CoreWeave's bonds, Oracle's swaps, and Meta's default protection hitting a record. On Friday the 23rd, we noted the junior slice of Anthropic's compute financing was paying a 10 percent coupon. Lenders were already charging Anthropic more. Then came equity, through the S-1. Now the chip vendor itself is arranging the loan. So what: chip vendors are now helping underwrite their own demand, and that is a risk to track, not just a sale to celebrate.

For Nvidia, our read is pressure at the margin, not displacement. Two leading labs adding non-Nvidia paths in one week weakens Nvidia's hand in the next pricing negotiation. But Nvidia's book isn't being replaced, and the next story shows why.

Now the other leading lab, moving the same direction with far less on paper. OpenAI is reported to be renting, or possibly planning to buy, Google TPU capacity. The reports disagree on which. Either way, OpenAI keeps its commitment to spend roughly 600 billion dollars on Nvidia server infrastructure by 2030. According to Data Center Dynamics, financial terms and chip volumes remain undisclosed, and neither company has publicly confirmed a deal. Treat this as reported direction, not fact. There's also a competing explanation. Against that Nvidia commitment, a TPU arrangement of unknown size could be leverage in OpenAI's Nvidia negotiations rather than a genuine migration. One line on the disclosure gap: Anthropic's compute financing is visible down to the tranche, while OpenAI's TPU terms aren't visible at all.

OpenAI also shipped GPT-6.1 Terra on Monday, which it positions as a cheaper, faster model for computer use and coding agents. It's rolling out in ChatGPT and Codex, and in the API. The price and speed claims are OpenAI's own and unverified, so take the positioning, not the numbers. The so what: OpenAI is pushing cost down on agentic workloads at the same moment it diversifies the silicon underneath them. Cheaper chips and cheaper tokens are one strategy, seen from two ends.

That TPU report matters even more for Google. Yesterday's frame was that Google resells OpenAI's models on Google Cloud. Shelf space is cheap, we said, and compute is scarce. If the TPU reports hold, Google is now selling OpenAI the scarce thing too. That's a stronger position than yesterday's framing allowed. Google would earn on both sides, from Gemini and from renting silicon to the lab Gemini competes with. There's a catch worth naming. Yesterday we flagged the incentive risk in Anthropic buying compute from SpaceX, which owns xAI. The same logic now points at Google. A supplier renting capacity to a rival learns how much that rival needs, and sits across the table at renewal. So what: on the winner-take-most question, owning scarce silicon and renting it to rivals is the strongest seat in the market, and this week Google moved closer to it.

Google also had the week's only fresh capability release from a frontier lab. DeepMind unveiled Genie 4, a real-time interactive world model. In DeepMind's words, it's its first real-time world model to generate audio natively. Access is limited to trusted testers, with no independent evaluation, so the realism claims are DeepMind's own for now. Still, it's a bet in a different lane from chatbots, one that could feed robotics simulation and synthetic training data. Neither OpenAI nor Anthropic has announced anything comparable. So on this week's evidence, Google gained ground on several separate dimensions at once.

Scoring it by dimension. On chip access, Anthropic diversified, and OpenAI is reportedly diversifying. On ecosystem control, Google and Broadcom both strengthened, as the designers of silicon their customers can't easily replace. On capital access, Wall Street underwriting is open for now, but the bond test is still pending. On inference cost, OpenAI is pushing down, on its own numbers. On raw capability, Genie 4 is unverified, and no lab made a verified frontier-model move this week.

Three things to watch. First, whether that roughly 21 billion dollar bond leg prices, and how its yield compares with CoreWeave's paper. Second, whether OpenAI or Google ever confirm actual TPU volumes. Third, whether Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro open weights, promised for November 4th, actually land.
