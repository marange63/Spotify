Transcript · September 16, 2026

Good morning, this is The Throughline for Wednesday, September 16th, 2026. Today's headline: the ten-year Treasury yield touched 5.0266 percent on Tuesday, the highest print since July of 2007, hours before a Fed decision that four of today's briefings pointedly refuse to call.

Start with the macro backdrop. Strategic Power has the first hard number behind an argument this beat has been making for weeks. PJM's market monitor attributes 6.2 billion dollars of cleared capacity-auction cost to data centers that have not been built, out of 16.4 billion in total, though that split is secondary reporting rather than PJM's own published report. Real money is changing hands today against a forecast, and nobody has found a primary answer for who pays if the load never shows up.

Capital Markets Cross-Asset Radar leads on that yield, and it leads carefully. The breach was an intraday touch, not a settled close, and futures on Tuesday priced a 92 percent chance of a quarter-point hike this afternoon. The sharper tell is gold, which fell to roughly 4,296 dollars an ounce with its war premium still intact — the hedge failed in exactly the conditions it was bought for.

Digital Money has the casualty of a different Washington vote. The Senate's cloture motion on crypto market-structure legislation failed 49 to 50 on Tuesday, and Coinbase and Circle together shed 7.9 billion dollars of market value in a session. That is three to four times the move in Bitcoin itself, which says these are short-duration, rate-sensitive stablecoin-reserve businesses rather than leveraged Bitcoin bets, and it is why this afternoon's Fed decision matters to them more than Congress does.

Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle has Fortress co-CEO Jack Neumark warning fellow lenders to be careful on AI data-center debt. Fitch's record 6.3 percent default rate on private credit borrowers turns out to be built mostly on maturity extensions rather than actual payment failure. And one nontraded fund, Cliffwater, took second-quarter redemption requests equal to 17 percent of shares against a cap of roughly 5 percent a quarter. Stress is showing up as extensions and exit queues, not yet as missed payments.

Private Equity and the Deal Economy has Apollo in talks to buy Johnson and Johnson's DePuy Synthes orthopedics unit for close to 20 billion dollars. It would be the largest healthcare buyout of this cycle, negotiated while Apollo's own flagship fund sits about halfway to target. Exits and fundraising are both narrowing: the twenty largest funds took more than half of all capital raised in the first half, and Thoma Bravo has agreed to take Accelerant private barely a year after it listed.

Now into the AI and compute stack, where this week's scarcity story moved from silicon to inventory. Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has Samsung and SK hynix finished-goods memory below ten days of supply, per trade press rather than company disclosure. That is the first physical-stock evidence behind a shortage that has lived in margins and guidance until now. HBM takes roughly three times the wafer input of DDR5 per Micron's own architecture fellow, so commodity DRAM is being crowded out, and that allocation only gets more extreme through 2027.

Frontier AI Lab Competition has OpenAI's Chris Lehane confirming weeks of safety talks with Anthropic and Google DeepMind. OpenAI says it doesn't think it needs an antitrust waiver to hold them, a more confident position than Anthropic's own request for one. But the pact cannot reach the party setting price per unit of quality: one evaluator found DeepSeek's V4.1-Flash at 98 percent of GPT-6 Astra's design quality for 1.4 percent of the cost.

AI Products and Applications has three vendors trading readiness for reach. Apple shipped a rebuilt Siri as an English-only beta, blocked in the EU. Salesforce rolled out agents claiming multi-week memory with no customer numbers at all, and OpenAI quietly stopped approving ChatGPT ads for standalone image and audio tools that compete with its own features.

Enterprise Technology closes that leg with a correction and a ladder. Monday's cybersecurity rally actually traded on a week-old Zscaler conference remark, not on fresh news. And ServiceNow's disclosed billion dollars of AI contract value dwarfs Zscaler's hundred-million-dollar bookings figure by an order of magnitude — the unit a vendor chooses to measure in is itself the signal.

Out at the applied frontier, machines and money are running on different clocks. Robotics and Physical AI has Agility Robotics unveiling Digit 5, a cageless humanoid with a ten-to-one battery run-to-charge ratio. Its 300-million-dollar order book turns out to be a single conditioned contract with a related party, while UBTech has opened a Chinese factory nameplated at 10,000 humanoids a year against order books nowhere near that size.

Space and the Orbital Economy has the Air Force acknowledging publicly, for the first time, that the US has space-control weapons on orbit. That is a deliberate trade of ambiguity for deterrence by declaration. Starship's Flight 14 slipped again to September 22nd with no catch attempt this time, and China's LandSpace flew ten Qianfan satellites on a commercially developed rocket, the first time that constellation has flown on anything but a state vehicle.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with Tempus AI committing to link 100,000 whole genomes to clinical outcomes, with the platform generally available mid-2027. The only real clinical readout of the day is Recursion's: a 43 percent median reduction in polyp burden, in a trial of six patients.

One thread is worth naming. Capital Markets Radar, Digital Money, Private Credit and Private Equity all explicitly declined to call this afternoon's Fed decision before it happens. Each still found real exposure already built up around it — a yield breach, a stablecoin repricing, a redemption queue, a buyout's financing cost. The vote hasn't happened yet, and the market has already spent the week positioning as if it knows the answer.

That's The Throughline. Have a good day.
