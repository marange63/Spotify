Good morning, this is The Throughline for Wednesday, August 26th, 2026. Today's headline: Nvidia reports after the close tonight. It has become the one print several of today's briefings are waiting on. It tests an AI lab's own inference chip, a record equity tape, and credit spreads sitting at the rich end of their own history.

Start with the macro backdrop, where authority keeps getting held rather than spent.

Strategic Power leads with a deadline. Fed Governor Lisa Cook has until today to respond to a renewed removal attempt. That response is the last procedural box the Supreme Court required before any termination could take effect. Neither side has confirmed a move. The same briefing has grid operator PJM's market monitor pricing the AI buildout. Data centers accounted for forty-six percent of capacity charges across PJM's last four auctions. That is specific enough to start driving state-level cost bills.

Capital Markets Radar carries the rates story forward and adds the oil side. Brent has given back its entire Iran-driven rally. Washington's latest sanctions package stopped short of hitting Iran's trading partners. But the war and the Strait of Hormuz constraint have not changed at all, so the premium came out on probability, not physics. Falling crude and falling long yields now point the same disinflationary way for the first time in weeks. That turns September's Fed debate into a growth and labor argument. It also loads the weight onto Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole speech as Fed chair, on Friday. Meanwhile high-yield spreads sit in the richest decile of that index's history. Equity desks have paid real money to hedge a semiconductor concentration shock. Credit has not priced it at all.

Digital Money has the flow side of the same liquidity story. Spot bitcoin ETFs have strung together six straight days of inflows worth about two point two billion dollars. That makes August the strongest flow month of the year, with category assets just under a hundred billion. But the milestone is partly reflexive. Much of that asset growth is bitcoin's price marking up units already outstanding. The same briefing found three separately published figures for August's biggest liquidation event, ranging from one point seven billion to almost three billion. A dispersion that wide undercuts every leverage statistic built on top of it. The one piece on a fixed clock is regulatory. The OCC says it intends to finalize stablecoin licensing rules by November, leaving about ten weeks before a January 2027 issuance ban bites.

Private Equity and the Deal Economy shows where liquidity now actually lives. GP-led secondaries volume grew thirty-five percent in the first half. The LP-led channel, the one allocators use to exit on their own terms, grew four percent. Single-asset continuation vehicles are up eighty-eight percent. They price at or above par, while most of the multi-asset book clears below it. Liquidity is increasingly something the general partner manufactures for itself.

Now the AI and compute stack, where held-versus-exercised turns into claim-versus-proof.

Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has OpenAI publishing its first benchmark for Jalapeño, its Broadcom-designed inference chip. The claim is up to one point nine times more inference work per watt than an Nvidia Blackwell system. Read that carefully. It is OpenAI's own result, on a benchmark OpenAI chose. The chip is reportedly still at engineering-sample status, while Nvidia's Rubin parts already ship to customers. The same briefing has TSMC outsourcing roughly a quarter-million wafers a year of advanced packaging. That relieves one bottleneck by creating a new dependency. Tonight's print is the first hard test of whether the efficiency claim bites into Nvidia's inference pricing power.

AI Products and Applications has both OpenAI and Google discounting flagship models on named expiry dates. OpenAI's cut runs through November 21st. Google's Gemini pricing runs through January 1st. That is a subsidy for agentic coding workloads with a scheduled end, not a durable price war. The more consequential move is plumbing. Anthropic shipped enterprise-managed authorization for its agent connectors. It clears the procurement and security gates that were actually blocking deployment, ahead of the neutral standards body meant to own that layer.

Enterprise Technology shows what that repricing anxiety looks like in public markets. A report says Anthropic is preparing to file for a share sale that could rival SpaceX's seventy-five billion dollar offering. That knocked MongoDB, Cloudflare, Okta and Zscaler down four to five percent in a single afternoon. It was a forecast about what a filing might disclose, not anything disclosed yet. No public data shows Claude displacing the per-seat software spend those four companies live on. CrowdStrike and Salesforce report after the close tonight, the first real test of whether the fear shows up in results.

Turning to the applied frontier, and the least provable layer of the whole stack.

AI in Biopharma R&D has Genentech exercising its option on an AI-derived neuroscience target from Recursion. That is the first time this beat has seen a partner pay for target selection rather than molecule design. But the payment was three million dollars, against a hundred and thirty-one million dollar quarterly loss. The target layer got priced, not funded.

One thread ties the day together. Yesterday this show closed on two scheduled tests: Nvidia's print and Warsh at Jackson Hole on Friday. Today that list grew. Cook's response is due today. The OCC's stablecoin rule is promised for November. And Genentech may or may not exercise a second option. None of today's eight briefings settled anything on its own. All eight pointed at a date still to come.

That's The Throughline. Have a good Wednesday.
