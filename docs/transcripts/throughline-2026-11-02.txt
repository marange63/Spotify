That's the macro backdrop. Now the AI and compute stack, where the model is fading behind whoever controls the product.

Enterprise Technology finds the market grading the forward commitment, not the quarter just delivered. Now the applied frontier, where capital is chasing the bottleneck rather than the headline product.

And Eli Lilly's R&D spend rose 23 percent last quarter, slower than its 39 percent revenue growth, with nothing in that figure attributed to AI.
