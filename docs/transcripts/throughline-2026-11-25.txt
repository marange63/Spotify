Today's headline: on Monday, Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive, and Nvidia alone supplied about a third of the S&P 500's gain that day. Huang's return restored leadership continuity, but the questions about Nvidia's accounting remain open.

We'll start with the macro backdrop. Strategic Power finds PJM's market monitor tying almost 10 dollars a megawatt-hour of this year's average wholesale price to data-center load, against a 32-dollar rise from a year earlier. The flagship new reactor built to serve a data center, Google's Hermes 2, doesn't target first power until 2030. So this decade's load falls to gas and existing reactors, not new nuclear.

From the grid to the tape, Capital Markets Radar finds Monday's rally was concentration dressed up as breadth, with the equal-weight S&P rising only about half as much as the cap-weighted index. On Tuesday, Nvidia gave back about 6 percent and the index slipped, while equal-weight funds closed modestly higher. The desk's so-what is that one company's governance risk has become index risk, while credit and the uranium miners are still de-risking underneath the bounce.

At the speculative end, Digital Money has spot bitcoin ETFs passing June's record for monthly outflows, at about 4.7 billion dollars on one tracker's count. Long-term holders are realizing losses at the heaviest pace since the FTX collapse, but Glassnode, the firm measuring it, calls this the first capitulation of the bear market, not a bottom. With futures leverage at its lowest since early March, the desk expects spot selling rather than forced liquidations to set the next move.

In lending, Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle has JPMorgan forecasting leveraged loan defaults roughly doubling, to 4.5 percent in 2027, as a maturity wall that's nearly half weak credit approaches. In the private book, Houlihan Lokey's stress test shows that a 100 basis point rise in base rates would push almost one borrower in five below break-even interest coverage. So the desk's open risk isn't today's default print, but a performing book still carried at model marks.

AI Products and Applications shows governance moving into the product itself. That breaks a pattern this show has tracked since mid-November, of agent launches keeping a human checkpoint, and it moves the real control point to whoever writes the policy file.

Enterprise Technology finds AI finally showing up as counted dollars, though not yet enough to set a stock price. That's the compute stack. Now the applied frontier, where filed numbers keep outrunning capacity claims.

Robotics and Physical AI has Symbotic posting its first full year of GAAP profit, with a backlog worth roughly eight years of revenue.
