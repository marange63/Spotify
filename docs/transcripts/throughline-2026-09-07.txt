Good morning, this is The Throughline for Monday, September 7th, 2026. Today's headline: Iran's Revolutionary Guard fired ballistic missiles at a US carrier and a destroyer in the Gulf on Saturday, the US struck back at three Iranian oil tankers, and the market opens this morning with almost no verified data on what any of it is worth.

Start with the macro backdrop. Strategic Power stays away from the Gulf and finds three other gates instead. ERCOT's Texas interconnection queue, headlined at 474 gigawatts, turns out to be a batch-study methodology change rather than a freeze. Trade-press tracking finds barely six gigawatts of that queue actually energized. That makes it a waiting list, not a demand forecast. Trump envoys also spent the weekend in Moscow and Kyiv without producing any disclosed peace terms.

Strategic Power's third gate is oil, and it hands straight to the day's biggest unknown. OPEC-plus is still withholding roughly two million barrels a day, a cushion this show had wrongly counted as spent. That is exactly the kind of number Capital Markets Cross-Asset Radar cannot get for the carrier attack itself. The last oil settle it can source predates the weekend. Hormuz transit counts are a week stale, and war-risk insurance pricing is seven weeks old. So this morning's tape is trading a headline with no confirming evidence behind it. What is confirmed is the calendar. The escalation lands on a Fed already leaning hawkish after Friday's blowout August payrolls report. That frees Chair Warsh to tighten into resilience rather than split his mandate. And both the VIX and high-yield spreads were marked for calm before any of this happened.

Digital Money has its own count-the-votes problem. The Senate's crypto market-structure bill faces a cloture vote on September 15th. Polymarket's contract on that bill passing has collapsed from 82 percent in February to 16 percent. But that price rests on a whip count six weeks old, and no senator has gone on record since the recess started. Meanwhile bitcoin's recovery back near 80,000 dollars is led by short covering, with futures open interest falling. That is a deleveraging bounce, not fresh conviction. Less leverage on the pile means a smaller move around the vote than the positioning chatter implies.

Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle has a rotation, not a retreat. New money into non-traded BDCs fell 82 percent year over year, while Blackstone's flagship fund gated redemptions for a second straight quarter. But institutional closed-end direct lending just posted its best quarter in two years. The retail wrapper and the institutional channel are moving in opposite directions, at exactly the moment private credit positions itself to fund hundreds of billions of AI data-center debt.

Private Equity and the Deal Economy has the same capital rotating toward liquidity rather than new deals. CVC closed its sixth secondaries fund at ten billion dollars. That is the firm's largest ever, nearly double its predecessor, and roughly half of it came from investors new to these funds. Limited partners are switching from funding new buyouts to buying existing fund stakes secondhand. The catch is that a fund close and a change of owner still are not the cash distributions investors are actually short of.

Now into the AI and compute stack, where a second supplier has arrived promising to reprice it. Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has AMD's rack-scale Helios system in what AMD calls full production. AMD claims up to 30 percent more tokens per dollar than Nvidia's Rubin rack. The independent read on that is blunt. The figure comes from AMD's own labs on a single workload, and customers should not expect to see it in practice. The harder data point is Nvidia's, and even that is reported rather than confirmed by the company. Nvidia will ship no new gaming GPU architecture at all in 2026, breaking a streak that runs back to 1999. The reason is memory, funneled instead to AI accelerators. So a second gigawatt-scale bidder does not create more HBM4. It raises the price Samsung, SK hynix and Micron can charge everyone.

AI Products and Applications has capability moving the opposite way, toward being given away. The Institute of Foundation Models released a six-model open family, with weights, code, and as much of the training data as its source licenses allowed. An independent tester then ran the smaller models and got broken output on a basic coding task. Call it a real but partial win for auditability, not a proof of capability.

Enterprise Technology has the market rewarding the unverifiable over the proven. Okta's stock jumped 29 percent on an AI-agent identity narrative its own finance chief called immaterial. Figma sold off despite disclosing weekly AI usage across more than 80 percent of its largest paid customers. Investors are paying for the story that is still too small to check.

Turning to the applied frontier, where the same measurement gap is physical. Robotics and Physical AI has Tesla's robotaxi fleet past one million unsupervised miles. But nobody outside Tesla knows the fleet size, and crowdsourced trackers disagree by more than two to one. And no operator in this sector, Tesla or Waymo, publishes an incident rate anyone outside the company can audit.

Space and the Orbital Economy has AST SpaceMobile with genuinely deployed direct-to-cell hardware and a testable promise attached. Its reaffirmed full-year guidance needs the back half to run two to three times the first half, in under four months. The sharper point on that beat is regulatory. When the FCC waived Amazon's Kuiper deployment deadline back in June, it demoted the late satellites in spectrum priority instead of fining anyone. That turns a rival's launch schedule into a competitive weapon.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with the denominator this beat had been missing. Of 117 AI-enabled drug candidates counted in human trials, only eight had completed Phase 2, the stage that tests whether the underlying biology was right. That tally comes from a conference abstract with a December cutoff, so it is nine months stale. Alongside it, Genentech paid a three-million-dollar option milestone against a Recursion collaboration whose disclosed ceiling runs past twelve billion.

Now the close. For three days running this show has said a version of the same thing. The AI and infrastructure boom rests on numbers nobody outside the company has checked. What changed today is that the unchecked numbers now have dates attached. PPI on Thursday, CPI on Friday, the cloture vote on the 15th, the Fed on the 16th. The money has moved well ahead of the evidence, and this is the week the evidence starts arriving.

That's The Throughline. Have a good week ahead.
