Good morning. This is The Throughline for Tuesday, September first, 2026. Today's headline: US strikes on Iranian assets in the Strait of Hormuz put a geopolitical risk premium back into oil and bonds in a single session, and it lands on a Fed chair who has argued the inflation problem is broad rather than narrow.

Start with the macro backdrop, and with the other authority moving markets today. Strategic Power has Texas Governor Greg Abbott pausing new data-center interconnection approvals in the ERCOT queue, pending an audit. The directive has no published end date and two carve-outs, for counties outside ERCOT's footprint and for behind-the-meter projects. So the real target is narrower than the headline four-hundred-seventy-four-gigawatt queue suggests. It is the most advanced projects, about two hundred gigawatts' worth. The number to carry is downstream of that. The Energy Information Administration cut its Texas 2027 load-growth forecast from fourteen percent to six. On this beat, that is the first official AI-power demand forecast to move down instead of up.

From a state rationing demand, to a market repricing a supply threat. Capital Markets Radar has Brent crude briefly above ninety dollars, after US forces struck Iranian assets preparing to mine Hormuz. The beat is careful about what actually came back: a threat premium, not an interrupted barrel. That collided with Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole case that fifty-four percent of the PCE basket is running above three percent. A chair arguing from breadth has less room to wave off an energy shock. The ten-year Treasury yield topped four-point-seven-five percent, its highest since January 2025. But gold and the dollar both refused to confirm that hawkish move. The beat's label is unresolved, and the trade is volatility rather than direction.

Digital Money takes the same rate question from the crypto side, and finds a change in who is buying. Strategy disclosed its first bitcoin purchase in ten weeks, 4,603 coins for about 370 million dollars. It funded that with fresh equity rather than debt, out of a raise of roughly 600 million. Another 200 million of the same raise went to defending its preferred stack. The second-order point is the one to bank. An equity-funded bid expands when sentiment is strong and contracts exactly when coins are cheapest. The marginal corporate bid has gone from always-on to pro-cyclical.

Private Credit has the collateral sitting underneath all of this. Blue Owl and PIMCO led a two-point-four-billion-dollar deal for cloud provider IREN at a nine-percent coupon, funding Nvidia GPUs. The beat calls that the cleanest quoted price yet on what AI-infrastructure collateral is worth. It also flags what the loan is not. The security is described only as senior secured, and IREN's one disclosed offtake contract sits at a different campus entirely. Then the harder data point. Recovery on First Brands' debtor-in-possession loan, the most protected claim in that structure, came back at 194 million dollars against 1.1 billion borrowed. Treat that as reported and incomplete, not a final rate. It is still the first live test of recovery assumptions the whole industry marks against.

Now into the AI and compute stack, where the same disclosure problem repeats. Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has a single analyst note claiming Intel's 18A yield jumped to 85 percent. That would clear Samsung and the threshold big customers reportedly want. Nobody at Intel, TSMC, or Samsung has confirmed it, and the same note's design orders from six major firms are unconfirmed too. Carry the beat's caution with it. Even a perfect 18A changes who you can negotiate with, not how many accelerators get finished, because packaging and memory are the actual queue.

Frontier AI Lab Competition and AI Products both covered the same fact, and they disagree on how solid it is. Anthropic scrapped a scheduled fifty-percent price increase on Claude Sonnet 5 and locked the launch rate in as standard. AI Products cites Anthropic's own pricing documentation. Frontier AI Labs went looking for that confirmation and found only a post on X, with no newsroom or pricing-page language behind it. Same company, same decision, two different levels of proof. Frontier AI Labs also has Alibaba open-weighting a real flagship model, and xAI's Grok 4.6 renting space inside Copilot, Vertex, and Foundry rather than fighting to own a surface.

Enterprise Technology has the opposite bet on pricing. GitHub is hardening the Copilot seat while everyone else abandons seats. From today, newly assigned seats require payment before access. The forced upfront charge on the installed base starts October first, so watch October's numbers rather than September's. The beat also does its own arithmetic on Salesforce, landing near a quarter of a million dollars per paying Agentforce customer. That is a platform number rather than a feature attach, if the customer count holds up.

Turning to the applied frontier, where the measuring problem is sharpest. Robotics and Physical AI sets Symbotic's audited 56 warehouse systems actually earning revenue against AgiBot's self-reported 8,400 humanoid shipments. Same end market, two incompatible units of account, priced in separate markets with no way to arbitrage the gap shut.

Space and the Orbital Economy has money and concrete arriving ahead of flights. Rocket Lab's record backlog is written mostly against Neutron, which has never left the ground. Blue Origin announced a second Cape pad while its only existing one has no confirmed return to flight. And AST SpaceMobile has built through satellite forty-two against thirteen launched, with the rest grounded on somebody else's manifest.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with the first real data point on an eight-year-old thesis. Genentech optioned its first target out of Recursion's cell-image atlas, one conversion out of a possible forty, five years into the deal. Meanwhile Isomorphic Labs' two-point-one-billion-dollar round sits at the design layer, against a field-wide approval count still at zero.

One thread to carry out of today. Two of this show's own beats looked at the identical Anthropic pricing claim and landed in different places on how confirmed it is. That is not a contradiction to resolve by picking a side. It is a reminder that permanent and confirmed are different words. That goes for Texas's queue, Intel's yield, and IREN's collateral too.
