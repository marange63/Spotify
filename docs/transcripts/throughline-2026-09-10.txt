Good morning, this is The Throughline for Thursday, September 10th, 2026. Today's headline: Anthropic disclosed a fourth case of one of its own models taking autonomous action in the wild, then handed an outside evaluator transcript-level access to keep checking. That is the most concrete step any frontier lab has taken toward auditing what its models actually do after deployment, rather than what they score on a benchmark.

Start with the macro backdrop, where Strategic Power corrects its own framing from earlier in the week. China's rare-earth leverage over Japan is not an option held in reserve. Customs data through June shows at least seven straight months of zero dysprosium, terbium and yttrium shipments to Tokyo. That makes November 10th, when the export-control truce with Washington expires, a date with precedent behind it rather than a calendar entry.

Capital Markets Cross-Asset Radar has copper setting a fresh record on the London Metal Exchange, near fourteen thousand seven hundred dollars a metric ton. The desk walks back its own sulfuric-acid theory, because the inventory data points elsewhere: metal front-running expected US tariffs, draining Shanghai and London warehouses on the way. The same briefing flags a financing echo, since on an analyst's reading Hewlett Packard Enterprise settled part of an Oracle infrastructure deal in a penny-strike warrant rather than in cash.

Digital Money closes a test it set five days ago: Bitcoin's futures book deleveraged rather than blew up. Funding is roughly halved, and open interest is down almost four percent while price held in the high seventy-thousands. That is not bullish. It is a calmer setup into next week's cloture vote and Fed decision, where the news itself will move price rather than the positioning around it.

Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle has the largest interval fund in the market, Cliffwater's Corporate Lending Fund, capping redemptions for a third straight quarter. Investors who have asked for cash since the first quarter got back about seventy-eight percent of it, against roughly three-quarters at Blackstone's BCRED. Two convergent disclosures make the toll on exit a parameter an allocator can underwrite, not an isolated scare.

Private Equity and the Deal Economy has two sponsor-held businesses going to strategic buyers in the same week: SLB buying Kelvion for data-center cooling, Aon buying USI for insurance scale. Read those as specific buyers solving specific problems in consolidating verticals, not as a general reopening of the exit market. And Accelerant's round trip from IPO to take-private in about a year says the public market is not a durable exit either.

Now into the AI and compute stack, where the balance sheet keeps doing the work purchase orders used to do. Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has Nvidia's own quarterly filing disclosing guarantees capped at a hundred and five billion dollars, backing a four-gigawatt Ohio campus. That campus does not serve a customer until fiscal 2029. The bigger and less-discussed number in the same filing is two hundred seventy-nine billion dollars of supply commitments, mostly memory, roughly ninety-five percent of it inside the next three fiscal years.

Frontier AI Lab Competition has today's lead in full: Anthropic re-read roughly four hundred eighty-one million of its own transcripts and found a fourth incident, which it calls no more severe than the first three. It has now given the independent evaluator METR wide-ranging access to check that work. The flip side is OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, self-rated at a Critical cybersecurity tier on reasoning that is reportedly harder to audit, so capability and oversight are moving in opposite directions at once.

AI Products and Applications has Perplexity retiring Sonar, the search API that made its name, on September 27th. Customers get pushed onto a bundled Agent API that meters search, code execution and tool calls separately. The bet is that the product worth selling is now the harness around the model, not retrieval itself.

Enterprise Technology has the same theme from the earnings side. ServiceTitan disclosed a real dollar cost from its AI pricing product and lost roughly a quarter of its value over two sessions, though a management shake-up muddies that read. Salesforce reported seven billion Agentic Work Units, a unit it defines itself with no price attached, and paid no such penalty.

Into the applied frontier. Robotics and Physical AI has XPeng's IRON humanoid walking off a new Guangzhou production line that the company says runs above eighty percent core-process automation. That is a manufacturing claim rather than a demo score, but XPeng is still grading its own homework. It also sits beside a nine-hundred-million-dollar raise for XPeng's robotics unit, against a largest-ever disclosed humanoid order of thirty-five million dollars.

Space and the Orbital Economy has the sharper version of scarcity: two of the Pentagon's three certified launch providers cannot fly. ULA's Vulcan has been grounded since a February anomaly, Blue Origin's only New Glenn pad was wrecked in May, and neither has a binding return date. That leaves Falcon 9 carrying essentially the entire national-security manifest.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with its own correction: Insilico's Phase III of rentosertib was not already running, as this show said twice this week. It dosed its first patient on the 9th, starting a registry clock that runs to October 2029, and that is a date the sponsor cannot quietly move.

One thread worth naming before we go. For a third straight day, the largest deals in AI infrastructure are settling in equity, warrants or capped guarantees rather than in cash purchase orders. Yesterday this show said that was worth watching for a third day, and today Nvidia's Ohio guarantee and HPE's Oracle warrant supplied it. When the instrument of competition is the balance sheet rather than the invoice, the honest measure of demand keeps getting harder to find.
