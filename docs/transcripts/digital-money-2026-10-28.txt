Good morning, and welcome to Cautious Optimism Briefings. It's Wednesday, October 28th. The Fed decides this afternoon, and the most important new signal in crypto isn't the price. It's who's now positioned short.

Quick scene-setting first. Bitcoin broke below eighty thousand dollars on Saturday, bottoming near $79,146, and this desk has covered that flush all week. What's new today is what happened to positioning afterward. Glassnode reports that large traders on Hyperliquid have flipped net-short on Bitcoin for the first time since March. Bitcoin itself was trading near $80,676 as of Tuesday evening. So this isn't a short base built during the panic. It's a short base built after the bounce.

That closes a question we've been asking all week. On Saturday, we said rising open interest could mean new longs or new shorts, and the data couldn't tell us which. On Monday, we said cheap funding might mean the book had tilted toward shorts or hedges, trading cascade risk for squeeze risk. Glassnode's read is the first direct evidence for that short-tilt story.

Now, how much should that evidence move you? Less than the headline suggests, for two reasons. First, this is one venue. Hyperliquid is a meaningful perpetuals market, but it's only a fraction of the broader Bitcoin derivatives complex, and its whales aren't the whole market. Second, and this matters more, a trader holding Bitcoin in cold storage or with a custodian can open a Hyperliquid short purely to hedge that holding. The data sees only the on-venue position, not the spot stack behind it. So a hedge and an outright bearish bet look identical. Treat the flip as one venue and one metric, not as smart money calling a top.

Here's the other side of the ledger. ETF buyers did the opposite of the whales. Spot Bitcoin ETFs took in $110.34 million on Monday, after two sessions of roughly $773.5 million in combined outflows. As we reported yesterday, BlackRock's IBIT carried that inflow. So heading into today's Fed decision, two of the marginal channels that matter most this week are leaning in opposite directions. Hyperliquid's perpetual whales are leaning short, and the ETF dip-buyers are leaning long. Whichever side proves wrong once the Fed speaks becomes forced flow in the other direction. That's our inference, not a measured flow.

That's the asymmetry to watch. Longs were already flushed out last weekend, and large traders now lean short. So the risk profile into this afternoon has shifted. It used to be cascade risk, meaning more forced long liquidations. Now it looks more like squeeze risk, a move higher that forces the new shorts to cover. A hawkish surprise would likely validate the short positioning. A dovish or in-line read could squeeze it instead. Don't take this as a prediction of which happens. It's a map of who gets hurt in each case.

There's also a concentration of options risk sitting two days behind the Fed. Roughly 35 percent of all Bitcoin options open interest on Deribit expires on Friday, October 30th. The notional value is on the order of eleven billion dollars, with max-pain estimates clustering around $78,000 to $79,000. A post-Fed move toward that band would validate both the short whales and the options sellers. A squeeze toward the low eighty thousands or beyond would hurt the shorts first. So read today's whale flip as a shift in who's exposed, not as a signal about where price is going.

One more data point belongs in this same frame, because it shows the de-risking across assets, not just inside Bitcoin's own order book. Bitcoin's 90-day correlation with gold has hit 54 percent, per Block Scholes. That's the highest reading in a series dating back to 2014. On its face, that sounds like the digital-gold thesis finally working. It isn't. A correlation says nothing about direction, and this record arrived in a week when both assets were falling. Monday's Throughline noted gold selling off alongside equities, which our Strategic Power desk read as a funding squeeze. That reading is inference, not a measured cause. But a record correlation during a joint decline looks like both assets being sold as sources of liquidity, not like hedging. And last Thursday, when Meta's AI-capex shock hit, Bitcoin traded with tech, not with gold. The digital-gold story keeps failing in exactly the way it's measured.

If positioning is setting Bitcoin's price this week, rulebooks are setting stablecoin market share. Now to Europe.

Two dated regulatory catalysts are splitting the euro stablecoin market by administrative fiat. On Friday, October 30th, Coinbase will automatically convert European users' remaining balances of fourteen non-compliant stablecoins at market rate. The list includes USDT, DAI, and USD1. Then on November 12th, direct supervision of USDC's reserves, liquidity, and redemption plans shifts from France's ACPR to the European Banking Authority. That follows the EBA's confirmation that USDC is the EU's first "significant" e-money token under MiCA. Both dates are still ahead.

The measurable result is already visible. Kaiko puts USDC's share of euro-paired stablecoin trading volume at 56 percent, up from just 10 percent in January of 2024. Keep in mind that's trading-volume share in euro pairs, not supply. And it isn't a demand story. It's a regulator rewriting the menu a venue is allowed to offer, and the share shift follows the rule. It echoes the OCC's final GENIUS Act rule, which we covered on October 25th, and which dropped the automatic seven-day extension on redemptions. On both sides of the Atlantic, compliant status is being bought with tighter reserve and redemption oversight, not with fewer constraints.

Here's the tension that makes this more than a regulatory footnote. USDC is winning Europe's trading volume while its global float keeps shrinking. USDC shed $4.08 billion over the past 30 days, the largest redemption of any stablecoin tracked. Meanwhile USDT still holds roughly $182.4 billion, about 60 percent of total dollar-stablecoin supply. So a regional win in trading share hasn't shown up in aggregate dollar float.

The honest bull case is still worth stating. A stablecoin under direct EBA supervision of its reserves is the version banks and institutions can actually hold on their balance sheets, not just trade. Will that supervisory premium eventually pull global float toward USDC? Or does the EU simply become a compliant pocket next to a USDT-dominated rest of the world? That's the open question. If stablecoin share keeps splitting by jurisdiction, demand for the short-dated government paper backing each token could split with it. That's our inference, not a measured flow, so hold it loosely. The takeaway is that share won by rulebook is not the same thing as adoption.

Coinbase sits at both ends of that stablecoin story, and it reports third-quarter results tomorrow after the close. Consensus points to about $1.2 billion in revenue, a roughly 37.4 percent decline from a year ago. These are estimates, not results. A drop that steep, expected even with Bitcoin near eighty thousand dollars, is consistent with trading activity that has thinned structurally, not just in one bad week. Coinbase is also the exchange executing Friday's European stablecoin conversion. So tomorrow's print poses a clear question. Can stablecoin and custody economics offset an exchange business where trading keeps thinning?

So, to pull it together. Positioning, not adoption, is setting Bitcoin's price into today's Fed decision, with perpetual whales and ETF buyers leaning opposite ways. And in stablecoins, rulebooks, not demand, are setting market share, with USDC winning Europe by conversion even as its global float contracts. Watch five things from here, all still pending: this afternoon's Fed decision, whether Hyperliquid's whales stay net-short afterward, Coinbase's print tomorrow, Friday's forced EU stablecoin conversion and options expiry, and the EBA's supervisory handover on November 12th.

That's the briefing for Wednesday, October 28th. Stay cautiously optimistic.
