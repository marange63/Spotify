Good morning, this is The Throughline for Friday, September 4th, 2026. Today's headline: OpenAI shipped GPT-6 Astra with benchmark scores so high they have stopped telling frontier systems apart, right as Anthropic's coming IPO prospectus prepares to make one lab's finances checkable for the first time. The capability scoreboard just broke. The financial one is about to turn on.

Start with the macro backdrop, where the counting is going badly. Strategic Power has two cabinet secretaries and the tanker trackers describing different Straits of Hormuz. The Energy Secretary says about nine million barrels a day are still moving. Treasury Secretary Bessent has claimed fifteen to seventeen. Independent trackers say seven at best. Use price as the tiebreaker. Brent closed above ninety-five dollars. War-risk premiums on a Hormuz transit are running three to ten percent of hull value, against a quarter of a percent before the war. The insurance market is voting with the trackers.

That gap between claim and count lands directly on the Fed. Capital Markets Radar has Governor Christopher Waller saying he would be inclined to hold rates on September 16th if next week's inflation data comes in cool. Hike odds fell thirteen points in a single session on that conditional alone, to roughly fifty-fifty. The Radar's warning is blunt: fifty-fifty is undecided, not dovish. It also has the hard flow number under the oil story. Vessel crossings through Hormuz collapsed from twenty-three the prior Wednesday to ten on Monday. That disruption lands in the very inflation prints on which Waller just conditioned his hedge.

Digital Money reads the same rate question from the crypto side, and finds the sector split in two. Bitcoin is stalled near seventy-seven thousand dollars, beneath a wall of long-term holder supply between eighty-three and eighty-six thousand. ETF flows have turned fast and two-way rather than a one-directional bid. Ether's eleventh straight inflow session is the closest thing to one-way money here, but it looks more like flow chasing a price move that already happened.

If rates stay where they are, exits stay shut, and Private Equity has someone pricing that assumption. EQT's purchase of the secondaries firm Coller Capital closed on time, funded mostly in stock, at roughly sixteen to eighteen times fee-related earnings. That is a full price, not a bargain. It is a bet that the industry's three point eight trillion dollar exit backlog takes nine to eleven years to clear. EQT is buying the toll booth rather than waiting for the thaw.

The same question, asserted versus checkable, runs straight into the AI and compute stack. Frontier AI Labs has the Astra story in full. Ninety-eight percent on FrontierMath's hardest tier, and a flat one hundred percent on ExploitBench. Scores that high cannot rank anything. Both are OpenAI's own numbers, unreplicated, with no system card retrievable at broadcast time. And Astra reasons in a way researchers cannot inspect.

The same briefing carries the other half of today's headline. Anthropic plans to release its IPO prospectus publicly after Labor Day, targeting a listing in late September or early October. A prospectus is an involuntary disclosure regime. It would force out contracted compute obligations, cumulative losses and customer concentration that no frontier lab has ever had to publish. It is the first checkable number this sector will get, arriving the same week the capability numbers stopped meaning anything.

The week's most credible piece of real agentic evidence came from a courtroom instead. In Apple's trade-secrets suit against OpenAI, a quoted defendant says his AI agent learned a specialized circuit-design tool in hours, cutting a full day's task to two. Treat that as an anecdote, not a measurement.

AI Products and Applications has Astra's actual rate card. Ten dollars per million input tokens, fifty per million output. That is the exact price Anthropic set for Claude Fable two days earlier. Headline pricing has stopped differentiating the two labs. The real competition moves to cache-read and cache-write fees that nobody publishes side by side.

Enterprise Technology has the one AI-attach number this earnings cluster can stand behind. Snowflake's product revenue grew thirty-seven percent, and the company calls that a third straight quarter of accelerating growth. Every other vendor in the cluster attached an AI number to a growth rate that was standing still. Palo Alto Networks, reporting a day earlier, shows where the bill lands. Gross margin fell a point on rising cloud and memory costs, and next year's platform growth is guided down by roughly two-thirds.

Turning to the applied frontier, where the self-grading problem is hardest. Robotics and Physical AI has Figure AI committing three and a half billion dollars of compute for up to a hundred thousand Nvidia GPUs. First deployment is targeted for the second half of 2027. Figure has never disclosed a robot-revenue figure. Unitree, the one humanoid maker with a real income statement, earned about two hundred fifty million dollars last year.

Space and the Orbital Economy has the sector's first genuine conversion story. Planet Labs posted its first adjusted-profitable quarter, carried by defense and intelligence revenue growing more than ninety percent, not by the commercial subscriptions it was built to sell. Starship's tower-catch attempt sits inside a three-way reporting dispute over whether the FAA has actually approved it.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with AI BioDesign, a Nobel-anchored Seattle consortium promising open models, data and benchmarks. The commitment is ninety-four point six million dollars over five years, with no named license and no shipping timeline attached yet. That is roughly a tenth of what its own co-founder's private, closed venture Xaira raised at launch. And Isomorphic Labs' headline claim, fifty percent design accuracy against AlphaFold 3's twenty-three, is again the company's own unreplicated number.

One thread is sharper today than it has been all week. This show has spent most of the past week naming the gap between claims and outside checks. Today that gap hit a ceiling. Astra's evals score so close to perfect that they can no longer rank anything. Isomorphic publishes the ranking that sells access while withholding the numbers that would let a buyer audit it. When a benchmark saturates, the honest move is to stop trusting the scoreboard, not to declare a winner. Watch for who replicates something first, rather than who claims it loudest.
