Good morning, this is The Throughline for Friday, August 7th, 2026. Today's headline: the Google DeepMind story that's been building all week finally resolved, and it's worse than a reshuffle — two of the researchers who could have shipped Gemini 4 left on the exact day their replacement was named, and they landed at a new science-AI venture that may or may not be partly funded by Google itself.

Start with the macro backdrop. Strategic Power has Taiwan rehearsing wartime production relocation this week, throttling mobile data for seventy-three percent of the population in staged regional blackouts, while carefully exempting the hours its own stock exchange trades — the one thing Taipei won't touch even in a drill. The same briefing has ERCOT's own chief executive telling Texas lawmakers the grid needs to nearly double capacity by 2032, bullish for power infrastructure if delivered, just announced-versus-built optimism if not.

That same tension between what's promised and what's confirmed carries into Capital Markets Radar, which built toward this morning's jobs report for four days and still can't hand you a clean number as of taping. What it can hand you is the setup: a Fed that just produced a rare hawkish dissent over energy-driven inflation, a labor market so uncertain that Wall Street forecasts range from eighteen thousand to eighty-three thousand payrolls, and a bond market that met this week's Hormuz-driven oil spike by pushing yields up rather than down — the term-premium reflex, not the flight-to-quality one. So credit has the least cushion of any asset class if today's number disappoints, with high-yield spreads sitting in the richest decile of history.

Digital Money finds that same gap between real demand and real price movement one layer down. Bitcoin ETFs just posted their strongest week since April, whale wallets are accumulating, and long-term holder supply hit an all-time high — yet Bitcoin still can't clear sixty-five thousand dollars. Underneath that, stablecoin supply has shrunk fifteen billion dollars since May, the sharpest contraction since Terra, as the GENIUS Act's no-yield rule pushes capital into tokenized Treasuries instead. And Tether's own excess-reserve cushion has roughly halved in a quarter, now more correlated to that same range-bound Bitcoin price.

Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle shows the same disconnect in lending. Blackstone Secured Lending's non-accruals actually improved this quarter, yet its net asset value took its second straight sharp markdown — a real inversion between the distress metric and the manager's own mark. Ares Capital told a similar story: its parent raised a record thirty-six billion dollars while the lending vehicle absorbed its largest new non-accrual and a falling NAV. And Blue Owl's retail credit funds hit their redemption cap for a second straight quarter, a structural liquidity mismatch, not an insolvency scare.

Now the AI and compute stack, where the same beat-but-punished pattern shows up in earnings. Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has July as the worst month for chip stocks since 2008, twenty-one percent down even as TSMC, AMD, and memory pricing all kept beating expectations. AMD grew data-center revenue 107 percent year over year and still fell after hours — the market now grades capital discipline, not growth, and its capex roughly tripled.

Frontier AI Lab Competition carries the day's headline in full: Jeff Dean and Oriol Vinyals didn't just leave Google, they founded Discovery Loop, a public-benefit corporation aimed at automating scientific experimentation, backed by Radical Ventures and Khosla Ventures. Reporting says Alphabet itself may have invested in the seed round; the lead investor's own announcement doesn't mention Alphabet at all, so treat that as reported but unconfirmed. What's not in dispute is timing — both departures landed on the same day Google named a new technical lead to ship the still-delayed Gemini 4.

AI Products and Applications shows the fight has moved past model quality, into adoption and trust. Microsoft's Copilot converts under four and a half percent of its four hundred fifty million seats even as Nadella reports thirty million paid seats and record satisfaction scores — both true at once, off a tiny base. OpenAI launched "Sign in with ChatGPT" to own the login layer as Google and Apple already do, and Anthropic shipped compliance-team inference hooks for Claude Enterprise, betting regulated buyers pay for a gatekeeper, not just a chatbot.

Enterprise Technology has that same bifurcation in hard numbers. Cloudflare and Datadog both beat revenue estimates this week and both got sold off — Cloudflare for disclosing a real restructuring charge tied to its AI pivot, Datadog for guidance implying decelerating growth off a concentrated customer base. Salesforce, meanwhile, put a genuine production deployment on the board: Agentforce is live for the US Army's 9.2 million soldiers, veterans, and families — the clearest evidence yet of enterprise AI at scale, not in pilot.

Finally, the applied frontier. Robotics and Physical AI has DeepMind's new Gemini Robotics 2 controlling a humanoid's full body under one policy — impressive, but its own benchmarks show a sixty-point success-rate swing between tasks, proof of progress, not proof of reliability. Uber committed ten billion dollars to robotaxi expansion owning none of the underlying autonomy, betting the value sits in aggregating demand.

Space and the Orbital Economy has Blue Origin saying it's identified and is fixing the valve that destroyed New Glenn in May, while NASA's own administrator publicly floats 2028 for the launch pad's repair — a two-year gap between company optimism and government skepticism, right as China keeps launching GuoWang satellites on an unglamorous, undisturbed monthly cadence.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes with Recursion Pharmaceuticals doing something rare: pairing quantified productivity claims with real capital discipline in the same earnings release. But every one of those gains sits in pre-clinical operations. Industry-wide, Phase Two success rates for AI-discovered drugs still track the historical baseline — the biology-bound part of the problem hasn't moved.
