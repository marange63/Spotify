Good morning, this is The Throughline for Tuesday, August 11th, 2026. Today's headline: a double energy shock in the Gulf just forced the rates market to round-trip a ten-day move, and three separate briefings are pricing the same collision between soft growth and reviving inflation from three different angles.

Start with the macro backdrop. Strategic Power has the physical story underneath it: the Strait of Hormuz sits at day 163 of de facto closure, and Saudi Arabia's Jazan refinery, hit by a Houthi drone strike on the Red Sea, has now been down for close to three weeks rather than the single Sunday incident the headlines suggested. Two chokepoints compromised at once is why crude broke sharply higher into the high eighties. Capital Markets Radar picks up the financial half of that same story: the ten-year yield fell to 4.641% on a shockingly negative jobs report, then climbed back above 4.7% as Hormuz talks broke down and oil jumped, with three Fed officials already dissenting hawkish under new chair Kevin Warsh. So the curve right now is trading oil headlines more than labor data, and credit is the one market still refusing to price any of the stress, with high-yield spreads sitting in the richest decile of their own history.

Digital Money shows what that calm-on-the-surface dynamic looks like inside crypto. Bitcoin is pinned near $64,000 even as Strategy, the largest corporate holder, sells bitcoin below its own cost basis in back-to-back weeks to service a dividend ratchet that only tightens, while BlackRock's IBIT absorbs three-quarters of all ETF inflows almost alone. Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle turns from eroding marks to active intervention this week: BlackRock's TCP Capital sold nearly half its loan book at a discount to delever, and KKR injected its own capital into FS KKR the same week, while banks are pulling higher-quality borrowers back out of the direct-lending market. Private Equity and the Deal Economy closes the macro tour with the EA buyout's fine print: the $55 billion take-private was financed with roughly 60% equity, a strikingly conservative structure that says sovereign capital, not the loan market, is what's underwriting the very top of the deal market right now.

Now the AI and compute stack, where capital keeps outrunning proof. Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has Intel selling stock to the public market for the first time since its 1971 IPO, a $15 billion raise possibly headed toward $20 billion, funding capital access without yet proving 18A can win outside customers. Frontier AI Lab Competition shows the same open-versus-closed split playing out in models: Meta's new Glimmer model is mid-pack on independent benchmarks but backed by a $145 billion capex program, while Anthropic's revenue run-rate has tripled to $30 billion on enterprises paying up for trust over cheaper open weights. AI Products and Applications carries that logic to the application layer: Meta gave away an always-on local agent for free while OpenAI added transaction-completing bookings inside ChatGPT, both pushing the model itself toward being the free part of the stack. Enterprise Technology has the one hard revenue confirmation in this group: Salesforce's Agentforce crossed a billion dollars in annual recurring revenue in the same window its usage data tripled, closing the adoption-versus-revenue question this show has carried since Monday — though Datadog's reminder that a locked-in customer can still cut usage fast keeps that win from looking permanent.

Turning to the applied frontier, where the pattern from the AI stack repeats in physical form. Robotics and Physical AI has the humanoid and robotaxi sectors both getting first real market tests this week: Unitree's IPO subscription came in thousands of times oversubscribed at six times its industry's reference multiple, and Zoox began charging real fares in Las Vegas under the first-ever federal exemption for a car with no manual controls — capital and regulators moving faster than either business has proven its unit economics. Space and the Orbital Economy shows the same pattern land on Rocket Lab: record revenue and a record backlog, but the stock sold off hard because Neutron still hasn't flown and prediction markets give it only a 14% shot of reaching orbit before 2027, even as the company bets its balance sheet on an $8 billion Iridium acquisition. AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with the field's first real clinical number: Insilico's rentosertib showed a roughly 119-milliliter placebo-adjusted lung-function improvement in a small trial without a disclosed p-value, while Isomorphic Labs, sitting on nearly $6 billion of capital and milestone potential combined, still hasn't dosed a single patient — the clearest evidence yet that capital and modeling sophistication aren't the constraint in drug discovery, clinical execution is.

One thread worth naming before we close. Across almost every briefing today, capital and regulators are moving well ahead of proof: Intel raising money before proving foundry leadership, Unitree and Zoox getting priced and licensed before their economics are visible, Salesforce being the rare exception that actually closed the gap between usage and revenue. That's the day's real connective tissue — proof of concept is now the scarcest thing in the market, scarcer than the capital chasing it.
