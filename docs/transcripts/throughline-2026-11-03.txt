Good morning, this is The Throughline for Tuesday, November 3rd, 2026.

Today's headline: SemiAnalysis's new outside benchmark, GDPx, has even the best frontier model scoring just twelve percent on hard professional work, with the leaders bunched too tightly for any lab to claim a moat.

Twelve briefings shipped today. We'll start with the macro backdrop, move through the AI and compute stack, and close on the applied frontier.

Strategic Power has OpenAI's new Grid Protectors deal letting Georgia Power curtail up to a thousand megawatts of its Project Camellia campus at peak demand. There's no published limit on how often, and it adds no supply, so it changes who gets priority on the grid rather than shrinking the shortfall. The same access fight runs through chips and minerals, as Taiwan weighs a bill to criminalize unauthorized chip exports to China, and Beijing's suspension of its expanded rare-earth controls lapses November 10th.

From physical access to financial pricing. Capital Markets Radar has Brent sliding more than seven and a half percent on hopes of Iran-Qatar talks, which softens one leg of the Fed's hike case without removing its core. Meanwhile, Oracle plans to sell ten billion dollars of bonds and a similar amount of stock, to fund AI capex and its dividend. The desk's point is that the AI buildout's own cost of capital is rising, even if the Fed's path softens.

Digital Money finds crypto's regulated 2026 ledger close to flat. One Friday of spot Bitcoin ETF outflows erased nearly two-thirds of the year's net inflow, and Monday added roughly 399 million dollars more. Stablecoin supply has been shrinking since May, a leak that predates last week's Binance hack entirely.

Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle asks what soft defaults eventually become. New research on coercive liability-management deals found thirty-six percent still ended in bankruptcy, and leverage didn't fall after the deal. The sample is syndicated debt, not private credit's bilateral amendments, but the desk reads the clock as pointing toward the 2027 and 2028 maturity wall.

Private Equity closes the macro tour with sponsors choosing to stay in rather than cash out. GTCR would reportedly roll its stake into a possible Global Payments take-private, and Bain Capital is rolling into a Surgery Partners sale priced below its own bid from early last year. In both deals the sponsors' limited partners get no cash, while Blackstone's fee earnings keep growing as its realizations fall.

That's the macro backdrop. Now the AI and compute stack, starting with memory, where the suppliers are setting the terms.

Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has Samsung reportedly asking more than triple its current HBM3E price for next-generation HBM4, a first look at the invoice for the memory squeeze this desk has tracked. It's an unconfirmed opening ask, and part of that jump is simply a new generation. AMD reports after today's close, and what it says about 2027 memory costs is the first read on who absorbs them.

A standing disclosure for the next two desks: Anthropic makes the model behind this show. Frontier AI Lab Competition has Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 topping GDPx, just over a point ahead of Google's Gemini 4 Carbon, with OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra a similar margin back. With no error bars, and task partners that sell to the labs being ranked, the desk won't call that lead decisive. OpenAI, in third, spent the week buying distribution instead, signing state-linked deals in Abu Dhabi with no dollar value attached.

AI Products and Applications finds both leading labs making the agent the default surface, faster than that twelve percent says agents can finish the job. OpenAI is testing a merged chat-and-work experience, and will reportedly pool ChatGPT and Codex into one shared allowance from December. Anthropic goes further, switching organizations that left the setting unset to Unified Claude on November 10th, even where admins had turned Cowork off.

Enterprise Technology has the application layer's cleanest proof yet, with Palantir's US commercial revenue up 148 percent and outselling its government business for the first time. But US commercial bookings grew just 11 percent, so revenue is outrunning the pipeline, the mirror image of last week's backlog-heavy hyperscalers.

That's the compute stack. Now the applied frontier, where the scarce asset keeps turning out to be something other than hardware.

Robotics and Physical AI has Meta building a humanoid research prototype on China's Fourier Intelligence platform, treating the robot body as something to source. American capital is chasing the brain instead, with Physical Intelligence reportedly in talks at a valuation near twenty billion dollars, more than triple its last announced round. And every humanoid body the desk covered, Meta's included, still runs on magnets exposed to China's rare-earth controls, whichever way November 10th breaks.

Space and the Orbital Economy finds access becoming the scarce resource in orbit too. Russian satellites have spent months lining up on a US-registered ICEYE radar satellite that supports Ukraine, and CSIS says policymakers should assume Cosmos 2612 could threaten it. Meanwhile, SpaceX is reportedly in talks for Boost Mobile or its network, a fallback after all three big US carriers turned it down.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with the first durable human data this show has seen from an AI-discovery platform in weeks. In Recursion's small, single-arm trial of REC-4881, eleven of thirteen patients stayed below their baseline polyp burden twelve weeks after stopping the drug. But Recursion licensed the molecule from Takeda, so this validates AI picking the target and indication, not designing the drug.

One change against the week behind us. On Friday, this show said the market was sorting AI spenders by earnings delivery, and on Saturday, by forward delivery. Today Capital Markets Radar adds a third lens, how the spenders fund the gap, as Oracle fell five percent on a raise that leans on new stock.
