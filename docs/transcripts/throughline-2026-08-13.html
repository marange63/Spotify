Good morning, this is The Throughline for Thursday, August 13th, 2026. Today's headline: OpenAI disclosed that an unreleased model called Astra showed capability strong enough that the company "cannot rule out" hitting the top tier of its own catastrophic-risk framework — the first real crack in this week's "model race has stalled" thesis, and a self-graded one at that.

Start with the macro backdrop. Strategic Power gave the announced-versus-delivered story of the AI power boom an actual number: grid operators are likely to commit to only about 28 percent of the 1,066 gigawatts of data-center power capacity requested nationwide, per Wood Mackenzie — though the briefing flags that figure sits awkwardly next to the firm's own separate 220-gigawatt pipeline estimate, so trust the direction, not the precise ratio. The same test showed up in a $400 million Pentagon-backed scandium project and in falling Taiwan air-incursion counts: announcements of future capacity are multiplying faster than delivered capacity is.

That same gap between headline and substance runs straight into Digital Money. A richer Bitcoin futures basis on August 7th briefly beat Treasury yields and looked like it might finally explain why ETF inflows aren't moving price. Within a day it collapsed back to a thin 2 percent. And Tether posted a strong $1.5 billion quarterly profit even as the reserve buffer protecting holders was cut in half.

Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle turns that same discipline on direct lending. Gross fundings across the twelve largest BDCs fell to $5.1 billion in the second quarter against $7.5 billion in repayments, and every one of the twelve posted a net outflow. The share of loans pricing tighter than SOFR plus 500 basis points roughly halved — a genuine trend break, not just repricing. Private Equity and the Deal Economy closes the macro tour with the cleanest version of the theme yet: CalPERS marked its private-equity book up 17 percent, but the secondary market — where LP stakes actually trade for cash — is clearing at just 87 percent of net asset value, a market-tested discount to the manager-marked number.

Now the AI and compute stack, where capital and pricing power are both showing their first real cracks even as physical shipments keep setting records. Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has SK hynix and Samsung both posting blowout earnings, yet their realized DRAM price increases landed below what Wall Street had modeled. The reason is that more volume is rotating into long-term contracts rather than trading at spot — a read on financing structure, not fading demand. Meanwhile TSMC's July revenue hit a fresh record. Frontier AI Lab Competition carries that capital theme to its sharpest point: alongside the Astra disclosure, Anthropic is financing its compute buildout through $71 billion in off-balance-sheet chip-lease debt in a single sixty-day window, backstopped by Broadcom — a structure hyperscalers are watching closely because it decides who actually eats the risk if AI revenue growth ever slows.

Enterprise Technology shows what happens once that AI revenue reaches a market that's learned to look past the top line. Cisco beat on nearly every metric but sold off on thinning AI-hardware margins. CoreWeave's equity jumped 20 percent on the same quarter its credit-default swaps implied nearly coin-flip default odds. And Cloudflare hit a record high selling AI-driven traffic growth at software margins instead of hardware ones. Margin and credit quality, not AI exposure itself, are now the scoring metrics.

Turning to the applied frontier, where the same headline-versus-verified test plays out in hardware and biology. Space and the Orbital Economy has Firefly Aerospace posting record revenue and marketing a $1.5 billion backlog that its own SEC filing shows is really $563.7 million in remaining performance obligations — roughly a third of the marketed figure — while losses widen and its core Alpha rocket keeps slipping. AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with capital again running ahead of proof: Pathos AI took on over $2 billion in oncology development obligations on the strength of a Phase 1, not late-stage, efficacy signal, while a new survey found biopharma sponsors trust their own AI output far more than they trust their contract-research vendors — a trust gap for the CRO industry, not yet a proven capability gap.

One thread worth naming before we close. Nearly every briefing today ran the identical check: take the marketed, self-reported, or marked-up number, and set it against the harder figure — a GAAP filing, a market-clearing trade, an independent verification, a signed contract — that's supposed to back it up. Firefly's backlog was three times its SEC-disclosed obligations. CalPERS's mark sat well above the secondary market's cash price. OpenAI's own eval was the only evidence behind its Astra disclosure. Wood Mackenzie's power figure didn't even agree with its own other estimate. This isn't a new instinct for this show, but today it wasn't confined to one or two stories — it was the organizing test in almost every domain at once, and in nearly every case, the harder number came in smaller or shakier than the headline.
