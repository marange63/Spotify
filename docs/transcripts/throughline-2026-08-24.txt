Good morning, this is The Throughline for Monday, August 24th, 2026. Today's headline: the government stopped only deciding who gets to build America's chip and power supply chain and started owning pieces of it outright, in a portfolio of thirty companies that Fortune tallies at $26.7 billion.

Start with the macro backdrop. Strategic Power has the full version, starting with letters of intent that would convert up to $874 million of CHIPS Act money into equity in six more chip and AI-supply-chain companies. Nothing has closed yet. The engine behind the push is Intel, where the government's stake carries an unrealized gain north of $70 billion, and the same Commerce Department that holds that equity also rules on whether Nvidia's China-bound B30 chip clears for export.

Now who is positioned for that risk. Capital Markets Radar argues the calm itself is the setup: a VIX near 15 and high-yield spreads at 275 basis points are the same crowding condition measured twice. Bank of America estimates more than $100 billion of systematic equity selling would follow an S&P drawdown past roughly three percent. The fundamental candidate to trip that is Walmart, which beat on both lines last Thursday and still fell nearly nine percent premarket on soft third-quarter guidance.

Digital Money has the collateral layer compounding underneath all of it. Tokenized Treasury funds are up 107 percent year over year to roughly $15 billion, over a stretch in which bitcoin fell 36 percent. And both BlackRock's BUIDL and Circle's USYC are now reported, thinly, to be accepted as real derivatives margin, which means stress in short-dated Treasury funds can reach crypto margin calls, and back.

Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle has the day's sharpest single data point. Delaware Life and Clear Spring restated their related-party investment exposure from roughly $1.4 billion to more than $17 billion, an error of about twelve times, after grand-jury subpoenas in February. S&P cut Delaware Life's outlook to negative while affirming the rating, which either means the assets are sound or tells you how little verification sits behind an affirmation.

Private Equity and the Deal Economy closes the macro leg on a split verdict. Bain finds roughly three-quarters of buyout assets exit above the mark their sponsor set just before the sale, so sponsors aren't systematically overstating what they choose to sell. But the same AI cycle feeding that confidence has cut American software marks by nearly nine percent, and software is exactly the sector sponsors most need to sell.

Now the AI and compute stack, where balance sheets did more work than chips this week. Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has Marvell buying a foothold across the stack surrounding Google's TPUs, handing Google a warrant for up to 59 million shares rather than beating Broadcom on spec. Broadcom, for its part, is reported to be raising up to $100 billion of debt with Blackstone and Apollo to fund Anthropic's data centers, which no filing confirms.

Frontier AI Lab Competition has OpenAI putting a number on caution. Its own estimate is that new safety monitoring around its next model, Astra, adds roughly 20 percent to the inference compute of the workloads it covers, while its largest planned frontier training run stays on hold. A second brake sits in Beijing, where ByteDance and Tencent have each drawn only about 10,000 chips against licenses for 100,000 H200s apiece, which points at China, not Washington, as the tighter constraint on Chinese compute.

AI Products and Applications has Nvidia arguing the harness now matters more than the model. Its AVO agent system scored a perfect 100 on the public ARC-AGI-3 set using the same Claude Opus 5 that scores roughly 30 percent unassisted, with persistent memory and a stall-detecting supervisor closing the gap. Nvidia ran the test itself, and nobody outside the company has reproduced it.

Enterprise Technology closes that leg on sheer volume. GitHub says monthly commits went from 1.4 billion in April to 2.9 billion by August, and on August 17th it failed to scale with the traffic, taking the site down for nearly eight hours. The same week, Stripe agreed to buy the model router OpenRouter for a reported $7 billion or more, over five times the valuation it raised at three months earlier.

Turning to the applied frontier, where the buying is running ahead of the working. Robotics and Physical AI has the first hard demand data from a profitable humanoid maker. Trade-press analysis of Unitree's own offering materials puts roughly 70 percent of its robots in universities and research institutions, and fewer than 10 percent in commercial deployments. Set that against Nevada, which just authorized about 7,000 robotaxi slots in Clark County alone: the fielded, permitted work is still wheeled and narrow.

Space and the Orbital Economy has SpaceX's first quarter as a public company. Starlink is the profit engine at a 39 percent segment operating margin, but 86 cents of every dollar of the quarter's $18.4 billion capex went to AI compute for the newly folded xAI and Grok business, not to launch. So launch scarcity, at the margin, is partly a choice the dominant supplier is making.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with the AI-natives buying rather than building. Pathos AI paid $125 million upfront to license a cancer molecule from a Chinese biotech whose own Phase III trial was already running. And Xaira Therapeutics, with roughly a billion dollars raised and no disclosed clinical candidate, is hunting data partnerships instead.

One thread ties back to yesterday's close, when this show named the day's binding constraint as the right to authorize. Today the authorizer became an owner, and the same upgrade from permission to position runs through private capital: Marvell traded equity for a shot at Google's business, Broadcom is reportedly trading a hundred billion dollars of debt to keep Anthropic, and Stripe paid a five-times markup to own the routing layer for AI spending. The position now gets bought outright rather than earned.

That's The Throughline. Have a good Monday.
