Good morning, this is The Throughline for Friday, September 11th, 2026. Today's headline: Saudi Arabia's last working oil export corridor is now itself the target, and that shock is showing up in rate-hike odds, in Bitcoin, and in the cost of the power feeding AI data centers.

Start with the macro backdrop. Strategic Power has Houthi forces striking energy facilities in Saudi Arabia's Jazan region on September 8th. The refinery there had already been down intermittently since a July 27th strike, so this is a compounding outage, not a fresh one. The bigger point is the map. The Strait of Hormuz has been effectively closed since late February, and still runs at three percent of pre-war traffic. Riyadh rerouted most of its crude through the Red Sea port of Yanbu. That leaves one export route, and the Houthis just demonstrated they can reach it. Brent crossed a hundred dollars a barrel. No verified figure exists for barrels actually lost, so that print may be pricing interdiction risk rather than physical shortage. Risk premia reverse. Shortages do not.

That oil move runs straight into Capital Markets Cross-Asset Radar. The desk said a cool producer price print would invert its hawkish framing. Yesterday's print was not cool. Core prices were tame, but the number that matters is the pipeline. Stage-one intermediate demand is up eleven point three percent over twelve months, against a headline running at barely half that pace. So the pass-through into finished goods is ahead of us, not behind us. Hike odds for the September sixteenth meeting moved from roughly a coin flip a week ago into a sixty to seventy percent band. A rate hike defends inflation expectations. It cannot add a barrel of supply.

Digital Money has that repricing landing on crypto in real time. The producer price print sent Bitcoin down about five and three-quarters percent on the week. Spot ETFs bled roughly four hundred fifty million dollars over three straight days, accelerating as the shock landed. The practical takeaway is a sizing one. If the inflation calendar is setting this price, you size Bitcoin against real yields, not against the Nasdaq.

The same rate path is what the private markets are waiting on. Private Equity and the Deal Economy had no fresh deal tape this week, so it worked the disclosures instead. KKR's largest monetization quarter ever realized eight hundred forty-eight million dollars of gains, and still left eighteen point two billion unrealized. The overhang, not the exit, is the story. And the fee machine keeps compounding on wealth-channel money that no redemption cycle has tested.

Now into the AI and compute stack, where the open question is which physical input runs out first. Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has TSMC posting a record August, up ten point one percent from July, but the monthly release is one consolidated revenue line, so it settles nothing about advanced packaging. The more interesting item is OpenAI publicly discussing next-generation custom silicon with Samsung Foundry alongside TSMC. It is real and it is thin: one country manager's quote, no node, no volume, no date. It matters because the challenger that finally drew a frontier lab onto the record was Samsung, not Intel.

Frontier AI Lab Competition applies the same scarcity logic to people and to power. One talent tracker has Google DeepMind's ratio of elite hires to departures falling from about twelve-to-one in mid-2023 to roughly two-to-one now. The loudest departures mislead, though. Jeff Dean's exit was to a Google-funded spinout, not to a rival. Anthropic is the counter-signal in the same dataset, at roughly twenty-two-to-one. Underneath all of it, SemiAnalysis says planned US data-center capacity has barely been cut despite the cancellation headlines. That makes power, not chips, the binding constraint on new buildout.

Enterprise Technology has the season's clearest answer to whether an AI line can move a whole incumbent. Oracle's total revenue rose thirty percent, on a hundred twenty-one percent surge in cloud infrastructure. But free cash flow was negative five billion dollars for the quarter. It covered that gap by completing a twenty billion dollar equity sale. So the AI line moved the top line and nothing else. The cash came from shareholders, not customers.

Into the applied frontier. Robotics and Physical AI has Skild AI claiming a robot that learns a new task from one video demonstration in eleven minutes, succeeding at sixty-six percent of steps, more than seven times its comparison system. That comparison does not survive Skild's own research post. The baseline was Skild's identical model with no post-training at all. Train that same architecture conventionally and it reaches eighty-six percent, beating the flagship. The data efficiency is real. The sevenfold gap was measured against a deliberately undertrained version of itself.

Space and the Orbital Economy grades an operator on results rather than promises. Planet Labs posted record quarterly revenue of a hundred sixteen million dollars and its first positive adjusted earnings per share. But backlog grew only eleven percent against fifty-eight percent revenue growth, so the pipeline is not keeping pace with the print. AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with Lila Sciences, which says an autonomous loop took a CAR-T program to primate data in under six months at a tenfold cost reduction. The primate data look genuinely strong. The cost claim has no outside verification.

One thread worth naming before we go. For four sessions running, this show has flagged that the largest AI-infrastructure commitments keep settling off the income statement. Qualcomm's warrants to Amazon. Nvidia's capped guarantees. HPE's warrant-settled Oracle deal. Today Oracle supplied the sharpest version yet, plugging its own capex gap with its own stock, sold into the public market. When the buildout is financed on balance sheets rather than invoices, the honest read on demand keeps getting harder to find. And one caveat sits over all of it. August consumer prices print at eight thirty this morning, after every one of these briefings was recorded, and the Fed does not decide until the sixteenth.
