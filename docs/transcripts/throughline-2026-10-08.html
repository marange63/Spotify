Good morning, this is The Throughline for Thursday, October 8th, 2026.

Today's headline: private credit is being asked to become the balance sheet behind OpenAI's compute bill, as Broadcom arranges more than fifty billion dollars in financing for the custom chip it's building with OpenAI.

Twelve briefings shipped today. We'll start with the macro backdrop, move into the AI and compute stack, then close with the applied frontier.

Strategic Power's theme is the gap between declaration and enforcement. One tanker-tracking reading shows crude through the Strait of Hormuz down 27 percent from the prior week's wartime high. Secretary of State Rubio says flows are near prewar levels. Both can be defensible, because Rubio's claim holds for the region's exports, not for the strait itself. At home, FERC has suspended PJM's backstop capacity procurement until February 2027. So the binding constraint on America's largest grid is no longer megawatts. It's who pays for data-center demand.

From physical flows to financial prices. Capital Markets Radar calls Wednesday the first session where the rate shock actually hit stocks. The Russell 2000 fell 1.31 percent, roughly six times the S&P's slip. Credit got there first, with high-yield spreads more than half a point wider in about a month. The desk also corrects itself: the Fed's minutes cite higher term premiums, so its policy-path reading of the yield selloff is open again.

Digital Money reports Bitcoin failing its breakeven test, dropping below $84,000. That turns the ETF cohort's breakeven band into overhead supply rather than a floor. Liquidations jumped 235 percent, but open interest slipped just 1 percent, so this reads as a flush, not fresh bearish bets. Underneath, Coinbase now has a CFTC-registered clearinghouse built on USDC, which makes the stablecoin derivatives collateral, not just a trading pair.

Private Credit carries the headline story, and the detail that matters is what's missing. Apollo and Blackstone are only in early talks on the Broadcom facility. No outlet says who borrows the money or what secures it. The same week, Apollo extended daily pricing across its $850 billion credit platform, with asset-level marks starting October 30th. Those are its own model estimates, not market-clearing prices. As the desk puts it, raising the frequency of a guess doesn't make it less of a guess.

Private Equity finds exits increasingly pre-sold rather than market-tested. In the signed Option Care Health take-private, McKesson takes 49 percent alongside CD&R. It also holds a right to buy CD&R's stake later, at a price nobody has disclosed. Meanwhile single-asset continuation vehicles trade at an average discount to NAV of just 2.9 percent. But the selling sponsor often anchors that valuation. Liquidity keeps showing up, but less of it is priced by an arm's-length buyer.

Now the AI and compute stack, where memory is getting pricier and tokens are getting cheaper.

Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure reports Samsung guiding to a record quarterly operating profit, up roughly 20 percent from last quarter. Memory pricing power is now in the income statement, not just in price asks. In logic, Intel's Terafab scare largely reversed after Elon Musk said TSMC would at most lease part of the site. But Intel's 14A node still has no committed anchor customer.

Frontier AI Lab Competition says two Western labs just stepped onto the cheap, open layer China had been setting. Anthropic cut Haiku 5.5's list price roughly 90 percent and shipped it on day one across AWS, Google Cloud, Azure, and GitHub Copilot. Mistral countered with a trillion-parameter model pitched as Europe's sovereign answer to Chinese open weights. Its weights and license haven't shipped yet. Disclosure: Anthropic makes the model behind this show.

AI Products and Applications picks up that price war. Haiku 5.5's ten-cent input rate matches OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna exactly. So the floor is converging, and long-prompt surcharges are now where the labs differ. Meanwhile OpenAI's Intelligent UI turns ChatGPT answers into generated mini-apps. That pressures single-purpose consumer utilities far more than stateful enterprise software.

Enterprise Technology leads with a live Atlassian exploit. Attackers began probing a critical, unauthenticated file-read bug in self-hosted Data Center products within two hours of a public proof-of-concept. The desk's read is that self-hosting is now a bet on patch latency. ServiceNow's Flow, metered on consumption with no implementation project, is the vendor-run answer to that risk.

Finally, the applied frontier, where lenders and governments, not labs, set the terms this week.

Robotics and Physical AI reports Waymo upsizing its debut private loan to five billion dollars. At five and a quarter points over the benchmark, that's ordinary direct-lending pricing, not distress terms. The other lever is China's rare-earth control suspension, due to expire November 10th, which could reach humanoids assembled abroad. Chinese vendors build over 95 percent of the world's humanoids. So that lever lands mostly on the small Western slice, Tesla included.

Space and the Orbital Economy says Washington answered its own question: the FCC is accelerating SpaceX, not restraining it. It granted a new fifteen-thousand-satellite direct-to-device constellation and waived the need for a carrier spectrum lease. Carrier deals become a price negotiation rather than a regulatory necessity. That means AST SpaceMobile's carrier-partner model now has to win on merit.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with money going upstream, into data and validation rather than models. Google DeepMind, Isomorphic Labs, and Meta are jointly putting $300 million into Biohub's Virtual Biology Initiative. That's less than one platform company's guided annual cash burn. Meanwhile Danaher is building an autonomous antibody lab exposed to total design volume, whichever model wins.

So here's the thread across today's briefings: the AI buildout's bill is being pushed onto new balance sheets. It's private credit behind OpenAI's chips, outside lenders behind Waymo's fleet, and a fight over whether PJM households or data centers pay for power. Meanwhile the price of a token keeps falling. Capacity is scarce and expensive, and intelligence is getting cheap.
