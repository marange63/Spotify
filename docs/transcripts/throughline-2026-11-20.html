Good morning, this is The Throughline for Friday, November 20th, 2026.

Today's headline: Anthropic's first trading day gave the AI race its first public price, near 1.8 trillion dollars, but the market around it sold off across the board.

A standing disclosure, since that headline touches this show directly: Anthropic makes the model behind this show.

We'll start with the macro backdrop. Capital Markets Cross-Asset Radar reads Thursday's tape as market-wide de-risking, not doubt about AI. The Dow closed below fifty thousand for the first time since June, falling about as hard as the Nasdaq, and gold, bitcoin, and Treasuries all failed to hedge, leaving only the dollar. And for the first time in the desk's coverage, credit stress has spread beyond the CCC tier into the broad high-yield index.

From equities to crypto, Digital Money corrects its own call from last weekend. Bitcoin's new four-month low came with spot ETF outflows of 582 million dollars on Thursday, nearly double the day before, so the ETF wrapper, not leftover leverage, now looks like the marginal seller. The open question is whether that's a broad exit or one big holder rebalancing, since BlackRock's IBIT alone accounted for about 93 percent of the day.

Staying with credit, Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle finally got a price this week, from Fullscript, the hung buyout loan that Morgan Stanley couldn't syndicate. Four direct lenders took it at four points over SOFR, issued at ninety-six cents on the dollar. So bank-to-private substitution now has a printed price, just as the ECB plans on-site inspections of the bank fund-financing lines those lenders lean on.

Private Equity and the Deal Economy finds the deal cycle moving, but only at the top. TPG signed its six-billion-dollar take-private of BILL Holdings on Thursday, and Corpay holds an option to buy the rest in 2030, so the exit route is wired in at entry. EY's data and KKR's record distributions show buying, selling, and cash returns all concentrating among the biggest deals and the biggest managers.

That's the macro backdrop. Now the AI and compute stack, starting where Washington just drew a new line.

Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has new Commerce rules, in effect since Thursday, that define AI-capable silicon by its memory and packaging, not just its process node. Chinese designers who relied on TSMC's self-certification route now lose leading-edge access unless they win licenses. Memory is also where the market charges most, with TrendForce projecting next year's HBM4 prices at up to five and a half dollars a gigabit, roughly two point seven times this year's level.

Frontier AI Lab Competition has Anthropic opening 34 percent above its offer price and closing up 17 percent, at roughly 1.8 trillion dollars fully diluted. That sits well above OpenAI's new private mark of 1.25 trillion, though a thin, rationed float makes the two hard to compare. Meanwhile Google, with no raise needed, keeps undercutting its own premium tier, so a public valuation buys capital access, not pricing power.

AI Products and Applications has Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, and Microsoft all drawing the same line this week. Free tiers get errands. Anything that touches a desktop, a speed tier, your financial accounts, or a customer record sits behind a subscription or a preview label.

Enterprise Technology follows that pricing logic into the seat license, where Salesforce just had its worst session since May 2024, for reasons the desk still holds open. Then Microsoft, the biggest seat vendor of all, chose to meter its new agent surfaces by usage rather than fold them into its flat per-seat Copilot license. The desk reads that as a concession that the seat may not capture what agent work is worth.

That's the compute stack. Now the applied frontier.

Robotics and Physical AI has humanoid deployment still arriving as options, not orders. GXO and Apptronik signed a letter of intent for up to a thousand Apollo robots, but trials don't start until 2027. The only hard reliability number in the briefing belongs to a quadruped, Boston Dynamics' Spot, with a reported ninety-nine percent success rate across more than ninety thousand inspections at a GlobalFoundries plant.

Space and the Orbital Economy finds SpaceX's moat and its credit spread widening at the same time, with its default-swap spread more than doubled since the June IPO. Roughly forty billion dollars of chip financing sits off its balance sheet, in a separate vehicle that buys the chips and leases them to SpaceX. Meanwhile, with Vulcan and New Glenn both still grounded, SpaceX's leverage over American heavy lift keeps growing.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with Isomorphic Labs signing an oncology partnership with Merck, two days after its five-billion-dollar raise. Isomorphic delivers the molecular designs, while Merck keeps all preclinical and clinical development, so pharma is buying AI designs and keeping the risk in-house. And with about six weeks left in the year, Isomorphic still hasn't dosed the first patient it targeted for year-end.

One change versus earlier this week. Since last week, this show has tracked three first public prices on AI risk, and two have now arrived: Nvidia has reported, and Anthropic now trades. The third, ING's data-center loan auction, the only one priced in credit, had bids due Thursday, but neither credit desk has seen a result.
