Good morning. It's Friday, November 13th, 2026, and this is your frontier AI labs briefing. One disclosure up front: Anthropic makes the model behind this show, and today's lead runs through Anthropic's IPO.

The theme this week has been the AI supply chain entangling itself with the labs it sells to. Today that entanglement gets numbers attached to it. TSMC's numbers show a supplier hedging across labs. The biggest checks show something closer to concentration. Separately, Google has opened a front in physical AI that neither of its cash-hungry rivals is contesting.

Start with TSMC. On Thursday, this desk named TSMC as one of seven buyers in Anthropic's twenty-six billion dollar private placement. We also said stake sizes weren't disclosed. Now TSMC's are. TSMC's own filing with the SEC, a copy of its board resolutions, spells out three separate approvals. The first is up to five hundred million dollars to buy Anthropic common shares at the IPO price. The second is a separate line item, up to five hundred million dollars to buy OpenAI preferred stock. The third is another five hundred million, unconnected to either lab, to set up a new investment company for the broader semiconductor ecosystem. So TSMC has capped its frontier-lab exposure at a billion dollars, split evenly between the two.

Two caveats before reading anything into that. These are "not to exceed" approvals, not completed purchases. And the filing doesn't say whether either deal has closed.

Now the split itself. A foundry that buys up to the same amount of each lab is indifferent to which one wins. So its participation can't be read as a vote for Anthropic's valuation over OpenAI's. It reads as a hedge across the two leading US labs. From TSMC's seat, that fits a market shaped as an oligopoly of a few huge compute buyers, not a winner-take-most race. The so-what for TSMC: this is relationship maintenance, not a conviction bet.

That bounds one piece of Thursday's argument. We said a weak Anthropic debut would mark the suppliers' own balance sheets. For TSMC, the Anthropic exposure is at most five hundred million dollars. On my arithmetic, that's under two percent of the twenty-six billion placement.

The other buyers are a different story. A secondary summary of Anthropic's amended S-1 gives approximate amounts for all seven, though we haven't checked those figures against the filing itself. On that summary, Nvidia and Google committed about ten billion dollars each. AMD put in about two and a half billion. Broadcom, Micron, and Microsoft each committed roughly a billion. That makes TSMC the smallest of the seven, at one-twentieth of Nvidia's or Google's check. So the placement isn't seven suppliers hedging in small slices. It's dominated by two buyers, each writing a large, single-lab check. The so-what: TSMC's hedge is the exception here, not the pattern. The circularity risk this desk flagged on Thursday is real, and it's concentrated in Nvidia and Google.

Back to TSMC, and which paper it chose. In Anthropic, it's buying plain common stock at whatever the IPO prints. In OpenAI, it's buying preferred. TokenPost, reading the deal terms, reports that it's convertible preferred and caps out at no more than four hundredths of a percent of OpenAI. The outlet calculates that this implies a valuation near one point two five trillion dollars. It flags that math as its own inference, not a disclosed price.

Here's why the instrument matters. TSMC took the senior paper in the lab whose losses are widening. The Wall Street Journal, citing investor documents OpenAI hasn't published, puts OpenAI's third-quarter operating loss at ten point nine billion dollars. That's up from ten point one billion the prior quarter. And it's now a reported figure, not one backed out of the margin. My inference, since the full preferred terms are undisclosed: TSMC took the more protected paper where the losses are larger and the disclosure is thinner. That looks like a supplier hedging its downside where the downside is biggest.

One thing this is not. Nothing in TSMC's filing ties either stake, or the new investment company, to wafer allocation or capacity priority. The filing's only capacity language sits in a separate capital item, justified by long-term capacity plans based on demand forecasts. So don't read this as TSMC selling queue position. Read it as a foundry hedging its customer relationships while keeping capacity decisions on a separate track.

The calendar makes this concrete. Nasdaq's own trader notice confirms that Anthropic begins trading on November 19th under the ticker A-N-T. Pricing is expected the day before, next Wednesday, though dates can still move. The marketed range is seventy to seventy-eight dollars a share. Because TSMC buys at the IPO price, that print sets the entry for a supplier that also holds OpenAI paper. So next Wednesday's print becomes a public reference point next to that implied OpenAI valuation. Any supplier sitting on both cap tables will re-mark against it.

Now the contender that needs nobody's money. Google is actually one of the two largest funders of Anthropic's placement, on that same secondary summary. Correction first, though: on Wednesday this desk said no verified Google move had surfaced in our research. That was wrong. Google DeepMind had announced Gemini Robotics 2.5 on November 10th.

Gemini Robotics 2.5 splits into two models. One handles task planning, and the other handles arm and hand control. DeepMind says the system can learn hundreds of everyday skills, from folding laundry to sorting car parts on a factory floor. The headline number comes from a live proof of concept on Mercedes-Benz production lines. There, DeepMind reports a ninety-six percent success rate on a parts-sorting and kitting task.

Be precise about what that number is. The write-up we relied on calls it strictly the result of a proof of concept for that one task. It's self-reported, with no independent audit, and no stated duration or volume. Access is rolling out to robotics partners, including Agile Robots, Apptronik, and Boston Dynamics, not the public. There's no pricing and no general-availability date. And we worked from DeepMind's own post plus a secondary write-up, not the full technical report.

It also fits a pattern. Google's ninety-six percent joins a week of narrow, vendor-run success rates across the robotics field. Direction, not proof.

Still, the competitive point holds. Neither OpenAI nor Anthropic has a comparable public offering to put in front of an auto manufacturer. Google is funding a long-dated, low-near-term-revenue bet from its own cash. Its two rivals, meanwhile, are raising money in pieces. Anthropic is doing it through an IPO and a supplier placement. OpenAI is doing it through investor rounds and now a slice of preferred sold to its foundry. My inference on the second-order effect: Google's partner-distribution model raises the bar for vertically integrated efforts like Tesla's Optimus to show comparable factory results with outside parties. The so-what: Google's edge here is distribution and capital, not demonstrated reliability at scale.

So, quickly by dimension. Capital access: Google self-funds and even helps fund a rival. Anthropic and OpenAI draw on supplier money that TSMC hedges but Nvidia and Google concentrate. Ecosystem control: TSMC is positioned to collect whichever lab leads, while the two biggest checks in Anthropic's placement are single-lab bets. My read is that the foundry's rents look durable either way, while Nvidia and Google carry real exposure to how Anthropic trades. Physical AI: Google alone has a named-partner story this week, and it's unverified at scale. Model capability: nothing independently verified in this window for any lab.

Three things to watch. First, whether TSMC's two lab stakes actually close, and on what terms. Second, where Anthropic prices next Wednesday against that seventy-to-seventy-eight-dollar range. Third, whether any Gemini Robotics partner publishes a third-party result, or Google sets a price, turning this week's demo into a product.

That's the briefing. Have a good Friday, and I'll be back with you next time.
