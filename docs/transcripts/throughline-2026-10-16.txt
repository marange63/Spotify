Good morning, this is The Throughline for Friday, October 16th, 2026.

Today's headline: a September producer-price print at nearly double the forecast reopened the Fed-hike debate, yet bonds treated AI-linked credit stress as the bigger risk.

Ten briefings shipped today. Digital Money and Robotics and Physical AI did not publish. One disclosure up front: Anthropic makes the model behind this show, and it features in several of today's briefings, so weigh them accordingly. We'll start with the macro backdrop, move into the AI and compute stack, then close with the applied frontier.

Strategic Power says Taiwan plans to make shipping AI chips and servers to mainland China a criminal offense, replacing its current named-blacklist approach. It's a drafted amendment, not enacted law, so that gate isn't built yet. In Washington, the Energy Department has conditionally committed a $4.2 billion loan to Vistra, which preserves about 4 gigawatts of existing nuclear capacity rather than adding new supply.

From policy gates to the capital pricing them. Capital Markets Radar says Thursday's producer-price index rose 1.3 percent on the month, nearly double the forecast, yet the long end didn't sell off. The desk reads that as a bet that credit and growth fear now outranks inflation risk. That fear has a ladder, from CoreWeave at the weak end to Oracle's record 288-basis-point swaps, with the hyperscalers' level still unconfirmed.

Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle finds more money than good loans to put it in. Direct-lending funds raised a record $129 billion through September, even as deployment fell and borrowers refinanced back out to cheaper syndicated markets. The marginal dollar is chasing concentrated bets like Volta's roughly $5 billion GPU-backed loan for Anthropic compute, relaunched more than a full point wider after its first pass failed to clear.

Private Equity and the Deal Economy finds this week's big sponsor deals are capital going in, not coming out. Thoma Bravo's Proofpoint is buying Varonis with fresh sponsor equity, adding to a five-year-old platform rather than returning cash to limited partners. The one real exit candidate, London Stock Exchange Group's talks to buy Permira's Octus, is still unsigned, so distributions remain the binding constraint on this cycle.

Now the AI and compute stack, where this week's self-reported numbers started meeting outside checks.

Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure says the AI buildout hits different ceilings depending on where you stand. Inside China it's policy: Huawei's unit share of data-center AI chips is 28 percent to Nvidia's 34, a gap set by export rules rather than buyers rejecting Nvidia. Outside China it's physics, with ASML warning that even a 30 percent capacity increase for 2027 won't fully meet demand through 2028.

Frontier AI Lab Competition says outsiders started grading the labs' safety homework this week. Eight state attorneys general sued OpenAI over ChatGPT's engagement design, and three fired OpenAI safety researchers published a letter warning that model monitorability is degrading. On revenue, accounting conventions suggest OpenAI's lead over Anthropic is probably larger than the headlines imply, not smaller.

AI Products and Applications reports that Google now lets enterprise admins switch on Anthropic's Claude models inside the Gemini Enterprise app, billed to the same Google Cloud account. Google's own Argon model switches on by default starting October 27th, while Claude waits for an admin to opt in. Model choice is getting easier, but lock-in may be moving up a layer, into the workflows built around the model.

Enterprise Technology says every layer of the stack is now quoting its own flattering unit of economics. Microsoft's in-house Maia 200 chip is running production Copilot workloads, and Anthropic reportedly told investors its third-quarter revenue hit $16.8 billion, with adjusted profitability. None of it comes from a filing, and that quarterly figure can't be reconciled with the annualized numbers circulating ahead of a reported IPO.

From self-reported numbers to self-reported schedules, and the applied frontier. Space and the Orbital Economy says markets stopped pricing ambition this week and started pricing dates. Rocket Lab's Neutron rocket slipped into 2027 on a manufacturing defect, and the stock fell about eight percent, then roughly eleven percent more the next morning. AST SpaceMobile was repriced too, and one independent analyst now doubts it reaches sixty satellites in orbit before 2028.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with a September contract that prices both the model and the data. Recursion licensed its RNA model to Tempus for $12 million over two years, yet owes Tempus a committed $14 million a year for data in the deal's later years. The rights aren't equal in scope, but so far data access is commanding firmer pricing than the models trained on it.

Here's the thread worth carrying out. Across today's briefings, self-reported numbers met outside checks almost everywhere. Attorneys general and former staff tested OpenAI's safety posture, accounting conventions tested the labs' revenue claims, and equity markets tested Rocket Lab's and AST's schedules. Filed numbers, not projections, are what would settle any of it.
