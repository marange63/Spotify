The week's other shock came from crypto, and it's worth separating cleanly from the equity story. Binance suffered what TRM Labs calls the largest crypto hack in history. Part of that freeze reportedly came from Tether and Circle locking linked stablecoin balances. That's a useful distinction. Regulated stablecoin rails can be frozen. Bearer assets can't. The realized loss is still unknown, because the stolen funds hadn't been converted to fiat at last report.

That breaks the eighty-thousand area this desk has watched all week. Yesterday we called crypto stabilization without conviction. The hack had already hit by then, and that read missed it.

But read the cause, not just the price. This is a venue-specific custody failure, not a macro risk-off signal. It sits in exchange wallet security, not in rates or growth expectations. And there's no evidence yet of contagion to other venues. So Bitcoin's break does not, by itself, confirm the equity selloff as broad risk-off.

The more durable effect may be structural. A state-linked actor draining the largest centralized exchange raises the custody risk premium across the whole complex. That premium can outlast the price move. Our inference, not a reported flow, is that it pushes the institutional marginal buyer further toward regulated ETF wrappers, and away from exchange balances. So this is not a macro signal, but possibly a lasting structural one.

Now the test this desk owes you before calling any of this idiosyncratic. A genuine risk-off wouldn't stay inside equities and crypto. Rates, credit and the dollar haven't confirmed that, and they haven't contradicted it either. That's the first gap to close next week.
