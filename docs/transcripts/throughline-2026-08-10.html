Good morning, this is The Throughline for Monday, August 10th, 2026. Today's headline: the fight over AI is settling one layer below the model, and today six briefings independently priced a piece of that same constraint — the power, the chips, the packaging, and the capital financing it all.

Start with the macro backdrop. Strategic Power has the clearest number of the day: PJM's capacity auction for 2028-2029 cleared at $325 per megawatt-day, procuring $16.4 billion of generation, and PJM's own release names large data-center loads as the cause. FERC's deadline for grid operators to justify or reform interconnection rules is now one week away, with the extension off-ramp already closed, so authority is racing to catch a cost the market has already priced. Layered on top, a shock July jobs report is pushing the Fed toward September cuts even as new Section 338 tariffs on Canada land August 19th — cheaper capital and rising input costs arriving at the same moment.

Capital Markets Radar picks up the tail-risk half of that picture. The Hormuz standoff re-escalated over the weekend, Brent above $83 and transit still choked to roughly 8 vessels a day, while equities and high-yield spreads sit at records that price none of it. On the credit side, Ares and Blackstone's BDC prints came in calmer than Blue Owl's scare last week, tilting the private-credit story toward idiosyncratic rather than systemic — though falling net asset values at both firms keep that question only provisionally closed.

Digital Money has Bitcoin absorbing its strongest ETF inflows since April, $853.5 million in a week, without moving at all, while Circle locked Coinbase into a renewed distribution deal through 2029 on the same lopsided revenue split the market already dislikes. Zoom out and dollar stablecoins are winning distribution everywhere, at home and abroad, faster than they're winning the economics or the rules underneath that dominance.

Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle ties directly back to the power story: Morgan Stanley now projects private credit will supply roughly $800 billion of data-center financing through 2028, arriving just as hyperscaler bond coverage has fallen from five times to under two times. That capital is flowing in at the top even as Blue Owl's dividend cut and a Loparex loan marked down from 63 cents to 5 cents show the existing book's first real cracks. Private Equity and the Deal Economy quantifies the exit drought this show has flagged all week — Bain's own report puts distributions below 15% of net asset value for four straight years, the worst run since 2008, with a live Delaware case now testing whether continuation vehicles, the industry's main liquidity workaround, are fair to the limited partners funding them.

Now the AI and compute stack, where today's real news lives. Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has the sharpest data yet on where the buildout is actually rationed: TSMC's advanced packaging is sold out through year-end with lead times past a year, forcing even Nvidia to buy capacity from outside subcontractors, while SK hynix's own CEO calls 2027 the worst supply year in memory-industry history. Frontier AI Lab Competition carries that same logic up to the labs themselves — SemiAnalysis estimates Gemini's growth is roughly halving even as Google's TPU cloud business booms, and Anthropic has confirmed it's building its own inference-chip team, the same move OpenAI already made with Broadcom's Jalapeño chip. So the competitive edge between labs is migrating to who owns the silicon, not who ships the better model.

AI Products and Applications shows the cost logic one layer up the stack: Meta's new Muse Code coding agent undercuts Anthropic's pricing by roughly sixty to seventy percent, and Cloudflare's from-scratch Kitesurf browser engine cuts agent hosting costs by three to seven times — both racing the price of running an agent toward zero. Enterprise Technology confirms the demand is real underneath all of it: AWS's backlog jumped to $496 billion and Google Cloud's to $514 billion, both growing faster than recognized revenue, so the open question is no longer whether enterprise AI demand exists but whether capacity can be built fast enough to serve what's already been sold.

Turning to the applied frontier. Robotics and Physical AI has NHTSA's investigation into Tesla's Full Self-Driving escalating to an Engineering Analysis, with a response deadline just two days away and Tesla's own filings conceding its camera-only detection system may have missed a third of the crashes under review — a live regulatory test of the vision-only bet underneath both FSD and Optimus. AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with Tempus AI's swing to a profit, on the back of an oncology foundation model delivered to AstraZeneca as a contractual product, the clearest evidence yet that the money in AI drug development is following the data-and-operations layer rather than any proof a model actually gets a molecule through the clinic.

One thread worth naming before we close. Today the "who controls the physical stack" story stopped being one briefing's argument and became the day's connective tissue: PJM's audited grid price, TSMC's sold-out packaging, Google's and Anthropic's chip moves, and $800 billion of private credit lining up to finance all of it are the same constraint priced five different ways. The model race looks stalled for now — the infrastructure race underneath it is where the money and the risk are actually moving.
