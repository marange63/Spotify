Good morning, this is The Throughline for Sunday, November 15th, 2026.

Today's headline: four nonprofits have sued Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI, and SpaceX's AI unit, asking a California court to freeze all five at their current capability, days before Anthropic is expected to price its IPO.

One standing disclosure: Anthropic makes the model behind this show.

Eleven briefings shipped today. We'll start with the macro backdrop, move through the AI and compute stack, and close on the applied frontier.

Strategic Power finds that shrinking data-center forecasts haven't shrunk the grid's bill. PJM's independent market monitor says capacity costs are up more than 80 percent this year, and it expects December's auction to clear at the cap again. Anthropic, meanwhile, reportedly agreed to pay $3.4 billion for gas generators, which the desk reads as buying a place in Caterpillar's queue.

From the grid to the tape, Capital Markets Radar has bitcoin, the only major market open this weekend, marking risk down another leg while the US spot ETFs were shut. Monday's open inherits that markdown, plus the first company-reported crack in the AI-infrastructure trade: Nebius fell 20 percent on a low-end 2026 revenue guide. Nvidia reports the next day, its first print under interim chief executive Colette Kress since Jensen Huang resigned.

Digital Money picks up the same weekend low, just under $62,000 on Coinbase, and finds leverage stacked across the whole crypto complex, with bitcoin only about a third of the latest liquidations. ETF outflows are fading even as price breaks lower, so the desk's read is that leverage, not the wrapper, is setting bitcoin's marginal price. Coinbase, despite a sizable earnings beat, still trades as a levered bet on the same positioning that's driving bitcoin.

Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle has the first crack in data-center debt showing up as a smaller deal, not a lower price. Blackstone-backed QTS cut a planned $800 million debt sale by about 60 percent rather than pay the higher yields buyers wanted. Because resizing hides the price, the desk says the first hard mark may still come from ING's data-center loan auction, with bids due Thursday.

Private Equity has Partners Group's flagship US evergreen fund paying out only a third of redemption requests, which ran at roughly three times its cap. In the same quarter, Blackstone's retail private-equity vehicle, BXPE, posted record gross sales of $2.9 billion, though Blackstone gave no redemption figure. The desk reads that as fundraising concentrating at the top while the exit backlog builds underneath.

That's the macro backdrop. Now the AI and compute stack, starting at the equipment layer underneath Nvidia.

Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has Applied Materials guiding next quarter's revenue up 58 percent year on year at the midpoint, so the 2027 tool step-up now rests on a company number, not just Lam's forecast. Applied's finance chief named clean-room capacity, not budgets, as the gating factor. The risk is policy, because China is still a quarter of Applied's revenue, and the desk sees tools as a natural next layer if Congress tightens export controls.

Frontier AI Lab Competition notes the suit's second ask, a ban on AI autonomously directing AI research, which the desk links to the threshold Anthropic's own IPO filing says it may be near. The desk calls an injunction a long shot and sees discovery as the more durable risk, given what a five-defendant suit can force into the public record. SpaceX's AI unit, a co-defendant, reportedly cut about 60 people from its video-generation team to chase coding and agents, where every major lab is now crowding.

AI Products and Applications has Google rolling its new Gemini 4 Hydrogen model into the surfaces it owns, from Search to Sheets, at one flat price across its full context window. Amazon's standing ban on outside shopping agents shows the same logic from the other side: whoever owns the checkout decides which agent gets in. Anthropic and GitHub, by contrast, are moving enterprise agents onto customers' own infrastructure, with pricing and scope still unfinished.

Enterprise Technology notes that Dell's stock fell more than 40 percent in about a month despite a record $95 billion AI backlog, which the desk reads as the market no longer taking backlog on trust. The more credible evidence is Five9, whose recognized AI revenue grew 91 percent while its seat-based core business still grew. And Anthropic switches its unified Chat and Cowork experience on by default tomorrow, three days before its shares trade, which the desk calls distribution, not adoption.

That's the compute stack. Now the applied frontier, where the robot brain got squeezed and a rocket cleared one hurdle while raising a new question.

Robotics and Physical AI has OpenAI disbanding its standalone robotics team, saying its general model already beats specialized robot models on a simulated Nvidia benchmark. The lead is wide, but it rests on just five runs per task, graded by Nvidia, which sells a rival robot brain. On the capital side, humanoid startup Foundation reportedly hasn't paid staff since missing its October 30th payroll, under a founder who is its only board member.

Space and the Orbital Economy has two outlets reporting that SpaceX caught a Starship upper stage with its tower for the first time, an engineering milestone if not yet an economic one. The same flight's booster aborted its catch, and SpaceX withdrew its first explanation without offering another. The desk thinks that missing cause matters more than the lost hardware, because programs riding on Starship, from the Starlink V3 buildout to NASA's lunar lander, can't yet price a repeatable recovery rate.
