Good morning, this is The Throughline for Tuesday, September 8th, 2026. Today's headline: the first market prints since Saturday's Gulf escalation are in, and they moved in two directions at once — Brent crude at a three-month high, and copper within a dime of its record, pricing the same shock through two different channels.

Start with the macro backdrop, where energy infrastructure is under attack on one side of the world and under strain on the other. Strategic Power counts Ukrainian drone strikes on eight of Russia's ten largest oil refineries, with only two left undamaged. The evidence that the campaign is biting sits downstream rather than at the plant gate. Russian gasoline prices are rising at their fastest pace in two decades, and Moscow has stretched its gasoline export ban through year-end. But there is no verified barrels-per-day figure for how much refining capacity is actually offline, so the magnitude is read off consumer symptoms rather than measured at the source. The same briefing has PJM's latest capacity auction clearing sixteen point four billion dollars, with data-center load — still mostly forecast, not metered — about forty percent of that bill.

Capital Markets Cross-Asset Radar has the market side of that same shock. Brent rose to just under ninety-nine dollars a barrel while WTI eased slightly, two grades reading one event differently. That argues the tape is pricing prolonged tightness rather than a confirmed physical loss. The genuinely new leg is copper, above six dollars sixty a pound on a theory that Gulf-linked sulfuric acid shortages are squeezing South American refiners. That mechanism rests on a single line of market commentary, so treat it as plausible rather than confirmed. Underneath both, futures now put roughly a sixty percent chance on a Fed hike at the September 16th meeting, while the VIX has sat between fourteen and seventeen for twenty-five straight sessions even as oil and copper volatility goes vertical.

Private Equity and the Deal Economy closes the macro leg with the number showing which exit route actually broke. PitchBook's second-quarter data has total US private equity exit value down roughly 46 percent, but sales to strategic corporate buyers, historically the largest channel, down 63 percent. That gap is a composition shift rather than a base effect, and it pushes the realization burden into sponsor-controlled continuation vehicles.

Now into the AI and compute stack, where this week's competition is being fought over supply chains rather than benchmarks. Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has the cost of that fight: HBM4 memory pricing near thirty-one dollars a gigabyte on TrendForce's estimates, close to double HBM3e, with Micron's output booked out through 2027. That pricing is contracted rather than spot. So if AI capex disappoints, the pain lands on memory makers as cancellations, not on buyers as price relief. Intel, meanwhile, hit its 18A-P milestone on schedule and still has no named external customer behind the bigger 14A claim.

Frontier AI Lab Competition has the same supply-chain logic showing up on cap tables. Mistral closed a three-billion-euro round led by Samsung, with Nvidia and ASML reported in it as well. The memory maker, the chipmaker and the lithography maker are all on one lab's register — a genuinely new funding template, though no supply terms have been disclosed alongside the equity. Anthropic is the other model, with reporting still expecting an autumn listing and press estimates, explicitly not company guidance, near a two-trillion-dollar valuation on a sixty-five-billion-dollar revenue run-rate.

Enterprise Technology has the public-versus-private version of that split. MongoDB beat and raised and still fell more than twelve percent, because its own guidance implied deceleration from 30 percent growth to about 20. Databricks signed a term sheet for five billion dollars the same week at a hundred-eighty-eight-billion valuation, on an eighty-percent growth claim it breaks down for nobody. The market there isn't paying for better information. It's paying for the absence of a checkable number.

Turning to the applied frontier, where the same supply-chain exposure turns physical. Robotics and Physical AI has Waymo, the best-permissioned operator in American autonomy, importing more than thirty-two hundred Chinese-built chassis and paying a tariff that more than doubles their cost. The Chinese sensors and compute are stripped out before those cars reach US roads. That the cost advantage survives being doubled is the whole story, and Senate pressure on the practice is escalating.

Space and the Orbital Economy has Rocket Lab's target of delivering Neutron to the pad in the fourth quarter — a line already sitting in its August earnings release before the financial press picked it up over the weekend. The stock fell 24 percent over two weeks anyway, because investors are now pricing schedule credibility itself rather than any single milestone. In China, LandSpace landed an orbital booster on its legs, then watched it catch fire and tip over. Guidance and propulsion, the hard part, are validated. Reflight, the part that actually lowers cost, is not.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with a reminder that a second look at old data is not new evidence. Insilico published a peer-reviewed biological-age readout drawn from the same forty-two-patient rentosertib trial this beat covered on Sunday. It is genuinely reviewed, but it is an exploratory endpoint, peaking at week four of twelve, with no path to a label. The briefing also corrected itself: Roche does not own TriNetX. Regeneron licensed access to that network back in April, for up to two hundred million dollars.

Now the close. Yesterday this show's headline was that the market opened with almost no verified data on what the Gulf escalation was worth. Today the first of that data arrived, with Brent, copper and Fed odds all repriced. But the two energy stories underneath it, Russia's refineries and America's grid, still have no verified volume attached to either one. Prices caught up to the story this week. The physical evidence did not.
