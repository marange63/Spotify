Good morning, this is The Throughline for Saturday, September 26th, 2026. Today's headline: oil fell on a fresh Hormuz peace signal, and the bond market ignored it, with the ten-year Treasury yield touching its highest intraday level since 2007.

In the macro backdrop, Capital Markets Cross-Asset Radar has West Texas Intermediate at 93 dollars a barrel on Friday, down on unconfirmed reports of a phased US-Iran deal to reopen Hormuz. Yet the ten-year closed at five-point-one-seven percent, the second time in a week that yields have shrugged off falling oil. The desk's read is that the long end no longer trades as a pure oil derivative, so a Hormuz deal may bring less bond relief than consensus assumes.

Digital Money finds crypto's bid surviving that yield shock, but fading. Spot bitcoin ETFs took in 134.5 million dollars on Friday, a seventh straight session above 100 million, yet the smallest of the seven. The bigger point is scale: this year's net inflows are under four percent of last year's full-year total, so the streak is a refill rather than a new wave, and the firmer floor evidence sits on-chain.

Private Equity and the Deal Economy has the harder liquidity problem, with 33,575 unsold PE-backed companies as of June, against just seventy PE-backed US IPOs since 2022. Secondaries were supposed to be the valve, but Jefferies has dedicated secondaries capital down about eleven percent in six months, even as deal volume runs at a record pace. EQT's completed deal for secondaries specialist Coller Capital shows the big managers' answer, which is to own the mechanism that clears everyone else's backlog.

Now to the AI and compute stack, where Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure finds the rack's CPU socket contested for the first time in years. Nvidia now sells its Arm-based Vera CPU standalone through the same server makers Intel and AMD sell through, while Arm sells its own finished server chip against its biggest licensees. Both still have to clear TSMC's packaging queue, where a Mizuho note now counts server CPUs as new bidders alongside GPUs.

From the chips to the invoice, AI Products and Applications finds the meter overtaking the seat. Microsoft's Copilot relaunch adds usage billing on top of the enterprise seat, and a modeled example in press coverage has more than half of one workload's bill coming from consumption. Perplexity goes further, dropping the seat and charging for each tool call an agent makes, but nobody has yet published a real customer invoice under Copilot's new pricing.

Enterprise Technology finds the week's new enterprise-AI products coming from the layer between the model labs and the systems of record. Palo Alto Networks launched a continuous agentic security service that routes each task across Anthropic's and OpenAI's models, which it says helps manage the cost of frontier AI. That reads as either leverage over the labs or dependence on them, and Palo Alto disclosed no pricing, customers or bookings to settle which.

Turning to the applied frontier, Robotics and Physical AI has Tesla's Optimus line reportedly jumping from a few dozen units a week to several hundred by August. But most of those robots go to Tesla's own testing and data collection, and the hand still carries more than a hundred screws and small parts assembled by hand. Aurora Innovation guided to just fourteen to sixteen million dollars of 2026 revenue, small money for a company targeting two hundred driverless trucks by year-end.

Space and the Orbital Economy sees the same gap between announced cadence and working hardware. The real money went to what already works, as Iridium shareholders approved Rocket Lab's buyout at an enterprise value of about 8 billion dollars, for a constellation earning fixed Pentagon fees. Meanwhile AST SpaceMobile is targeting six satellites a month, a pace it has never shown, and China's Qianfan keeps launching with no published count of working satellites.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with Anthropic, which makes the model behind this show, reporting that a Claude agent swarm flagged a previously uncharacterized enzyme system in protein databases. The identical search missed it in ten reruns, and independent scientists note the enzyme itself was already known. So this is a known enzyme placed into a new hypothesis with unconfirmed function, and the bottleneck moves back to bench validation.

One change is worth marking. On Wednesday this show had risk assets pricing peace while rates priced persistence, and a session later rates won and nearly everything sold off. On Friday the split reopened, with the S&P 500 up about half a percent even as the ten-year hit its nineteen-year intraday high.
