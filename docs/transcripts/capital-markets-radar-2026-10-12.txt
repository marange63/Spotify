Good morning, and welcome to Capital Markets Radar for Monday, October 12th.

Cash markets were closed for the weekend, so the freshest information is not a price. It is a flow dataset, and it confirms a call this desk made last week. The speculative sleeve is de-risking, and the full-week numbers say it is happening everywhere at once.

Start with the aggregate. According to The Block, the three spot crypto ETF categories, bitcoin, ether and Solana, lost about 1.25 billion dollars last week. That is the week ending October 9th. It is the cleanest single measure of the reversal.

Take the pieces one at a time. Spot bitcoin ETFs lost 681.1 million dollars, per SoSoValue data. That ended three straight weeks of inflows. Most of the damage landed on a single day. Wednesday alone accounted for 487.1 million dollars of redemptions.

The year-to-date picture shows how fast the cushion thinned. The Block reports that the bitcoin funds' 2026 net inflows fell to 494.1 million dollars, from 1.18 billion a week earlier. So one bad week erased more than half of the year's accumulated net buying.

Ether is the weaker leg. Spot ether ETFs recorded 542.1 million dollars of net outflows. That is their worst week since January. The funds have now posted outflows for nine consecutive trading days.

Now the part that matters most for the thesis. Spot Solana ETFs ended a record fourteen-week inflow streak. They lost only 24.8 million dollars over the week, which is small. But the streak topped a thirteen-week run that began at launch and ended in late January. Solana was the last product still attracting steady marginal money. When the last holdout stops buying, a flow trend becomes a sleeve-wide event.

Two cautions before we read too much into this. First, one week of outflows is a tell, not a trend break. Flows like these can reflect basis-trade unwinds or rotation between products rather than a verdict on the asset class. Second, we have no on-chain, funding or options data this morning from Glassnode, Coin Metrics or Kaiko. So we are reading flows without the positioning data that would tell us who is forced and who is choosing.

On price, The Block put bitcoin near 82,900 dollars on Saturday morning. Treat that as approximate and dated. It is a secondary quote, not an exchange print, and Monday's open may differ. The takeaway is that bitcoin has held the low 80s while the ETF bid vanished, which means price has not yet capitulated to the flow signal.

So why now? The macro tape explains the timing. This is where the dollar and the long end come in.

The dollar index ended the week around 102.25. That completed a fourth consecutive weekly gain, per Profile News. A rising dollar tightens dollar funding at the margin. It also raises the hurdle for anything that has to be valued on distant cash flows or on sentiment.

The long end points the same way. The ten-year Treasury yield finished Friday at about 5.261 percent. It had touched a 24-year high of 5.364 percent earlier in the week. Sources differ on the exact close, and the Treasury's own par curve ran a bit lower, so treat the close as approximate and the high as an intraday touch.

Put those together and the argument is straightforward. A dollar that keeps rising and a ten-year above 5.2 percent raise the discount rate and tighten funding at the same moment. Crypto has been behaving like long-duration risk, not like a debasement hedge. The marginal dollar leaves the speculative sleeve first, because that is where the cash flows are most distant and the cushion is thinnest.

That leads to a second-order point about what crypto is correlated with. Gold is the contrast. Spot gold traded around 4,194 dollars late Friday, per Profile News. It held that level with a ten-year at 5.26 percent. Normally that combination should hurt gold, because real yields are the usual enemy.

Instead gold is being carried by a safe-haven and geopolitical bid. Brent settled at 104.72 dollars on Friday, up 0.42 percent, per the same source. Another outlet had Brent lower for the same session, so treat the level as approximate. The direction is what matters. Oil above 100 dollars keeps the geopolitical premium alive.

The implication is that the two alternative stores of value have decoupled. Gold trades on fear. Crypto trades on liquidity and discount rates. So when yields and the dollar rise together, gold holds and crypto bleeds. Crypto's equity-style behavior leaves it exposed to any further rate shock or any wobble in the AI complex.

Which brings us to the core, and the split that matters. The S&P 500 gained 0.59 percent on Friday to 7,811.51, a record-area close. So the core shrugs while the edge de-risks. That gap has been widening all week. It has not resolved.

There are two ways to read that gap. The benign reading says crypto is simply the most sensitive instrument in the system, and the index will follow only if rates keep climbing. The less benign reading says crypto is the canary. The edge reprices first, and the cap-weighted index catches up later.

I lean toward the second reading, but with humility. The reason is the rates engine, which deserves a brief reminder because the source is a primary one.

The September FOMC minutes, released last week, point to higher term premiums and Treasury yields. They name two drivers. One is uncertainty related to the Treasury's announcement and implementation of the buyback program. The other is heavy private debt issuance to finance the development of artificial intelligence infrastructure.

A caution on attribution. The extract does not make clear whether that language is the staff's characterization or the participants'. So do not over-attribute it to the Committee as a whole.

Still, the second-order link is worth stating. The AI buildout is competing with the Treasury for duration buyers. That keeps term premium elevated regardless of where the Fed goes next. And that is the same channel that raises the hurdle for crypto, for real assets and for private credit. Crypto is just the asset where the hurdle shows up first.

Now, what to watch this week, with no outcomes assumed.

Bank earnings begin. JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup and Wells Fargo will report third-quarter results. TradingKey notes that the recent sharp rise in long-term Treasury yields has put notable pressure on bank stocks. So the question is whether credit and trading commentary shows the edge stress reaching the core.

Then comes the September CPI report on Wednesday, October 14th, at 8:30 Eastern. It has not been released. Markets currently expect headline growth around 3.7 percent year over year, according to TradingKey. That is an expectation, not a result. And markets are reportedly leaning toward a Fed pause at the late-October meeting, which is a positioning statement, not a decision.

The asymmetry is the point. With the ten-year this high and the dollar still climbing, the sleeve that is already bleeding has the most to lose from a hot print. An inflation surprise would test that 5.364 percent high. A soft one would be the first real relief for long-duration assets.

To sum up. The weekend gave us the confirmation, not a new story. Crypto ETF flows reversed across bitcoin, ether and Solana together, roughly 1.25 billion dollars out in a week. A fourth weekly dollar gain and a ten-year above 5.2 percent explain why. Gold holding while crypto bleeds tells you which hedge is still working. The thing to track is whether the core keeps ignoring it.
