Cautious Optimism Briefings

Digital Money, Crypto, and Market Structure

Transcript · November 25, 2026

Good morning, it's Wednesday, November 25th, and here's where digital money stands. The selling has now reached the long-term holders who were supposed to be bitcoin's floor. The textbook bottoming signals are stacking up fast. But the firm calling this a capitulation says it's the first one of this bear market, not the last. So treat any bottom call with real caution.

On Monday and again yesterday, this desk flagged one thing to watch: whether November would pass June's record for spot bitcoin ETF outflows. On one tracker's count, it has. SoSoValue puts the month's net outflow at four point seven one billion dollars. That's past June's prior record of roughly four and a half billion. Monday did the damage. Per Farside data, five hundred seventy million dollars left the funds that day.

The concentration is the familiar part. BlackRock's IBIT accounted for five hundred nine million of Monday's outflow, roughly eighty-nine percent. This desk has watched IBIT dominate the biggest days all month, including about ninety-three percent of one session last week. There are two readings, and the data doesn't settle them. One is a single large holder rebalancing, which ends when the rebalancing is done. The other is that a rebalancer still selling this hard, in a record month, is behaving like an exit.

One real caveat on the record itself. Farside's own tally doesn't match SoSoValue's. From November 5th through Monday, Farside counted four point two seven billion dollars leaving the funds. Farside's window starts on the fifth, not the first of the month, so the two aren't a clean comparison. Still, the record is one tracker's number, not a figure both agree on. Either way, the wrapper that built the institutional-adoption bull case is having one of its worst months ever, with several sessions still to go.

The wrapper isn't the only long-horizon holder selling at a loss. Glassnode's weekly report says long-term holders' realized losses have almost doubled since last week. They are now the heaviest since the FTX collapse in November 2022. Glassnode calls this, in its own words, the first major capitulation event of this bear market. That word first matters. The same report says a further drop of about ten percent would not be unusual. That would take bitcoin down to its realized price, near fifty-three thousand two hundred dollars. In other words, the people measuring this capitulation are not calling a bottom.

There's also a complication worth naming plainly. Glassnode defines a long-term holder as someone who has held coins for about five months or more. That bar is lower than the label suggests. By our arithmetic, anyone who bought in late June or earlier now clears it. So today's long-term holders can include buyers from earlier this year, not just the multi-cycle holders the phrase implies. To be clear, that's our inference from the definition. Glassnode doesn't say which vintages are doing the selling. It does say recent buyers have taken heavy losses again as well. So capitulation is genuinely under way, and across cohorts. But it may be a weaker bottoming signal than the FTX comparison implies, because some sellers may simply be this year's buyers giving up.

Capitulation in spot has a mirror in derivatives. Glassnode's Market Pulse from Monday puts bitcoin futures open interest at twenty-nine point three billion dollars. That's down twelve percent on the week, and the lowest since early March. Other trackers cover different exchanges and print different levels, so treat Glassnode's as the single read. Separately, DWF Labs estimates about ten point seven billion dollars of longs were cleared over the past two weeks. That's an estimate, not an exchange-reported total. Three days ago, this desk flagged that the long book kept re-forming between flushes. This week it didn't rebuild. It shrank.

Options tell a similar story. Per Glassnode, the put-to-call ratio on open interest is the highest since May 2021. On volume, one point seven puts traded for every call on a seven-day average, the most since June 2022. There are two ways to read that. The bullish read is that heavy put buying can be spot holders insuring positions rather than selling them. A hedged, deleveraged market also has less fuel for a forced cascade. The bearish read is ours, from history. The last time put volume ran this heavy, the FTX collapse was still five months away. Both readings fit the same data. The practical takeaway, also our inference, is that leverage is no longer the accelerant it was in this month's earlier flushes. The next move is more likely to be set by spot flows, from the ETFs and long-term holders, than by liquidations.

The equity market is pricing the same retreat. Coinbase closed Tuesday at one hundred two dollars and thirty-five cents, just above the one-year low it set last Friday. That leaves the stock sixty-three percent below its peak from last December. And the read-through investors most want is one Coinbase no longer headlines. As this desk noted Saturday, its 10-Q says that since the second quarter, it no longer includes trading volume as a key metric. So treat the drawdown as the market pricing a slump in activity, not as a disclosed revenue hit.

On the on-chain cash side, the institutional showcase is shrinking too. Start with BlackRock's tokenized Treasury fund, known as BUIDL. A secondary data aggregator, Castle Labs, puts it at one point five seven billion dollars as of last Friday. That's about a thirteen percent share of tokenized Treasury funds. It's also below where this desk had BUIDL last month, though that earlier figure came from a different tracker. So the flagship fund is getting smaller, not bigger.

BlackRock is still widening the product line, though. According to Securitize's latest SEC filing, BUIDL now runs on an additional blockchain, called Tempo. BlackRock has also launched a second tokenized fund. It's a money market fund called BRSRV, designed to support stablecoin reserve management. We have no assets figure for it, and no sign yet that stablecoin issuers hold it. So the shelf keeps growing while the money in the flagship shrinks. That further weakens the rotation story this desk tested on Saturday, that tokenized Treasuries are absorbing the dollars leaving stablecoins. It also means any T-bill demand from that rotation is thinner than the adoption narrative implies.

So where does that leave the two cases? Start with the bull case, built only from confirmed facts. Long-term holder losses are running at FTX scale. Futures leverage is at its lowest since early March. Options are positioned for disaster. Bulls will argue that this stacked combination is what a washout looks like.

Now the skeptical case. The ETF outflow record is still running, and it rests on one tracker's count. The only source calling this capitulation calls it the first, and it flags a realized price about ten percent below spot. Some of the long-term holders selling may be this year's buyers underwater, not conviction breaking. And Coinbase, the clearest public proxy for crypto activity, is trading like it expects a long drought, not a turn.

Three things to watch from here. First, whether Tuesday's ETF print extends the record, and on which tracker. Second, whether bitcoin tests Glassnode's realized price, near fifty-three thousand two hundred dollars. Third, whether open interest rebuilds off this March-level low, or stays compressed.

And one date to mark. Strategy, the bitcoin treasury company that has been the largest steady corporate buyer in this market, is deleted from MSCI's indexes after the November 30th close. That means a forced index sale of its stock, whatever bitcoin itself does.

Bottom line: leveraged longs, the ETF wrapper, and now long-term holders have all shown up on the sell side this month. The purge is real in the data. But the people measuring it haven't declared it over, and some of the sellers may be newer money than the long-term holder label suggests.

That's digital money for November 25th. Talk to you tomorrow.
