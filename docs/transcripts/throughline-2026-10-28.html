Ten briefings shipped today. We'll start with the macro backdrop, move through the AI and compute stack, and close on the applied frontier.

Strategic Power says the contest over infrastructure has moved from building capacity to allocating it. PJM has previewed a one-time backstop auction for about 6.8 gigawatts of new supply. Hyperscalers, meanwhile, have contracted about 8.5 gigawatts of existing nuclear output, not new construction. The desk's point is that decisions which used to run through public policy now run through private contracts.

From physical allocation to financial pricing. That's the macro backdrop. Now the AI and compute stack, starting with the deal at the center of the day.

Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure supplies the mechanics behind Broadcom's Anthropic financing. Bank of America, Blackstone, Citigroup, and Morgan Stanley are funding it through a special-purpose vehicle. Broadcom is providing residual-value support that protects those lenders if Anthropic defaults. The desk reads that as lender caution more than vendor confidence, since the banks wanted the guarantee and reportedly secured higher rates too. So investors pricing Broadcom's AI revenue should start asking how much of it now carries a contingent guarantee.

Frontier AI Lab Competition takes the same deal and asks who holds the risk later. Its read is that this is a financing wrapper, not 63 billion dollars of new demand. The real test is a bond leg of up to 21 billion dollars that banks plan to sell as soon as early next year, and nothing has priced yet. Meanwhile, OpenAI is reported to be renting or buying Google TPU capacity, while keeping its roughly 600 billion dollar Nvidia commitment. So in the desk's view, leverage is moving toward whoever can design the chip and get it financed.

From the money behind the chips to the software running on them. AI Products and Applications has coding agents moving from writing code to running it. Cognition's Devin Oncall and Replit's Monitoring Agent both reached general availability in the past two weeks, to triage and fix live production issues. The product decision that matters isn't the model. It's where each vendor places the human approval gate, and for now a person still has to flip it.

So the market is pricing a security-spending conclusion that no quarterly report has confirmed.
