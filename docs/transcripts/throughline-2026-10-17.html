Good morning, this is The Throughline for Saturday, October 17th, 2026.

Today's headline: rates and the dollar are pricing a credit scare that the credit data itself hasn't confirmed, and that gap runs under most of today's briefings.

One disclosure up front. Anthropic makes the model behind this show, and it features in one of today's briefings, so weigh that one accordingly.

Nine briefings shipped today. AI Products and Applications, Private Equity and the Deal Economy, and AI in Biopharma R&D did not publish. We'll start with the macro backdrop, move into the AI and compute stack, then close with the applied frontier.

Strategic Power says PJM's spot price topped a thousand dollars a megawatt hour this week, and the grid declared a Maximum Generation Alert. PJM's own operators blame warm weather colliding with fall maintenance outages, and data centers were never ordered onto backup power. Separately, Taiwan announced a record defense budget above three percent of GDP, and China answered with live-fire drills that Taipei reads as rehearsal for a blockade, not an invasion.

From chokepoints to the capital pricing them. Capital Markets Cross-Asset Radar says Treasuries and the dollar spent the week betting that a credit scare will stay the Fed's hand. But the credit data doesn't confirm it yet. CCC junk spreads widened roughly two hundred basis points in four weeks, the widest since 2022, while the investment-grade index moved just six. CoreWeave's bonds, which pushed past a sixteen percent yield on Wednesday, are the single-name face of that bottom-tier stress, not proof it's spreading upward.

Digital Money sees the same split in crypto. Bitcoin broke below eighty thousand dollars on Thursday and was back above it within hours. But positioning data suggests fresh leverage built that bounce rather than spot demand, and ETF flows were flat to negative. Underneath, with its stablecoin float range-bound, Circle is buying Singapore payments firm Tazapay for four hundred million dollars, chasing payment volume instead of reserve growth.

Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle finds that the default rate has become a definitional choice. Moody's says counting PIK amendments and stressed maturity extensions in middle-market CLO loans lifts a reported four percent default rate to 5.8, echoing Fitch earlier this week. The stress still sits inside borrowers, not CLO tranches. And the two valves that would force it into the open, redemptions and loan trading, are both still in managers' hands.

Now the AI and compute stack, where the same split shows up as who pays versus what's scarce.

Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure says US chip stocks shed roughly two trillion dollars in value, by a Reuters estimate. The triggers were a hot inflation print, then a financing scare around the neocloud CoreWeave. Yet TSMC raised its growth forecast, ASML says 2027 and 2028 supply still won't meet demand, and no chip supplier has cut a forecast. The desk's read is that the market repriced who can pay for the buildout, not what's physically scarce inside it.

Frontier AI Lab Competition finds the big distributors working to make the labs beneath them replaceable. Microsoft finished pre-training an in-house base model it documents as several times more compute-efficient than DeepSeek's best open base model, on Microsoft's own evaluations. Its AI chief also reportedly told staff the model beats OpenAI's flagship, a claim no outsider has verified. Meanwhile Google is routing enterprise work across Gemini and Anthropic's Claude, and Meta's new open-weight model still trails Moonshot's Kimi on a key coding benchmark.

Enterprise Technology shows how fast that who-pays question flips in equities. Software names rallied Thursday on a rotation out of debt-funded AI infrastructure, then gave it back Friday, with the same financing worry cited for both moves. Oracle's problem is duration rather than demand, because OpenAI, its anchor customer, doesn't have to pay on the data center before 2027. And private investors just valued AI infrastructure startup Modal at about sixteen times run-rate revenue, with no disclosed margins.

Finally, the applied frontier, where most of the numbers are self-reported.

Robotics and Physical AI finds the sector's scale finally showing up as hard figures. Tesla says its Austin Cybercab fleet grew roughly eightfold in a month, and robot-brain startup Skild AI claims a hundred-million-dollar revenue run rate. But almost none of it is independently verified, and even two IDC-sourced figures for China's humanoid shipment share don't reconcile.

Space and the Orbital Economy closes the tour with Blue Origin asking the FAA to raise its Florida launch cap from twelve to fifty a year. That's for a rocket that hasn't flown since a May test explosion damaged its pad. With Rocket Lab's Neutron also slipping into 2027 this week, the desk's inference is that near-term national-security and constellation launches default to SpaceX.

Here's the thread worth carrying out. On Thursday, this show said credit stress was climbing from the weakest link toward investment grade, and on Friday it traced that ladder up to Oracle's record swap spread. Today's numbers show the broad investment-grade market hasn't followed. CCC spreads, CoreWeave's bonds, and bitcoin's leverage show real stress at the bottom of several stacks. But investment-grade credit, ETF flows, and chip suppliers' forecasts are still calm. The honest read isn't that the scare is over. It's that markets are pricing a contagion the data still confines to the edges.
