Good morning, this is The Throughline for Thursday, November 19th, 2026.

Today's headline: Anthropic priced its IPO above its range at 83 dollars a share, raising 81.3 billion dollars in the largest initial public offering on record, with shares due to begin trading today under the ticker ANT. A standing disclosure, since that headline touches this show directly: Anthropic makes the model behind this show.

We'll start with the macro backdrop. Strategic Power has Hormuz now priced as closed by the people who actually cross it. UK Maritime Trade Operations counted just 308 vessel crossings in the week ending November 11th, about sixty-five percent below pre-war levels, and that was before this week's wider exchange of strikes. The insurance cost per transit is disputed, but the desk reads war-risk cover as a tax on Gulf flows and a quiet subsidy to Atlantic and US supply.

From the Gulf to the tape, Capital Markets Radar finds the market buying new AI equity while selling the AI it already owns. The S&P 500 fell for a fifth straight session, and Nvidia dropped nearly three percent to its lowest close since June of last year, despite data-center revenue up 110 percent. Yet the ten-year yield actually eased after Governor Waller's hawkish call, so the discount-rate story the desk has leaned on all week no longer explains the selling on its own.

Digital Money turns to plumbing, and finds Coinbase still trading on the cycle, not on its new full federal trust charter from the OCC. Strategy's ratio is corrected today to about six dollars spent retiring preferred stock for every dollar put into bitcoin, down from the nearly seven reported yesterday. Most of the cash from Strategy's new common-share sales is going to those buybacks, so common holders are being diluted to defend the preferred stack, not to buy more coin.

Staying with credit, Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle has the Fed's Financial Stability Report adding, for the first time, a section on leverage in the data-center buildout. Debt issuance tied to advanced computing has run past 680 billion dollars, about five times 2023's pace, and the Fed says it still can't map who holds the riskiest slices. The twist, per Governor Barr, is that hyperscalers and chipmakers like Nvidia often guarantee the resale value of pledged GPUs, so the first direct losses on a default may land on them rather than on private lenders.

That's the macro backdrop. Now the AI and compute stack, with Anthropic's print at its center.

Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has Nvidia's earnings call filling in what the press release left out. Vera Rubin brought in nearly 20 billion dollars in its first shipping quarter, and licensed Hopper sales to China are now at zero, an assumption turned operating fact. Management also dated the margin bottom to this quarter, but sketched only a slow climb of about a point over the following year, and still hasn't named the cause.

Frontier AI Lab Competition notes that a meaningful slice of the IPO's demand came from strategic investors including Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and AMD, companies that sell Anthropic compute, compete with it, or both. One of those backers, Microsoft, launched its own coding model this week, MAI Code 2, claiming on its own benchmarks 96 percent of Claude Opus's coding performance at about a twentieth of the price. So the desk reads the IPO as winning Anthropic access to capital, not proof of pricing power.

AI Products has agent commerce going from a paywalled US feature to a free rollout across six countries in four days. Google's Tasks now beats OpenAI's Wallet on reach, but both lean on the same card networks. So the desk reads Visa and Mastercard, not the chatbots, as the real trust layer.

Enterprise Technology frames the week as a fight over which layer of the stack survives commoditized models. Salesforce is ceding the chat interface to Microsoft's Copilot while keeping the data permissions, and Workday is buying a semantic-layer company to own the arithmetic under the agent. Palo Alto Networks went the other way, agreeing to pay 4.5 billion dollars in stock for Applied Compute, a platform for running open-weight models, to buy straight into the model layer itself.

That's the compute stack. Now the applied frontier.

Robotics and Physical AI has permission moving in both directions at once. California pulled Waymo's driverless freeway service after its cars entered an active construction closure, with no return date, while Nevada cleared Uber's robotaxi partners for up to a thousand vehicles in Clark County. The desk's refinement is that permission isn't a one-way inflection anymore, but a two-way variable sitting on top of capability.

AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour with Alphabet and Nvidia shipping biology models through opposite distribution logic. DeepMind's AlphaGenome 2 reaches researchers through Google's API, and in a preprint not yet peer-reviewed it nearly doubled its predecessor's hit rate on one immune-cell benchmark. Nvidia's Nemotron for Science runs locally on a desktop box, which the desk reads as selling hardware and data sovereignty rather than a model subscription.

One thread worth naming before we close. Since last week, this show has watched three first public prices on AI risk: Nvidia's earnings, Anthropic's IPO, and ING's half-billion-dollar auction of data-center loans, the only one of the three priced in credit. Nvidia has reported, and Anthropic has priced. Today Anthropic's shares trade for the first time and the ING bids come in, though the auction result may take longer to surface.

That's The Throughline. Stay cautiously optimistic, and I'll talk to you tomorrow.
