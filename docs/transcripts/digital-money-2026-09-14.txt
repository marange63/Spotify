Good morning. It's Monday, September 14th, and this is your digital money briefing.

Start with Bitcoin, because it's the cleanest read on institutional risk appetite right now. Spot is sitting around seventy-seven thousand dollars. It tried to reclaim eighty thousand last week, got within a few hundred dollars of it intraday, and got turned back. Underneath that price action, US spot Bitcoin ETFs have now posted four straight sessions of redemptions running into September 11th. One caveat on every flow number you're about to hear. None of it comes from a primary issuer disclosure. It's aggregator data, the Farside and SoSoValue lineage, so attribute it rather than treat it as audited.

Put a number on the streak, because the arithmetic matters more than the headline. September is still net positive month to date, but only barely. Roughly three hundred and seven million dollars in net inflows across eight trading days, three up against five down. Now strip out one session. On September 3rd the group pulled in seven hundred and thirty-one million dollars in a single day, more than sixty percent of it into BlackRock's IBIT alone. Take that day out and the rest of the month is decisively negative.

This show flagged that concentration pattern on the 10th, with only five sessions of data, and called it one day making the month. Three sessions later the claim is stronger, not weaker. Back then, stripping September 3rd left the month roughly flat. Now it leaves the month several hundred million in the hole. The marginal buyer in this market is a wrapper that can swing by hundreds of millions of dollars on a single headline day. That is not the profile of a stable, sticky bid.

So what's driving the redemptions. The likely answer sits in the rates market, not in anything crypto-specific. Market-implied odds of a Federal Reserve rate hike at this week's meeting have moved sharply. Back in early August, after a weak July jobs report, hike odds sat at thirty to forty percent. Ahead of the August CPI print, they had climbed to around seventy. By last Friday and Saturday, CME FedWatch pricing put them in an eighty-five to eighty-seven percent band. That is a genuine repricing of the policy path, and it lines up almost exactly with the ETF outflow streak.

The mechanism is straightforward. A hike would be a hawkish surprise against the disinflation-and-cuts story that framed most of this year. Bitcoin is a high-beta, non-yielding asset with no cash flow to discount. It is exactly the kind of instrument that kind of repricing should hit first.

To be clear, this is inference, not proof. The FOMC has not met. The decision lands Wednesday afternoon. Nothing about a hike, a hold, or a cut is confirmed, and everything before that meeting is market-implied probability, not outcome. The thing to watch through it is IBIT's own creation and redemption pace. That is the cleanest live read on whether allocators are modelling Bitcoin as a rate-sensitive risk asset or as a diversifier, and it is not resolved yet.

Now the skeptic's case, on both halves of that argument. First, the rates story isn't the only explanation sitting in the same dataset. On September 11th, the same day Bitcoin ETFs were still redeeming, Ethereum ETFs pulled in two hundred and sixteen million dollars. That was their strongest single day of the week. It could be risk-off spilling out of crypto broadly. It could just as easily be rotation within crypto beta, money leaving Bitcoin wrappers for Ethereum ones for reasons that have nothing to do with the Fed. Flow data alone cannot separate those two stories. The honest test comes after Wednesday. If both wrappers move together, that points to a shared macro driver. If they diverge, that points to rotation.

Second, and this cuts against the obvious read. At roughly eighty-six percent implied, a hike is close to fully priced. That means the asymmetric surprise this week isn't the hike landing. It's the Fed not hiking. If you're looking for where Bitcoin could actually move sharply off this meeting, the bigger repricing sits on the low-probability side of that distribution. Don't assume high hike odds mean more downside ahead for crypto. In a fully-priced scenario, they mean most of that downside has already happened.

Now pivot, because the same rate move that's draining the Bitcoin ETF wrapper reads the opposite way one layer down the stack, in stablecoins.

Total stablecoin supply stood at three hundred and two point eight billion dollars as of September 10th, and it has contracted, down eight tenths of a percent over the trailing ninety days. Handle that figure with care. It's one aggregator's read, not DefiLlama or Coin Metrics directly, and rival trackers currently disagree on the headline number by tens of billions. What survives the disagreement is the direction. This pool is no longer compounding. That is a real plateau after a year of steady growth.

Inside it, Tether's USDT still dominates on raw size, at a hundred and eighty-three billion dollars, roughly sixty percent of the market. Circle's USDC sits at seventy-four billion, about twenty-four percent, and has been closing that share gap. Both operate under the GENIUS Act, the federal stablecoin law signed in July of last year. It requires one-to-one cash and short-Treasury reserves, monthly attestations, and, critically, it bars issuers from passing yield through to holders. One precision point. That regime is law, but it is not yet fully in force. The effective date is the earlier of roughly January 2027 or a hundred and twenty days after final implementing regulations.

The yield-sharing ban is the mechanism worth sitting with. Because issuers can't share yield with holders, every dollar of reserve income from a higher front end accrues to the issuer, not to the person holding the coin. And there's now a primary-source anchor for how real that income line is. Circle's own second-quarter results show reserve income of six hundred and sixty-eight million dollars, up five percent year over year. That's the striking part, because the rate it earned on reserves actually fell sixty-six basis points over that stretch, tracking SOFR down. Income still grew, because average USDC in circulation grew twenty-five percent. Volume did the work the rate couldn't. On the other side of the market, Tether's most recent quarterly attestation, through the end of June, shows net operating profit of about one and a half billion dollars, led by Treasury and repo income.

Two things follow. One: this confirms the mechanism qualitatively. Higher rates do lift issuer income. But Circle's own numbers show volume growth did more of the work than the rate level did this past year. So resist putting a dollar figure on what a hike this week would add. Neither issuer discloses a per-basis-point sensitivity, and nobody should invent one.

Two, and this is the sharper point. Because the overall pool is shrinking rather than growing, a higher front end from here is a margin story, not a volume story. Issuers earn more per dollar of float, sitting on fewer dollars of float. And the same ban that pads those margins caps the product's ability to compete for balances. A bank deposit or a money market fund can pay a holder. A GENIUS-compliant stablecoin cannot. A higher front end widens that gap at exactly the moment being paid matters most.

That also reframes the USDC-versus-USDT share shift. USDC gaining ground inside a pool that contracted is redistribution, not adoption. The bullish case for stablecoins as a growth story requires the pool itself to expand, and on this reading it isn't. There's a Treasury-market consequence too. Stablecoin reserves are a price-insensitive buyer of short-dated bills. A bid that has stopped growing matters for the front end at precisely the moment bill supply and the rate path are the market's central question.

Zoom out and the spine of this week is simple. On one side, the Federal Reserve sets the price of Bitcoin, transmitted through an ETF wrapper already showing four days of stress. On the other, the same rate move quietly pads the margins of the issuers underwriting the dollar plumbing beneath it. Neither question is settled. The Fed decides Wednesday, and the Senate's procedural vote on crypto market-structure legislation is still pending too. But whichever way this week lands, it will not land the same way on both halves of this market.
