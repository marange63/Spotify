Good morning, and welcome to Cautious Optimism Briefings for Sunday, November 22nd.

Start with a correction, because this desk owes you one. In Friday's briefing, the read here was that neutral funding meant no crowded long book was left. We said the marginal seller had rotated from levered futures traders to the spot ETF wrapper. And we floated a bull case that any further flush could be mechanically bounded. The tape since then says otherwise.

Bitcoin started Friday in the low sixty-thousands. Per Crypto Briefing, it hit an intraday low of $57,716 that day, its lowest price since September 2024. By Saturday it was near $55,750, down about ten percent in twenty-four hours, per a research note from Au79. Treat the exact prints as approximate. Trackers disagree by hundreds of dollars, sometimes thousands, depending on the hour.

Those prints sit below the liquidation band we flagged from Glassnode. One correction. Glassnode's November 17th report puts the top of that band at $62,200, a touch below the figure we gave you. It also puts the June low a little lower, at $58,500. Either way, bitcoin is now beneath the whole zone, though Glassnode hasn't published since the break. The levels flagged in advance did not hold.

Here's what broke Friday's read. Per the Au79 note, market-wide liquidations jumped 326 percent in twenty-four hours, to roughly $1.51 billion. Bitcoin longs alone accounted for $1.03 billion of that. Longs were the large majority of bitcoin's forced closures. That is not a small book.

And it isn't the first flush of that size. On November 13th, CoinDesk counted $1.64 billion of liquidations as bitcoin fell below $70,000. Two flushes of similar size, about eight days apart, say something specific. The long book keeps re-forming between flushes.

On the 16th, this desk said funding measures what leverage costs, not how much of it there is. On Friday, we forgot our own point. A calm funding rate after a liquidation wave tells you the price of leverage reset. It doesn't tell you the quantity did. We also can't tell you whether open interest fell through this flush. The trackers disagree too widely to quote. So dip-buyers re-levering is itself the risk, and a quiet funding print is a weak all-clear.

So who is the marginal seller, the ETF wrapper or levered futures? The honest answer is both, doing different jobs. Per TFTC, spot bitcoin ETFs shed roughly $3.5 billion over November's first fourteen trading sessions. That persistent redemption trend sets the direction. Leverage then sets how violently each level breaks once the trend is in place. There's no verified ETF flow data yet for Friday, so we can't say the wrapper sold into this leg. But our inference is that this combination has no natural stopping point. Every new low invites fresh leverage, and that leverage becomes fuel for the next flush.

Now look at what else traded Friday, because it shows how macro markets read this selloff. Per the Au79 note, the ten-year yield fell about ten basis points, to just under 5 percent, and the dollar index held firm near 103.7.

Meanwhile, the S&P 500 fell 3.70 percent, and bitcoin fell with it. Treasuries and the dollar did the hedging. Bitcoin traded like a risk asset, not a haven. That cuts against the case for bitcoin as an equity diversifier, at exactly the moment an allocator would want it to work. Still, one session is evidence, not a regime.

It also shifts the macro story. Earlier this month, the read here leaned on rates. Hike pricing in futures, and hawkish readings of the Fed minutes, were marking down a zero-yield asset. Friday doesn't fit that frame. Yields fell, and bitcoin fell anyway. Our inference is that this leg is being priced as risk-off, not as a rate shock. If so, rate relief alone may not rescue bitcoin.

Risk-off tends to find the most levered equity proxy first, and that brings us to Strategy. By CryptoTicker's calculation, Strategy's mNAV fell to 0.97 on Friday, with the stock at $81.03. In plain terms, the company is now worth less on the stock market than its own bitcoin. This desk has never been able to say that before. Treat it as one tracker's snapshot. Other methods give different readings, and this one predates the weekend's further leg down.

The mechanism matters. As CryptoTicker puts it, below an mNAV of one, selling new shares to buy bitcoin dilutes existing shareholders. So the engine that grew the coin stack, issuing stock above the value of the holdings, now runs in reverse. Strategy's bitcoin buying had already slowed this month. Below one, the marginal corporate bid goes quiet just as the ETF bid has turned negative.

Be clear about what this is not. It isn't a margin call, and nothing in the mNAV number forces a coin sale. Nothing in hand shows a fourth-quarter coin sale either. Strategy's disclosed coin sales ran through September, under its monetization program. This is a financing problem, not a forced-selling problem.

The balance-sheet damage has escalated fast. On November 10th, Strategy's stack sat near breakeven. By the 19th, Bitcoin Magazine estimated it about $9.7 billion underwater. At the weekend's low, by our arithmetic, the loss is about $16.8 billion, roughly a quarter below cost. Again, that's an accounting mark, not a solvency event.

Then there's the index event. MSCI deletes Strategy from its global indexes, including MSCI USA, after the close on November 30th. The only earlier estimate of the forced selling was a year-old JPMorgan figure. Now there's a fresh one. Per CoinDesk, BloFin Research estimates index-tracking funds must sell about 13 million shares. That's roughly 3.1 percent of shares outstanding.

Strategy's own analysis lands on the same share count, worth about $1.6 billion. Two cautions apply. It's an estimate of passive selling, not a measured flow. And one of the two sources is the company itself, which has an interest in the number. For scale, by our arithmetic, $1.6 billion is five to six weeks of the $283.8 million in common stock Strategy raised in a single week earlier this month. The point: a passive sale of that size lands on a stock that already trades below its own coin.

If there's a bullish case in this evidence, here it is. Two flushes totaling over $3 billion have now cleared, and the latest was dominated by longs. Treasuries and the dollar worked as hedges, rather than freezing up. That looks like a sharp deleveraging, not a systemic liquidity break. The MSCI overhang is dated, sized, and smaller than that year-old estimate. And below an mNAV of one, retiring common stock is accretive in principle. That last point is our arithmetic, not a company plan. What the bull case still lacks is a confirmed buyer. ETF flows past November 19th aren't verified, and Strategy's bid has gone quiet.

The bull case also has to get past a seller overhang. Glassnode's November 17th report put the short-term holder cost basis at $74,100. At the weekend's low, by our arithmetic, that cohort sits roughly 25 percent below it. That's a pool of supply waiting for any bounce, which is our inference, not a measured flow.

Three things to watch from here. First, ETF flows from Friday onward, once the data lands, and whether the wrapper is still redeeming. Second, whether Strategy's mNAV climbs back above one before the November 30th deletion, and whether its next filing shows buying, selling, or buybacks. Third, whether the long book re-levers a third time. If the selling extends, the next on-chain marker is Glassnode's realized price, which its November 17th report put at $53,700.

The bottom line: this break had two causes, not one. ETF redemptions set the trend, and a stubbornly rebuilding long book set the speed. And it landed hardest on the one equity that turned bitcoin's premium into leverage.

That's the briefing for today. Stay cautiously optimistic, and I'll talk to you tomorrow.
