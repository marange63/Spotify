Capital Markets Cross-Asset Radar

Transcript · October 11, 2026

Good morning, this is your Capital Markets Radar for Sunday, October 11th.

Here's the frame for today. The Fed hike did not disappear this week. It moved to December. Meanwhile, a near two-trillion-dollar deficit and a wave of AI borrowing are leaning on the same duration buyers. The bond market is pricing a higher-for-longer regime that equity volatility still refuses to price. And the weakest AI-linked credits are paying for that regime first.

Start with the policy path. CME FedWatch, in Yahoo Finance coverage dated October 7th, puts the odds of a December rate hike at 86 percent. On Thursday, this desk called the collapse in October-hike odds an over-read. This is why. The hike wasn't abandoned. It was deferred. One caution on that number. It's a single read, taken before Friday's oil move. Earlier soundings on the same contract reportedly ran several points lower, though we couldn't verify them. So treat it as directional, not a stable consensus. The so-what is simple. The market still expects this Fed to tighten again. It has only stopped betting on the timing.

Now the supply side of the same debate. On Thursday, this desk said the Fed's own minutes reopened the term-premium question. This week loaded both sides of that scale at once. Last fiscal year's federal deficit came in at 1.993 trillion dollars. And per the same Yahoo Finance coverage, Treasuries are competing with roughly 489 billion dollars of debt issued to build AI infrastructure. Set that against the December hike pricing. The policy-path camp has its number. The term-premium camp now has numbers of its own. Two cautions apply. A deficit print is backward-looking, and term premium responds to forward issuance, which we don't have. And that AI figure comes from a different source and cutoff than the hyperscaler data coming up, so don't add them together.

There's also a credible counter-argument on the AI channel, and it deserves airtime. PIMCO's Credit Market Lens research, published in late September, ran an event study around large AI debt deals. It found that surprise AI issuance does not produce a statistically significant increase in Treasury yields. It found no significant increase in term premia either. And it found little systematic response in swap spreads. PIMCO's public commentary on this question hasn't been uniform this year. But this is its most rigorous statement, and it cuts against the easy narrative. So the crowding-out story is contested, not settled. Hold it loosely.

That brings us to the test this desk has set all month. Can the long end fall on calming news? Friday delivered the first genuine calming input. Per Forbes, Brent fell just under one percent on Friday, to $102.78 a barrel. Providers quote different contract months, so treat the level as approximate. The direction is what matters here.

So did yields fall? They did not. Treasury's own daily par yield curve has the 10-year closing at 5.22 percent on Thursday and 5.24 percent on Friday. The 2-year rose too, from 4.75 to 4.80 percent. The 30-year simply held flat.

The test failed, at least on Friday's data. Yields ticked up while the main inflation input cited for this selloff cooled. Two cautions keep that from becoming an over-read. One session is a small sample. And a small dip in Brent is a premium trim, not barrels restored. Yesterday, this desk reported Hormuz flows still running about 30 percent below pre-war. Nothing in Friday's tape reverses that. Still, the takeaway holds. Whatever is driving the long end is sturdier than the oil premium alone. That points back to the hike repricing, and, with the PIMCO caveat attached, to supply. The second-order cost lands on duration-heavy equity. A higher hurdle rate hits the furthest-dated cash flows hardest, and the AI trade is built on exactly those.

If AI borrowers are part of the supply story, the place to see their financing cost is their own credit. Start at the top of the ladder, with the hyperscalers. Per S&P Global data, they issued about 115 billion dollars of debt in the first quarter alone. That compares with roughly 70 billion for all of last year. That is a buildout being financed in the bond market, not just out of cash flow.

Spreads have widened, but only modestly. Short-dated hyperscaler paper moved to 40 basis points over Treasuries, from 30 last year. Intermediate paper moved to 60, from 50. Be clear about the dates. That spread and issuance data runs through August 10th, so this is stock-taking, not this week's news. But it carries a tell. As of late July, 78 of this year's hyperscaler bonds were trading at higher yields than at issue. For the strongest balance sheets in the buildout, the toll is real but small. Volume is surging, while the price of that volume barely moves.

Now go down the ladder, to the leveraged counterparty funding the frontier labs. SoftBank priced an 11.1 billion dollar high-yield bond on September 24th to fund its OpenAI commitments. Per Bloomberg and LSEG data, it's the largest junk-bond sale on record, topping a Numericable deal from more than a decade ago. The longest tranche carries a 9.75 percent coupon. CreditSights said the risks to SoftBank's credit are material and have increased. It pointed to rising concentration and material strain on cash flow.

So here's the ladder. The hyperscalers borrow at a few dozen basis points over Treasuries. The leveraged partner bankrolling OpenAI pays close to ten percent. Note, too, that SoftBank's record deal cleared. Like Thursday's thirty-year reopening, demand is there at a price. The over-read is that AI credit is cracking. The better read is that the market is pricing counterparty concentration, not hyperscaler solvency. It echoes the CCC widening this desk flagged yesterday, while the broad high-yield index stayed calm. That's dispersion, not contagion.

Two implications follow, and both are inference on my part. First, if direct issuance keeps getting dearer, hyperscalers have more reason to push AI financing onto vendors, special-purpose vehicles and leveraged partners. Second, SoftBank's credit is now a leveraged proxy on OpenAI's commitments. A single lab counterparty sits behind a record junk deal. So what: stress in the AI buildout prices at its weakest financing link first, and that link is concentrated.

Credit is pricing the regime at the edges. Volatility markets show the same split at the core. The MOVE index of Treasury volatility peaked at 113.60 on October 5th, per Streetstats. Three days later it was still at 100.70. Equity volatility never came close. The VIX, which this desk cited yesterday, closed both Thursday and Friday below 16.

Now layer on valuation. The S&P 500's Shiller CAPE ratio sits near its highest level since early 2001. That gives you two readings of the same tape. In the first, earnings have been resilient enough that equities are rationally shrugging off a bond-market regime shift. Low equity vol is defensible if the bank prints and the AI names keep delivering. In the second, equities are absorbing a higher discount rate with the least valuation cushion in a quarter century, and haven't priced it yet. A gap between bond vol and equity vol can persist for months, so it times nothing on its own. The so-what: bonds are pricing a regime, while equities are pricing a string of one-off shocks.

One counter-signal sits on the real-asset side. Copper jumped 2.21 percent on Friday to $6.66 a pound, per Trading Economics. One reading is that power and infrastructure exposure is being bid while rate-sensitive assets struggle. That AI-and-power link is my inference, not a quoted driver. Copper may simply have rallied with a risk-on tape and softer oil. Treat it as a reading, not a confirmed rotation.

So, what to watch, with no outcomes assumed. JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup and Wells Fargo report on Tuesday. That's the first test of whether higher funding costs are showing up in credit costs. September CPI follows on Wednesday, and PPI on Thursday. The Fed decides on the 28th. The single thread through all of it: does that December hike pricing survive a softer oil tape, or does it fade the way October's odds already did?

That's the radar for today. Trade carefully, and I'll see you back here tomorrow.
