Good morning, this is your Capital Markets Radar for Friday, November 27th.

US cash markets were shut Thursday for Thanksgiving. So Wednesday's close is still the last tape we have, and a shortened Friday session is ahead. Yesterday this briefing focused on the bond market looking past a hot inflation print. But the more important Wednesday news was somewhere else. A rating agency moved to price governance risk at Nvidia.

Start there. S&P Global Ratings placed Nvidia's double-A issuer rating on CreditWatch negative. To be plain, that is a watch, not a downgrade. S&P's language, as reported in the press, is blunt: "recent events undermine our view of NVIDIA's M&G framework." M&G means management and governance. The events are Jensen Huang's brief departure and reinstatement as chief executive. Nvidia's board has said that reinstatement followed input from senior U.S. government officials.

All week, the worry around Nvidia has been key-man risk in the heaviest name in the index. On Wednesday, S&P carried that risk into a credit rating. What makes this non-consensus is the backdrop. Nvidia just posted a record 115.8 billion dollars in revenue, per a Yahoo Finance headline. This is a rating action on a company whose income statement is not the problem.

So treat this as a signal, not a funding event. Per CNBC's reporting, S&P says any downgrade would be limited to one notch, to double-A-minus. S&P also put a 90-day window on resolving the watch. The real tail sits in what the review could turn up. S&P pointed to questions about potential export-control violations. It also flagged internal controls around Nvidia's exposure to China. If those questions harden into findings, a governance footnote becomes a legal and fundamental credit event.

Moody's is not following, at least not yet. Per Newsquawk, Moody's kept Nvidia at double-A-one with a stable outlook. It said the reinstatement "restores strategic continuity but leaves some governance questions unanswered." So the two big agencies do not yet agree that this risk warrants a rating action. That split argues against reading S&P's move as a consensus credit view.

The derivatives market leans the same way. Nvidia's five-year credit default swaps traded near 143 basis points early on Tuesday, the day before S&P acted. That was close to a record for the name. But it is a long way from distress pricing. And because it predates the watch, it mostly reflects the reinstatement drama, not the rating action. Set that against the stock. Nvidia closed Wednesday roughly 2.3 trillion dollars short of its October peak in market value. The equity has done the heavy repricing, while credit has barely flinched by comparison. So the headline isn't that Nvidia is suddenly a worse credit. It's that a rating agency is now pricing who controls the company, not what it earns.

There's a second-order channel here, and it's this desk's inference. Apollo's Marc Rowan has described Nvidia as a guarantor for the neoclouds. His warning was that funding for a large number of them disappears if that function is taken away. So Nvidia's governance risk now has a credit channel as well as an equity one. Any dent in its perceived credit standing weakens that implicit backstop for the neoclouds.

If a rating agency is pricing governance at the top of the quality stack, it's worth asking what credit is pricing at the bottom. The answer is dispersion, not panic. The ICE BofA CCC-and-lower option-adjusted spread stood at 13.87 percent on November 23rd. A year earlier, it was 9.20 percent. Yet Vanguard's read is that double-B and single-B spreads have stayed relatively contained. The same pattern has already shown up at the deal level, where AI data-center debt has been pricing by tenant. Now that discrimination shows up by rating tier.

Push on the obvious readings, though. A CCC blowout in a quarter of strong growth is not a recession signal. Our inference is that it's a rates story wearing a credit costume. A hiking Fed squeezes refinancing for the weakest balance sheets first. So credit is sorting, not breaking. The tell to watch is migration into single-B. If that comes, this stops being a bottom-tier story.

Why would the weakest credits crack in a quarter this strong? Look at the rate path. The BEA's second estimate of third-quarter GDP was revised up 0.9 of a percentage point from the advance estimate. The agency said that "primarily" reflected upward revisions to investment and consumer spending. This is a second estimate, and a third follows in December. Together with Wednesday's hot core PCE print and a Fed survey pointing to sticky inflation, the revision hardened rate pricing. Per Reuters, markets now expect a quarter-point hike in December and another in early 2027.

Track how far that has moved. December hike odds were 67 percent on November 5th, per CME FedWatch. One tracker now puts them at 97.8 percent. In three weeks, a likely hike has become a near-certain one.

Now look inside the growth number. In our read, that investment-led upgrade is substantially the AI capex boom. It's the same boom that equity and credit markets are now charging a premium to fund. So strong backward-looking growth and rising AI-financing risk are two readings of the same fact. And a Fed hiking into an investment boom raises the floor on refinancing costs exactly where the CCC stress already sits.

The same sort runs all the way out to the most speculative end of markets. Spot bitcoin ETFs logged about 5.39 billion dollars of net outflows this November, per SoSoValue data cited by The Block. That surpasses the previous monthly record, with trading days still left.

Wednesday stretched the outflow streak to seven straight sessions. BlackRock's IBIT accounted for 329.7 million dollars of that day's 453.1 million, nearly three quarters. Flow that concentrated can mean a few large holders rebalancing rather than a broad exit. Either way, the regulated wrapper is still the marginal seller.

Price has now tested a named line. Galaxy treats a daily close below about 58,500 dollars, the June 30th closing low, as the start of a move toward a materially lower low.

On Wednesday, bitcoin fell as low as about 57,300 dollars, more than a thousand dollars under that line. That was an intraday breach. But Galaxy's trigger is a daily close, and we have no confirmed close below the line. By early Thursday, bitcoin was back near 60,500, about two thousand dollars above Galaxy's level. That bounce came in thin holiday trading, so don't read much conviction into it.

Stablecoins tell a related story. Dollar stablecoin supply has fallen 20.7 billion dollars so far this year, per Crypto Briefing. November posted the steepest monthly drop since 2022. One explanation is rotation. Higher bill yields could be pulling idle cash into yield-bearing tokenized money-market funds. HSBC, for instance, opened a tokenized Hong Kong dollar money-market fund to retail clients on November 22nd, on its RedCoin platform. That's a genuine structural marker for tokenized finance.

But a simple size test says rotation can't be the whole story. The entire tokenized money-market fund complex holds only about 17.1 billion dollars, per Coutts. That's roughly six percent of stablecoin supply. And it's everything those funds hold, not just what flowed in this year. So even if every dollar in them had left stablecoins this year, rotation still couldn't account for all of the 20.7 billion dollar drop. Some of the rest may be trading firms cutting leverage. Either way, a meaningful share of the cash is leaving the rails, not migrating within them. A smaller float also trims issuers' Treasury bill demand at the margin. That's a small effect, not a driver of the front end. The bigger point is that the marginal buyer is retreating exactly where liquidity is thinnest. That's one reason this week's bitcoin bounce looks fragile rather than durable.

Pull the thread together. Markets are rewarding strong growth less than they are punishing weak governance and weak refinancing positions. None of the next tests has happened yet. November payrolls land December 4th, and the Fed decides on the 9th. November CPI follows a day later, and S&P's Nvidia review has yet to resolve. The question to carry into next week is whether this quality sort stays confined to CCC credit and crypto. If it climbs into single-B, a sorting exercise becomes something the whole market has to price.

That's the radar for this Friday. Stay cautious, stay optimistic, and have a good weekend.
