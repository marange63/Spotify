Good morning. It's Friday, September 4th, and this is your digital money briefing.

Start with the stall. Bitcoin held near seventy-seven thousand two hundred dollars on September 3rd, after its late-August recovery failed to establish support above eighty thousand. That failure has a concrete ceiling attached to it. Glassnode data shows long-term holder supply concentrated between eighty-three and eighty-six thousand dollars, and that band is exactly where the mid-August short squeeze ran out of road. This is not a vague resistance line drawn on a chart. It is a specific, falsifiable marker. Until Bitcoin clears that band, the rally has not broken out. It has stalled beneath a known wall of coins that someone else already owns at a lower price.

Now the flow data, and here a callback is worth making explicit. Yesterday this show reported a single day's spot Bitcoin ETF outflow of two hundred and thirty-six point five million dollars on September 1st, and called it a data point to watch, not an established trend. September 2nd answered that. Flows reversed to a net inflow of one hundred and one million the very next session. That is a swing of roughly three hundred and forty million dollars inside twenty-four hours. One provenance note before anyone builds a thesis on it. These are aggregator fund-level figures, not issuer disclosures, so treat the direction as solid and the decimals loosely. And both numbers are small against the roughly three and a half billion dollars this complex took in across August. Neither day is a regime change on its own.

So the honest read is not that demand turned, and it is not that demand snapped back either. It is that ETF flows have become fast, two-way and price-sensitive. Look at the composition of the reversal and that reading gets stronger. BlackRock's IBIT added one hundred and fifteen million dollars, while Grayscale's GBTC bled out fifty-six million on the same day. A meaningful share of that gross inflow is Grayscale's long-running structural outflow washing through the total, not fresh institutional allocation. That matters for valuation, not just for narrative. The permanent bid framing is what bulls have been using to justify a higher price floor, on the theory that ETF demand absorbs selling rather than transmits it. A day like September 2nd says the opposite. The wrapper made this market's marginal buyer faster and more reactive, not stickier. If you were discounting a structural bid into your downside case, that support is not there.

There is a supply-side mechanism behind the stall too, and it cuts against how it is usually described. Glassnode also found sixty-eight percent of Bitcoin's supply sitting in profit near current prices, against sixty-five percent back in May when Bitcoin traded around similar levels. That is not a bullish statistic on its own. More coins in profit at a comparable price than three months ago means a larger cohort of holders now has the option to sell into any push toward that eighty-three to eighty-six thousand dollar band. That is the actual mechanism behind the stall. More potential sellers are sitting exactly where the price needs to go. So if you want to know whether this rally has room, watch the profit-supply ratio rather than the flow headline.

If the Bitcoin bid has turned two-way, the natural question is whether anything in this market is still taking one-directional money. That brings us to Ether. US spot Ether ETFs pulled in eighty-seven point six million dollars on September 1st, which extended their streak of net inflow sessions to eleven straight. Eleven consecutive sessions is the closest thing to one-way flow anywhere in this complex right now. One correction to this show's record while we are here. That eighty-seven point six million is a better-sourced figure than the altcoin fund numbers I quoted yesterday, and it is larger than them.

The size objection still survives. Set eighty-seven point six million against the roughly three hundred and forty million dollars the Bitcoin complex swung across September 1st and 2nd alone, and Ether's best day is still a fraction of Bitcoin's noise. Press coverage describes back-to-back record weekly Ether inflows, the strongest of the year, alongside a climb from around nineteen hundred dollars to above twenty-five hundred in late August. Those weekly totals are press-reported and unconfirmed to this show's standard, so I will not quote you a number. And a record measured against Ether's own small base can still be immaterial next to Bitcoin.

The more plausible explanation for eleven green sessions is not allocators rotating out of Bitcoin wrappers. It is flow chasing a price move that already happened. That is momentum following the asset, not an allocation decision preceding it. What would settle it is a confirmed weekly Ether inflow that exceeds the Bitcoin complex's swing over the same window, and no such number exists yet. Until then, treat the rotation story as unproven.

That raises a harder question for the sector. If the Bitcoin bid is two-way, and the Ether bid looks like beta, what in this market actually earns money regardless of which coin is bid? Coinbase's July print is five weeks old and is not news, but it is the cleanest settled evidence available. Net revenue excluding Bitcoin spot trading was eighty-eight percent of the total last quarter. The sharper marker of the shift is the subscription line. Subscription and services revenue was forty-eight percent of net revenue, up from twenty-nine percent in the fourth quarter of 2024. Prediction markets revenue more than doubled quarter over quarter. On paper, that is genuine diversification away from Bitcoin-price sensitivity.

Look inside that subscription line, though, and the diversification story gets complicated. Average USDC held in Coinbase products hit an all-time high of twenty billion dollars last quarter, more than thirty percent of all USDC in circulation. That balance generates yield-share income tied directly to short-term interest rates. It is a rate rent, not a subscription. So Coinbase has not removed its exposure to a single macro variable. It has swapped one for another. Bitcoin-price beta became front-end-rate beta. And the swap is not obviously a win yet, because the quarter that produced those diversification numbers still posted a three hundred and fifty-nine million dollar net loss, and the loss driver is not disclosed. Diversified is not the same as profitable.

That swap also means this sector no longer trades as one position. Higher short-term rates raise the opportunity cost of holding an asset that pays no yield, which is a headwind for Bitcoin itself. Those same higher rates directly lift the yield-share income that is now the largest growth line at the sector's biggest listed exchange. A Federal Reserve cutting cycle flips both signs at once. It would compress the rate rent while improving Bitcoin's competitiveness against cash. The practical consequence is that the asset and the equity exposed to this theme have stopped being the same trade, and the pair of them is now closer to a rates spread than to a single crypto beta. Anyone still sizing them as one position is carrying a hedge they did not intend.

Which brings us to today. The August payrolls report lands at eight thirty this morning, Eastern time, and as of this recording it has not been released. I will not characterize the print or the market's reaction, because neither has happened. But it is worth naming as the live catalyst, because it is a front-end rate signal, and the front end is now the variable that moves both halves of this sector in opposite directions on the same morning. A weak print and the rate rent gets marked down while the coin gets a tailwind. A strong one and the pair moves the other way.

That's your briefing for September 4th. Have a good weekend.
