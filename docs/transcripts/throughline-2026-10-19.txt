Good morning, this is The Throughline for Monday, October 19th, 2026.

Today's headline: hedge funds reversed all of their AI stock buying since the start of 2025 in a single week, but Goldman's prime book shows it was de-grossing, not conviction shorting, and that book is still levered near five-year extremes.

Six briefings shipped today, weighted toward the macro desks. Private Credit, Semiconductors and Compute, Frontier AI Labs, Enterprise Technology, Robotics and Physical AI, and Space and the Orbital Economy did not publish. We'll start with the macro backdrop, move to AI products, and finish on the applied frontier in biopharma.

Strategic Power leads with oil, where Brent touched $110 a barrel on Friday on a real physical shortfall, with Kpler putting Hormuz crude flows at only about seventy percent of their pre-war average. Washington keeps loosening on Russian energy anyway, extending Lukoil's overseas retail licenses into 2027, so it is spending sanctions credibility while the chokepoint still sets the price. On the grid, PJM's independent market monitor says forty-two percent of forecast data-center load growth in a window through 2029 has been canceled or delayed, yet ratepayers are still paying firm-capacity prices built on that forecast.

From the physical flows to the financial ones. Capital Markets Cross-Asset Radar has the day's lead: hedge funds net-sold a global AI stock basket for five straight sessions, with long sales outpacing shorts three and a half to one. That ratio makes it de-grossing rather than fresh bearish bets, so a clean AI-infrastructure print could pull capital back fast, while leverage near five-year extremes keeps the downside live too. Friday's tell was the VIX jumping roughly thirty percent on an unconfirmed rumor of an AI-fund blowup, which the desk infers means tight dealer risk limits can now turn a whisper into a systemic trigger.

The same crowding question carries into crypto. Digital Money finds bitcoin boxed between an $81,500 ETF cost-basis floor and an $88,000 wall where six-to-twelve-month holders break even. The inside of that range is rented by leveraged longs paying nearly eleven percent annualized funding, not owned by buyers who don't care about price. The owners are stepping back: spot ETFs have turned net sellers, giving back roughly a third of their late-September inflow, and Strategy spent about nine times more last week buying back its own preferred stock than buying bitcoin.

Private Equity and the Deal Economy adds a fourth signed take-private to the show's weekly tally, and this one is a full-price entry rather than a distressed scoop. It's EQT's binding deal for Australia's Cleanaway Waste Management, at roughly nine point three billion Australian dollars of enterprise value, with a premium measured against a clean, undisturbed base. Against those four, the desk still counts zero signed third-party exits, and CVC's ten-billion-dollar secondaries close shows fresh capital concentrating at the top rather than loosening distribution, which makes Blackstone's third-quarter print this Thursday the next real test.

From capital flows to the AI products beat. AI Products and Applications covers two pieces of agentic infrastructure that shipped as open standards, starting with Google's Universal Cart, now live for US users, which keeps products found across Search and Gemini in one persistent cart with checkout on an open protocol. The desk's read is that Google opened the rails but kept the station, since the cart, the discovery surface, and the monitoring stay on its own properties, and no merchant count has been disclosed. Perplexity went further by open-sourcing its SPACE sandbox, which saw no VM escape across 108 red-team runs, though four models bypassed its network controls before a patch, and the testing was Perplexity's own.

Finally, the applied frontier, where the gap between headline and proof is sharpest. AI in Biopharma R&D covers Recursion's REC-4881 data in familial adenomatous polyposis, where eighty-seven percent of patients cut their polyp burden and most stayed below baseline twelve weeks after dosing stopped, though the trial is open-label, with sixteen evaluable patients and no disclosed control arm. The bigger caveat is the AI claim: REC-4881 is a Takeda molecule from around 2006, so the platform proved indication selection on known chemistry, not new biology. And on predicting how cells respond to gene perturbations, the desk's standing reference still finds foundation models failing to beat simple linear baselines.

Here's the thread worth carrying out of today. Three of the macro desks asked who is actually holding the position, and got the same answer. In AI stocks, the book is lighter but still crowded. In bitcoin, leverage is renting the range. In private equity, capital keeps going in while exits stay at zero. The money is still in each trade, and none of them has yet shown it can get out cleanly, which is why Thursday's Blackstone print and any sign of prime-broker re-grossing are this week's tells.

That's The Throughline. Have a good Monday.
