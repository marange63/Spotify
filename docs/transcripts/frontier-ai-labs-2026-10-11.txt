Good morning, it's Sunday, October 11th. This week Claude started showing up inside rivals' products, and the first independent scores arrived to test whether it deserves the seat.

Start with Elon Musk. Nine to Five Mac reports that as of October 7th, Grok Bot runs Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 for its hardest tasks. Grok Bot is the consumer AI app from SpaceXAI, Musk's merged SpaceX and xAI entity. For disclosure, Anthropic makes the model behind this show. One correction to our own coverage: yesterday we called Grok Bot upcoming and treated the routing as a plan, and the reporting says it is already live.

Now the evidence, because it's thinner than the headline. The entire public record is one post from Musk. It reads, in full: "Going forward, SpaceX will use the best back end model for any given task, including Claude Opus 5.5, MidJourney, Suno and other leading APIs. Whatever is most likely to give you the best outcome." That's it. The AI blog CellCog checked and found no SpaceXAI news post and no entry in the Grok Bot changelog. As CellCog also notes, the post doesn't say when routing starts, or which tasks go to which vendor. Nobody has disclosed what share of traffic reaches Claude, or on what commercial terms. So "Grok Bot runs Claude" is a credible report resting on a single post. It is not a documented product change. It could turn out to be a narrow slice of tasks presented as a headline.

There's also a publicity reading worth holding. Announcing a best-of-breed router lets Musk frame a lagging in-house model as a sophisticated product choice. The competing read is more generous. A model-agnostic consumer app is a sound strategy for a company whose real advantages are capital and distribution, not weights. Both readings can be true at once.

Either way, the routing rule cuts both ways for Anthropic. Musk's standard is whichever model works best, including a rival's. That standard is what let Claude in. The same standard puts Claude's seat up for contest at every new release from OpenAI or Google. My inference is that more of Anthropic's revenue may start arriving wholesale, through other companies' surfaces. If so, its pricing power depends on staying on top of someone else's routing evaluation, not on owning the user. Winning a routing slot is distribution. It is not ownership of the customer.

There's a second layer that complicates the easy "xAI concedes defeat" story. Back in May, SpaceXAI signed an agreement giving Anthropic access to its Colossus 1 supercomputer. So these two companies already did business before Musk's post. Anthropic buys compute from SpaceXAI. Now, reportedly, SpaceXAI's consumer app buys intelligence from Anthropic. The relationship runs in both directions.

Add one more piece from earlier this week. SpaceX is reported to be in early talks to raise debt to buy Nvidia chips for xAI. So the same Musk stack is lining up enormous compute, while its most visible consumer product leans on a rival's model for the hard work. Compute access and model leadership are separate competitive dimensions. This is the clearest case yet of a company strong on one and, for now, behind on the other.

The obvious follow-up is whether the big platforms are doing the same thing on the enterprise side. They are, but in a more limited form than some reporting this week suggested. Google's own release notes show Claude Opus 5.5 arriving in Google Cloud's Model Garden catalog on September 22nd. Claude Sonnet 5.5 followed on September 28th. The entry reads simply: "Claude Opus 5.5 is available in Model Garden." That's routine cloud resale. It's the same channel that put Anthropic's new small model, Haiku 5.5, on all three hyperscalers on launch day.

What doesn't check out is the stronger claim. Some reports had Claude becoming selectable inside Gemini Enterprise, Google's own business assistant, on October 8th. Google's release notes have no such entry. We found no primary evidence that Gemini Enterprise users can pick Claude at all.

The more telling Google data point is a different one. This week Anthropic opened a beta of Claude inside Google Workspace, installed through Google's Marketplace, with Google's admins holding the switch. Put those two facts together and a shape emerges. Google lets Claude in at the catalog and marketplace layers, where Google collects the platform economics. My inference is that Google is guarding the layer that matters most, which is the model picker inside its own assistant. The platforms keep the customer relationship and rent the model. That pushes the model layer toward a contestable input rather than a moat.

Which brings us to the question underneath all of this. If routers and catalogs pick whichever model works best, who decides what works best? That's where this week's first independent numbers come in.

Until now, Anthropic's benchmark claims for Haiku 5.5 had no outside check. Haiku 5.5 is priced at ten cents per million input tokens, roughly a ninety percent cut from the prior Haiku. Anthropic calls it "the cheapest, fastest, and most capable small model we've ever released." Now there's an outside number. Artificial Analysis, the third-party benchmarking firm, scores Haiku 5.5 at 43 on its Intelligence Index.

Here's how to read that 43. On the same index, Mistral's new flagship, Large 4 Preview, scores 38. So Anthropic's ten-cent small model outscores a European lab's top model by five points. That is the sharpest fact in this segment. My read is that the price of a given level of capability is still falling fast. And that squeezes any lab whose pitch rests on its top model alone.

Still, it's a real receipt, but a partial one. Anthropic's launch claimed that Haiku beats OpenAI's small model, GPT-6 Luna, on a computer-use benchmark. A composite index score doesn't replicate that specific head-to-head. And Artificial Analysis is one aggregator, with its own task weighting. Indices already disagreed this week about Google's Gemini 4 Argon. One leaderboard, the Vals Index, ranked Argon first, while Artificial Analysis only placed it level with an OpenAI model. So call the 43 evidence, not a verdict. The customer results in Anthropic's launch materials remain self-reported, and we're not counting them as evidence at all.

Back to Mistral, because the same aggregator gave it a title. Artificial Analysis says France is back to having the most intelligent model from outside the US and China. Its 38 also sits well above the median of 26 for comparable models. But notice what that title actually measures. It's a geographic ranking, not a frontier one. By definition it excludes the US labs and the Chinese labs, including Chinese open-weight models that may sit in the same tier. It's also a Preview score, with Mistral's weights and license still due by month's end. The sovereignty story is real. The capability story is modest.

Here's the larger point from both scores. Once routers choose by performance, third-party scorekeepers become a gate to the router slot. That raises the stakes on whose index buyers trust, at a moment when the indices still disagree with each other.

So where does that leave OpenAI? Mostly on the sidelines this week. We found no new OpenAI announcement in the last several days. Its newest shipped model is still GPT-6.1 Sol, launched at its developer conference on September 29th. OpenAI isn't named in Musk's router post. And its small model, Luna, is the one Anthropic's computer-use claim targets, vendor-reported as that is. OpenAI didn't clearly lose ground this week. But it sat out the distribution shift that defined it.

One line on capital, because it breaks a pattern. Meta had been quiet all week. Now Meta resurfaced, though through a paid-partnership interview rather than an earnings call or a filing. Meta's infrastructure chief said the company will spend "well over $100 billion this year alone." Take the source caveat seriously. Still, it puts Meta on the capex dimension, not the capability one. Capital commitments keep climbing even for labs without a clear model-quality win right now.

So here's the scorecard by dimension. On distribution, Anthropic gained ground, as rivals' surfaces carry Claude. On customer ownership, the platforms doing the hosting keep it, whether that's Musk's app or Google's cloud. On verified capability, the receipts are partial and still contested. And on capex, spending keeps rising regardless of who leads the leaderboard this week.

Three things to watch. First, any SpaceXAI or Anthropic confirmation of how much Grok Bot traffic actually routes to Claude. Second, independent replication of Haiku 5.5's computer-use claims. Third, whether Mistral's weights and license land by month's end.

That's the briefing. Have a good Sunday.
