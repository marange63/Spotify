Good morning, it's Tuesday, October 27th, and this is your digital money briefing. The Fed decides tomorrow, and the real question is which channel a surprise would hit first.

Start with Bitcoin positioning, because the signals there are pulling in different directions. Last Thursday, US spot Bitcoin ETFs saw a $635.6 million outflow, the largest single-day withdrawal since June. BlackRock's IBIT and Fidelity's FBTC did most of that selling. The tape kept moving after that. Friday brought another $137.94 million out the door. Then Monday reversed. US spot Bitcoin ETFs took in $110.34 million, ending two sessions of outflows, and BlackRock's IBIT was the only fund to record an inflow that day.

So the same fund led the selling on the way down and the buying on the way back up. That's flow leadership concentrated in one issuer in both directions. It is not a broad institutional consensus forming either way. The so-what is simple. Daily ETF flows this week are telling you what one large wrapper's clients are doing, not what institutions as a class have decided.

On price, the line everyone is watching is a daily close below eighty thousand dollars. The answer is genuinely murky. Yahoo Finance's daily record shows Monday's close at $79,722, just under the line. At least one other tracker shows the same day settling slightly above eighty thousand. Those readings straddle the exact level, so treat "did Bitcoin close below eighty thousand" as unresolved rather than settled.

Now set the flow tape against the options market, because it's telling a different story. Over the week that included the biggest ETF outflow since June, Derive's options-implied odds of Bitcoin trading above $100,000 by Christmas rose from 9.7 percent to 12 percent. Over the same week, the implied odds of a drop below $70,000 fell from 15.6 percent to 11 percent. So while ETF owners were pulling money out, options traders were pricing less tail risk, not more.

There are two ways to read that, and you should hold both. One is that conviction is quietly returning even as some holders take profits. The other is that existing hedges simply got monetized and closed. That mechanically cheapens downside protection without anyone's risk appetite actually improving. And these are risk-neutral prices from a single venue, covering the week before Saturday's liquidations. Don't read falling crash odds as falling crash risk.

The plain bullish case still deserves its sentence. A market that just absorbed its largest one-day ETF outflow since June, and still sees options traders shrinking the left tail, is not a market in panic.

That brings in the leverage book. Saturday brought a sharp long-side flush, with about $815 million liquidated over twenty-four hours, overwhelmingly longs. What matters more is what happened to open interest in that same window. Crypto futures open interest fell 2.2 percent, to about $140 billion. After earlier October washouts, leverage had rebuilt within days. This time the book shrank instead. That's an all-crypto reading, and the earlier rebuild came from a different series, so read it as a contrast rather than a trend.

The funding data fills in the other half of the picture. There's a simple two-part test for a forced-selling cascade. The price half is a break below support in the low eighty thousands, and that has fired. The leverage half is perpetual funding back above 8 percent annualized. A macro research note dated Monday puts BTC and ETH perpetual funding at a neutral 0.004 to 0.006 percent per eight-hour window. That annualizes to roughly four and a half to six and a half percent, comfortably under the trigger. It's one snapshot, and venues vary. Still, on the evidence we have, the leverage half of the test has not fired.

Put the pieces together. Tail pricing eased, open interest fell, and funding came in cool. That's a market with less fuel for a forced-selling cascade on a hawkish surprise. Cheap leverage isn't the same as harmless leverage, but the crowded-long setup isn't there. So if a Fed shock hits crypto this week, it's more likely to show up first in redemptions and ETF outflows than in a liquidation spike. If leverage isn't the channel, dollars are. That's where the Fed comes in.

The dollar float is the more interesting channel heading into tomorrow. Total stablecoin supply sat near $300 billion as of Sunday, per Bitcoin.com News. That's down about $12.7 billion since the May 22nd peak. The same source flags USDC as the main driver of the recent losses, down $4.69 billion over the past thirty days.

Why would USDC be the one leaking? A BIS working paper published October 13th offers a mechanism. It found that higher US deposit rates slow stablecoin growth. And USDC's measured sensitivity was roughly two and a half times USDT's. The logic is intuitive. USDC lives mostly inside the regulated US financial system, so it competes directly with bank deposit and money-market yields. USDT's base is more offshore, and stickier.

Two caveats before you lean on it. The first is about the source. The finding reaches us through a secondary summary that doesn't give units or horizon, so take it as a historical sensitivity, not a forecast for tomorrow. The second matters more. There's a cyclical alternative. USDC's float also tends to shrink when trading risk appetite falls, independent of deposit rates. The honest framing is that deposit-rate competition is a candidate cause, not an established one.

Now the second-order implication, which is our inference. If USDC is the more rate-sensitive token, then the balance between USDT and USDC is partly a function of US monetary policy. Tight policy tilts the dollar float offshore, toward USDT. Easier policy should favor the onshore, regulated token. In other words, the Fed is indirectly shaping the composition of the dollar float, not just its size.

Here's the bull case on the same mechanism, stated plainly. If it cuts both ways, then a future move lower in deposit yields should pull USDC's float back up fastest, precisely because it's the most rate-sensitive token. On that view, the current drain is cyclical and reversible, not a verdict on whether stablecoins matter. That's a conditional, not a call on Wednesday.

Either way, the practical takeaway holds. Stablecoins are behaving like a rate-sensitive cash product competing with bank deposits. They are not a one-way adoption curve that mechanically buys more Treasury bills every quarter. For bill demand, that's a small subtraction at the margin, nothing more. So tomorrow's Fed is a direct input into the dollar float, and USDC is the sensitive end.

The trading side of this story gets its public-equity test on Thursday, when Coinbase reports third-quarter results. Coinbase's second-quarter filing showed Monthly Transacting Users falling to 7.6 million, from 8.7 million a year earlier. The company attributed that to fewer trading users amid market conditions. That figure is months old, so treat it as context, not news.

The profit picture is split. That same quarter, Coinbase posted Adjusted EBITDA of $207.8 million, against a GAAP net loss of $359.5 million. The GAAP loss and the operating picture tell different stories, and bulls and bears will each pick their line on Thursday.

Now set Coinbase's falling user count against spot Bitcoin ETFs holding more than $100 billion in combined assets. You get a real question. Is the ETF wrapper substituting for exchange activity rather than adding to it? That's our inference, not a measured relationship, and a simple cyclical retail pullback explains the user decline just as well. One more timing point. The third quarter ended in September, before last week's selloff. So Thursday's guidance and commentary will say more than the reported numbers can.

Put it together. Crypto is de-risking in leverage and repricing in dollars, and the dollar float is where tomorrow's Fed decision lands most directly.

Four things to watch, all still pending. First, the Fed on Wednesday, October 28th. Second, whether USDC's redemptions keep running. Third, whether open interest keeps shrinking or starts rebuilding. And fourth, Coinbase after the close on Thursday, October 29th.

That's the briefing for Tuesday, October 27th. Stay cautiously optimistic.
