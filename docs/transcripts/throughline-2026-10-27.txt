Good morning, this is The Throughline for Tuesday, October 27th, 2026.

Today's headline: Anthropic's IPO filing went public on Monday, and for the first time a frontier lab is asking public equity investors to fund its losses directly. A standing disclosure, since that story runs through several desks today: Anthropic makes the model behind this show.

Eight briefings shipped today. We'll start with the macro backdrop, move through the AI and compute stack, and close on the applied frontier.

Strategic Power opens by correcting its own Monday call. The US-China rare-earth truce is two suspensions lapsing November 10th and November 27th, not one deadline running to January 10th, and the military end-use ban and heavy rare-earth licensing were never suspended at all. On power, South Korea says under 2 percent of the projects that applied for grid connections have secured supply, which echoes last week's PJM markdowns and points to interconnection, not appetite, as the binding constraint on the data-center buildout.

From physical constraints to financial ones. Capital Markets Radar calls the S-1 the top rung of a financing ladder that climbed from CoreWeave's bonds, to default swaps, to private lenders, and now to equity, the layer that absorbs losses first. The filing shows a net loss of 80.7 billion dollars through June, against 42 billion for all of last year, and the company says most of it is accounting charges without saying which kind. The desk's read is that markets are paying up to avoid the first loss, with one-month S&P skew jumping from the 15th to the 82nd percentile ahead of Wednesday's Fed decision.

Carrying that caution into crypto, Digital Money notes spot Bitcoin ETFs swung from Thursday's 635.6 million dollar outflow, the largest since June, to a 110 million dollar inflow on Monday, with BlackRock's IBIT leading both moves. Open interest also shrank after Saturday's liquidations, instead of rebuilding the way it did earlier this month. So if the Fed surprises, the desk expects the hit in redemptions and the stablecoin float rather than a leverage cascade, with USDC, the most rate-sensitive token, as the channel to watch.

That's the macro backdrop. Now the AI and compute stack, starting with the hardware underneath it.

Semiconductors and Compute finds the bottleneck has reached Nvidia's own spec sheet. Vera Rubin's listed memory bandwidth per GPU is about 13 percent lower than on an archived August page, while compute and capacity held, and the desk reads that as worse news for memory-bound inference than the headline number suggests. SK hynix reports Wednesday, and if HBM prices more than double next year, as one Korean report expects, memory takes a bigger bite out of Nvidia's and AMD's margins alike.

Frontier AI Lab Competition takes Anthropic's filing into the compute itself. The S-1 discloses up to 120.5 billion dollars in commitments to SpaceX, the company that owns Anthropic's rival xAI, with full delivery expected in December. The desk's read is that distribution is commoditizing, with Google now reselling OpenAI's models next to its own, while compute is concentrating among the few who own the chips and the power.

AI Products and Applications moves from compute cost to deployment friction. OpenAI's new GPT-6.1 Terra is priced at a tenth of its flagship's per-token rate and landed in GitHub Copilot a day after Google's Gemini 4 Argon took the same shelf, but nobody has published a cost per completed task. Meanwhile Anthropic's unified agent experience reportedly leaves out its most regulated customers, so permission and setup, not price, are now the real adoption bottlenecks.

Enterprise Technology follows the money behind that adoption, starting with Microsoft's buyout offer to roughly 60,000 eligible employees, which excludes data centers, sales, and marketing, the functions that build and sell AI capacity. On the buyer side, a Futurum survey puts 2026 IT budget growth at just 3.84 percent, and over a third of firms covering AI overruns did it by cutting other software licenses. So AI is being paid for by cutting, not adding, on both sides of the vendor-customer table.

Finally, the applied frontier. Space and the Orbital Economy finds that outside SpaceX, Western orbital capacity is still mostly a plan dressed as a deployment: AST SpaceMobile's new Brazilian carrier deal isn't expected to support permanent service until the end of 2027, Rocket Lab's Neutron slipped again to early 2027, and Blue Origin's New Glenn hasn't flown since May. SpaceX's own third-quarter filing, now confirmed, shows revenue up 161 percent, but its launch business grew only 6 percent from a year ago, so the growth is coming from AI and connectivity, not rockets.

One thread worth carrying out. On Sunday, this show said the public S-1 would show whether Anthropic's margins can carry its compute bill. The first answer is split: Frontier Labs reports the operating line swung to a 974 million dollar profit in the second quarter, even as Capital Markets has the net loss at 80.7 billion for the half. One quarter doesn't make a trend, so watch which of those two numbers the roadshow is built around.

That's The Throughline. Have a good Tuesday.
