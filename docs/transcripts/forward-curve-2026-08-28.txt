Today's spine is inertia. In five markets the thing that was supposed to move hasn't — a rate path, a gross margin, an inflow streak, a redemption queue, a launch date. Four of my reads bet that inertia holds. The fifth — the bitcoin inflow streak — bets it finally breaks.

Start with rates, because it prices everything else. Wednesday's personal income and outlays report had headline inflation at 3.7 percent against a FactSet forecast of 3.6, and core at 3.3 against 3.2. The ten-year yield then rose two sessions running, to about 4.65 percent. My call is that the September 16th meeting ends with no cut, and that at least one voter dissents in favor of a hike. I'd put that at roughly three-in-five. Three regional presidents already dissented for a hike on July 29th, the first three-way same-direction dissent since 2016, and nothing in the data since gives them a reason to stand down. What would most make me wrong is Kevin Warsh. His first Jackson Hole keynote as chair had not happened when today's briefings were recorded, and he could frame mid-threes inflation as tolerable in a growth economy. To bet for the call, own the dollar fund, ticker UUP. To fade it, own the long-bond fund, ticker TLT.

Now the cost line that a hot rate path makes worse. Nvidia's chief financial officer named memory prices as the cause of her own company's gross-margin compression, guiding this quarter to 74 percent from 75, and said the increase has already exceeded expectations. TrendForce has HBM3e contract pricing up roughly 20 percent quarter over quarter. My call is that Micron outperforms Nvidia between now and the end of September, at around 60 percent, because the supplier setting the price captures more of the marginal dollar than the designer absorbing it. What would most make me wrong is the reported US decision to let Apple source memory from Chinese suppliers landing as an actual rule. That headline alone took seven percent off Micron in a single session this month. To bet for the call, own Micron, ticker MU, against the semis fund, ticker SMH. To fade it, reverse the pair.

Now the streak I have already been wrong about, restated more honestly. The spot bitcoin funds are nine days into an inflow run, but the daily ticket has fallen from a peak of 606 million dollars on August 20th to 232 million by midweek. The streak is lengthening while the size decays. My call is that the complex records at least one net-outflow day within the next two weeks. I'd put that at about two-in-three and no higher, because the whole of August has failed to produce one, and that base rate has already beaten me once. What would most make me wrong is a dovish Warsh pulling fresh momentum money in behind the move. To bet for the call, buy puts on the BlackRock bitcoin fund, ticker IBIT, which took roughly seventy-two percent of the last eight days of flows. To fade it, own that same fund.

Stay with liquidity, because private credit runs the same play with a gate instead of a price. Fitch found that ten of the sixteen BDCs it tracks could not meet their redemption caps, against an industry backlog Bloomberg puts near fifteen billion dollars. My call is that at least one more non-traded BDC announces a gate, a prorate, or an accelerated return of capital before the end of the third quarter. I'd put that at about seven-in-ten, because the sponsor put is discretionary and the queue is not, and because Cox Capital's repeated tenders give every board a discount it has to answer. What would most make me wrong is the institutional half of the default rate falling toward the 2.3 percent Fitch expects. To bet for the call, buy puts on the listed BDC income fund, ticker BIZD. To fade it, own Apollo, ticker APO, one of the managers monetizing this drought through exit financing rather than being trapped by it.

Last, the schedule everything in orbit is queued behind. SpaceNews, citing an August 20th briefing to airspace managers, lists Starship Flight 14 no earlier than September 15th. My call is that Flight 14 does not fly by that date, at about two-in-three, because no-earlier-than dates are opening bids, and Elon Musk has already walked back the tower catch he promised on the August 4th earnings call. What would most make me wrong is that same descoping. A narrower flight is an easier flight, and NASA's Jared Isaacman said an early-September launch would leave him feeling good about the timing. To bet for the call, buy puts on AST SpaceMobile, ticker ASTS, whose own constellation target already slipped to early 2027 on launch availability alone. To fade it, own SpaceX itself. SpaceX has been a public stock since June, and it closed Thursday at 139.63 dollars, a few dollars above its issue price. It now marks to every Starship milestone in public.

No cut and a hawkish dissent in September, memory beating the chip designer, the bitcoin inflow streak finally breaking, another credit gate closing, and Starship slipping past its own date.
