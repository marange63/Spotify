Good morning, this is The Throughline for Sunday, August 23rd, 2026. Today's headline: Friday's flash PMI put hard evidence behind the growth story this show spent all week dismissing, and gold, the dollar and crypto did not flinch — which leaves Kevin Warsh's first Jackson Hole speech as Fed chair, this Friday, as the event that settles which story is right.

Start with the macro backdrop. Strategic Power stacks two gatekeepers into one briefing. Lloyd's List Intelligence now says U.S.-Iran diplomacy has, in its words, effectively collapsed, yet six months into the Hormuz disruption there is still no oil price spike, because the toll is being collected in tanker charter rates and Gulf port downtime rather than at the pump. And Governor Abbott's own release confirms ERCOT is auditing roughly 474 gigawatts of data-center interconnection requests with untested authority to deny them, even as Eagle Point Credit closes $1.3 billion of mezzanine debt for a Texas campus that skips the grid with its own gas plant. Capital Markets Radar carries the headline itself. The flash Composite PMI jumped to 56.0, the fastest US growth since April 2022, but manufacturing actually missed, so the whole beat rests on one series. And the fastest growth in four years arrived with the slowest input-cost inflation since February, which is the signature of a supply shock rather than an overheating economy, so the growth case is real but not obviously hawkish. Digital Money has the same tension from the crypto side. Friday's run to about $79,500 reversed on Saturday into $529 million of futures liquidations in a single hour, most of it longs, and roughly a fifth the size of Wednesday's short squeeze. Spot ETFs pulled in a genuinely bullish $2.6 billion for the week, still less than what got liquidated in that single Wednesday session. Private Credit and the Corporate Credit Cycle finally has an answer to the mark-staleness question it has run for five straight days. Blue Owl's Loparex position went from 99 and 63 cents on the dollar to 34 and 5 in a single quarter, which says private marks reset in a step tied to the deal calendar, not in a slow drift. Private Equity and the Deal Economy closes the macro leg with an industry that has decided to buy rather than sell. KKR has made an unsolicited approach for the utility UGI at about $9 billion, framed in the press as an AI-power play the disclosed numbers do not yet support. Thoma Bravo agreed to take Accelerant private below its own IPO price from a year earlier, while roughly $1.2 trillion of zombie-fund assets keeps aging rather than clearing.

Now the AI and compute stack, where the build keeps being asked whether it can pay for itself. Semiconductors and Compute Infrastructure has a Bank of America estimate, unconfirmed by TSMC, putting the Arizona fab's net margin at 38 percent. That is a real inflection off a deeply negative base, and still short of home-market economics, while Applied Materials shows AI demand growing faster than its China business is shrinking. Frontier AI Lab Competition has the first outside number in the Anthropic-OpenAI argument. Ramp's spend data has Anthropic's share of business AI spending rising to nearly 44 percent in July, even as OpenAI reportedly gains faster inside the current quarter. The reason given is Fable 5's price and data-retention terms, a safety posture showing up as a cost line, just as Anthropic reportedly seeks a listing at $2 trillion or more. AI Products and Applications has the connective tissue for the whole stack. The Model Context Protocol's new roadmap admits agents still have no standardized way to prove their own identity, and proposes routing it through identity infrastructure enterprises already run rather than any one vendor's system. That gap is live across most enterprise AI volume, because OpenAI's own June data, published this month, puts agentic Codex use at 64 percent of enterprise output tokens. Enterprise Technology closes that leg with Atlassian quietly converting governance into a paid upgrade. Metadata contribution cannot be switched off below its Enterprise tier, a default that sticks precisely because most buyers cannot see what data they are sitting on.

Turning to the applied frontier, where that same permission question shows up as a literal regulatory gate. Robotics and Physical AI sorts the week by who had to verify the number. Zoox charges driverless fares in Las Vegas on a federal NHTSA exemption, and Symbotic's backlog sits in a filed GAAP statement, while Tesla's zero-notable-incidents claim rests on its own telling, beside the crash reports it filed with NHTSA. Space and the Orbital Economy names launch capacity, not capital, as the binding constraint. Starcloud raised $250 million at a $2.3 billion valuation on the strength of one Nvidia chip in orbit, and its chief executive says the biggest near-term cost is not compute but securing a ride. Washington's new space transportation policy targets more than 1,000 launches and reentries a year by 2030, with no appropriation attached to the ground and airspace limits it just conceded. AI in Biopharma R&D closes the tour on the same disclosure gap. Chai Discovery signed Bristol Myers Squibb as its second big-pharma partner in eleven weeks, and neither that deal nor the Pfizer one carries a dollar figure. Roche's chief executive could quantify AI adoption and filing speed, and still could not put a number on whether AI is producing better drugs.

One thread is worth naming, because it surfaced in several briefings today. The binding constraint has quietly stopped being capability or capital, and become the right to authorize. Iran is holding a strait half-closed rather than shutting it. ERCOT is auditing a grid connection it may not have clear power to deny. Atlassian is deciding what a customer is allowed to withhold. And the Model Context Protocol's steering group is still trying to standardize how an agent proves who it is. Different gatekeepers, same question: not what can be built, but who gets to say yes.
