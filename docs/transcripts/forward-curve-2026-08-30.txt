Today's spine is a single question. Which prices are actually being set by the people who claim to set them. Start at the long end, because everything else discounts off it.

The strategic-power read is blunt on this. Treasury doubled its longer-dated buybacks, to four billion dollars an operation, and the thirty-year fell nine basis points on the announcement, to about 5.20 percent. Within hours it was back above where it started. Then Kevin Warsh turned hawkish at Jackson Hole on Friday, and the thirty-year closed the week right back above 5.2 percent. My call is that the thirty-year yield does not close below five percent at any point before the September 16th Fed meeting, at roughly three-in-four. Two interventions this month, one fiscal and one rhetorical, both failed to hold the long end down, and an operation that size is small against the debt it is meant to support. What would most make me wrong is a soft payrolls print on September 4th killing the hike story, which hands duration back its bid in a single session. To bet for the call, buy puts on the long-bond fund, ticker TLT. To fade it, own that same fund.

Now to what a five-percent long end actually finances, which is the AI buildout. The same strategic-power read gives the number that matters: roughly sixteen gigawatts of data-center capacity scheduled for 2026 operations, against about five gigawatts actually under construction. The binding constraint is transformers, switchgear and interconnection queues, not chips and not money. My call is that at least one more named, large US data-center project publicly slips its energization date on power, permitting or grid-equipment grounds before the end of September, at about two-in-three. The base rate is this show's own tape, where the STACK Infrastructure and Oracle site already went to 2029 on gas-pipeline permitting alone. What would most make me wrong is hyperscalers absorbing the delay quietly inside unchanged capex guidance, so nothing gets announced even though everything slips. To bet for the call, own grid and turbine hardware, GE Vernova, ticker GEV, where the scarcity value now sits. To fade it, own a data-center landlord like Digital Realty, ticker DLR, which needs those schedules to hold.

Stay with the buildout, because the demand side has the same credibility problem. Both the semis read and the frontier-labs read took apart the same deal today. Marvell's headline with Google is up to a hundred and twenty billion dollars, but the mechanism is a warrant vesting in five-hundred-million-dollar slices through fiscal 2033. That is a ceiling, not an order book. My call is that no hyperscaler custom-silicon announcement between now and the end of September comes with a firm, disclosed dollar purchase commitment rather than a ceiling, warrant or capacity-option structure. I put that high, around four-in-five, because this show has taken apart three headline numbers in three straight days and every one shrank on inspection. What would most make me wrong is Broadcom disclosing a booked backlog with contracted delivery dates on its next report. To bet for the call, own Nvidia, ticker NVDA, since optioned competition is a slower threat than announced competition. To fade it, own the custom-silicon pair, Broadcom and Marvell, tickers AVGO and MRVL.
